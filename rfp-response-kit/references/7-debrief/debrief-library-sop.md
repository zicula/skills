# Step 8 — Debrief & Library Update SOP (1 hour, within 5 days of the decision)

The decision letter arrives — win or loss — and the informal version of you closes the
folder and moves on. The systematic version spends one hour extracting the tuition you
already paid. This SOP is that hour. It runs within five days, while memories and
emails are fresh, whether the news was good or bad.

## 1. Request the debrief (day 0 — ten minutes)

Most buyers offer a debrief if asked, and most vendors forget to ask. Send this the day
the decision lands:

> Subject: [RFP reference] — debrief request
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Thank you for the decision on [RFP reference]. Regardless of the outcome, we invest in
> improving every response we submit. Would you offer a short debrief — 20–30 minutes by
> call, at your convenience — on how our submission scored against the evaluation
> criteria? We are asking to learn, not to contest anything.
>
> Regards, [Name, role, phone]

Log the request and the reply date in the deal folder. If the buyer declines, note it —
a pattern of declined debriefs in one buyer segment is itself market intelligence.

## 2. Run the debrief (20–30 minutes)

Ask exactly five questions, and write down the answers verbatim where possible:

1. Where did our submission score strongest against the criteria?
2. Where did it score weakest — and what would a stronger answer have contained?
3. Was anything unclear, missing, or hard to find in our documents?
4. How did the pricing read against the field? (ask carefully; buyers vary in what
   they can share)
5. What would make us a stronger bidder for your next opportunity?

Rules: listen, thank, take notes — no defending, no relitigating. The debrief is a
source, not a courtroom.

## 3. The one-hour extraction (do it the same week)

**a) Update the library (20 min).** Open `4-library/answer-library.csv`:
- Answers the evaluator praised → mark improved wording back into the row, set
  `last_reviewed` to today
- Answers that drew doubt → rewrite the template with what a better answer needed
- Questions the RFP asked that the library lacked → new rows, next `L-1xx` numbers

**b) Update the bid log (10 min).** Scorecard `Log` sheet, one row: date, opportunity,
final score from the go/no-go run, hard gates at decision time, outcome, and the
one-sentence reason from the debrief.

**c) Re-weight the scorecard (10 min, quarterly rhythm).** Look at the last ~5 logged
bids: did the scores predict the outcomes? If lost bids scored 70+, your Reachability
criteria are flattered — tighten the weights (keep the total at 100) and date the change
in the Log. If won bids nearly failed the gate, loosen it — you may be leaving good
work on the table.

**d) Archive the bid room (15 min).** One folder per opportunity (`OPP-YYMM-XX`):
intake form, scorecard snapshot, matrix, submitted files, debrief notes. Wins become
case-study seeds (ask the client what may be published); losses become the cheapest
consulting you ever bought.

**e) Decide the follow-up (5 min).** Winners: kickoff logistics. Losers: the decline-
grace note in reverse — a short thank-you that asks to stay on the vendor list, plus a
calendar reminder for the contract's likely re-tender date. Bids lost today are
relationships won two years out; re-tenders reward the vendor that stayed professional
and stayed visible.

## 4. The metric that matters

After six logged bids, compute two numbers: your win rate, and your average go/no-go
score on won vs lost bids. If the gap is growing, the system is learning. If it is not,
the problem is usually upstream — the scorecard passing deals the market has already
priced you out of. That is a weights conversation, and now you have the log to have it
with evidence.
