# Step 2 — The Bid / No-Bid Decision (30 minutes)

**The most profitable bid decision is often "no."** Every bid you write costs real hours
from people who also have billable work. A small team that chases every RFP writes worse
responses and wins less than a team that answers fewer bids, better. This file explains
the scorecard and the discipline around it.

## The spreadsheet

`bid-no-bid-scorecard.xlsx` has three sheets:

- **Scorecard** — 12 criteria in 3 groups (Fit 30, Reachability 40, Capacity 30 — total
  weight 100). You score each criterion 0–4. Weighted points compute themselves; the
  verdict cell reads the total and the hard gates. The sheet ships with a worked example
  filled in (italic) — overwrite it with your opportunity.
- **HardGates** — 6 stay-out conditions (a mandatory qualification you lack, a deadline
  you cannot staff, terms you cannot accept, and so on). If any gate reads "Yes", the
  verdict says STAY OUT no matter how high the total scores. That is the point: enthusiasm
  does not override a missing certification.
- **Log** — one row per decision: date, opportunity, score, gates, decision, one-line
  reason. After a few months this log is the most valuable spreadsheet you own — it is
  how you learn which bids you should actually chase.

## The scoring rule

Score what is true today, not what could be true with effort:

- **4** — true and evidenced (we have done this, the reference exists, the person is named)
- **3** — true with a small gap we can close in days
- **2** — partly true; closing it costs weeks or real money
- **1** — mostly false; a stretch
- **0** — false today

Read the criterion, then find the evidence before choosing a number. If you cannot name
the evidence, the score is at most 2.

## The verdict bands

| Total | Verdict | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Any hard gate = Yes | **STAY OUT** | Send the decline. Do not argue with the gate. |
| 65–100 | **Bid** | Commit the team, book the calendar from the intake form |
| 45–64 | **Conditional** | Write the 2–3 conditions in the Log row ("bid only if partner X confirms by Friday"). Re-score when resolved |
| 0–44 | **No-bid** | Send the decline, keep the relationship, log it |

Bands are starting points. A 68 built on two 1-scores in Reachability ("we do not know
the buyer") is weaker than the number says — read the spread, not just the sum.

## The worked example (shipped in the sheet)

A managed-IT team scores a municipal IT-support RFP: core scope matches (Fit 22.5/30),
the buyer is familiar, the price-to-win works — but the RFP requires a security
certification they do not hold and cannot obtain before questions-due. HardGate 1 = Yes.
Total weighted score 71 — strong enough for a "bid" on the bands alone — and the verdict
still reads **STAY OUT**, correctly. That one saved verdict pays for the whole kit in
saved hours. The same numbers ship in the spreadsheet as the italic worked example.

## How to decline without burning the relationship

Buyers keep lists of credible bidders. A clean, early decline keeps you on them; a
late, sloppy withdrawal does not. Send it before the Q&A window closes, keep it short,
and ask to stay informed:

> Subject: RFP [reference] — [Company] response
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Thank you for the invitation to respond to [RFP reference]. After reviewing the
> requirements against our current commitments, we will sit this one out — [one honest,
> non-blaming sentence, e.g. "the timeline overlaps with an existing implementation"].
> We would value being considered for future opportunities: if you run a vendor list,
> please keep us on it, and feel free to invite us to your next pre-bid briefing.
>
> Regards, [Name, role, phone]

Two rules: decline **early** (buyers plan evaluation capacity around expected bids), and
decline **once** (do not resurface with a half-bid at the deadline).

## Conditional bids — the honest middle

"Conditional" is a real verdict, not procrastination. Write the conditions in the Log
row with dates. If a condition has not resolved 48 hours after the go/no-go meeting,
treat the bid as a no-bid: late uncertainty produces the worst responses.

## Re-weighting the scorecard

Your first weights (30/40/30) are the kit's defaults. After every debrief (Step 8), ask:
did the criteria predict the result? If bids you lost all scored 70+, your Reachability
weights are too generous. Adjust weights in the `Log` notes and date the change — the
scorecard is a system you tune, not a constant. Keep total weight at 100 so bands keep
their meaning.
