# Response Outline — IT / Managed Services Bids

Use for: managed IT, MSP, software implementation, infrastructure, support services.
Skeleton with word budgets for a 20-page technical volume (scale proportionally; obey
the RFP's own structure first — if the RFP numbers its sections, mirror those numbers
exactly; evaluators score faster when the response maps 1:1 to their checklist).

## Structure

**§1 Executive summary** (1–1.5 pages)
One page: their problem in their words, our three win themes with evidence, the
outcome we commit to, commercial shape (term, model), and why us in one sentence.
Write it last, place it first. Use `executive-summary-skeleton.md`.

**§2 Understanding of requirements** (1–2 pages)
Restate the buyer's objectives and constraints (budget cycle, existing systems, service
history). Name the hardest requirement from your matrix and own it explicitly —
evaluators trust vendors who name the hard part.

**§3 Proposed solution / service model** (4–6 pages)
- Service catalog mapped to the requirement rows (reference the RFP's § numbers inline)
- Operating model: who does what, where, hours, coverage
- Tools and platforms (named, licensed, who owns what)
- For each service area: the commitment, not the capability — "response within X",
  "monthly review on Y"

**§4 Transition / implementation plan** (2–3 pages)
Phased plan with dates offset from award, named milestones with acceptance criteria,
risk register for the transition itself, and the rollback story. If there is an
incumbent: the handover mechanics, stated respectfully and specifically.

**§5 Team and governance** (1–2 pages)
Named engagement lead with CV extract, deputy, escalation matrix, org chart for this
contract, and the governance calendar (weekly status, monthly review, QBR).

**§6 Service levels and reporting** (1–2 pages)
The SLA table with definitions and measurement method, the reporting pack with samples,
what happens on a miss (credit schedule). Put the table early; prose is secondary here.

**§7 Security and compliance** (1–2 pages)
Controls mapped to the buyer's stated requirements, certifications with certificate
numbers and scope, data handling, subprocessors. Answer the security questionnaire
format if the RFP provides one — do not substitute your own document.

**§8 Relevant experience** (1–2 pages)
Two or three case studies in the standard format (context → scope → measurable result →
relevance to this requirement), references listed with consent noted.

**§9 Assumptions and dependencies** (0.5 page)
The honest list (from the intake form), each with its fallback. Buyers read this as
maturity, not weakness.

**Pricing volume** — separate file unless the RFP says otherwise; price only what §3
committed to; basis of estimate auditable; escalation clause stated.

## Word-budget discipline

Numbers above assume ~400 words/page. If the RFP caps pages, cut §3 and §5 prose first;
cut §6's table content last. Every section opens with its answer in the first sentence.

## The three mistakes that sink IT bids

1. **Feature catalog instead of service commitment** — the buyer is buying outcomes
   with numbers attached; "we support Windows and Linux" is not a service level.
2. **Transition hand-waving** — the riskiest phase gets the vagarest section; give it
   dates, names and a rollback.
3. **Security by certificate** — a cert logo with no scope statement answers nothing;
   map controls to their stated requirements.
