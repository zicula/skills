# Step 7 — Red-Team Review Checklist (three rounds + submission day)

A red-team review means someone who did not write the draft attacks it the way an
evaluator will. Run the three rounds in order on different passes — compliance flaws
hide under craft polish and vice versa. Book the reviewers when the writing calendar is
booked; a red team scheduled after the deadline is a ceremony, not a review.

**Who reviews:** Round 1 needs a detail person who did not draft. Round 2 needs your
best writer. Round 3 needs someone who can play the buyer (ideally someone who has
scored bids, or at least bought services). The bid owner attends, listens, takes notes,
and does not defend the draft.

---

## Round 1 — Compliance (pass/fail, ~1 hour)

Open `shredding-matrix.xlsx` and the draft side by side. For every row:

- [ ] The requirement is answered — every `Mandatory` row maps to a passage by section
- [ ] Every part of compound requirements is addressed ("describe A, B and C" — all three)
- [ ] The answer responds to the verbatim ask, not to a neighboring question
- [ ] Every required form is completed and signed (compare against the RFP's form list)
- [ ] Format rules hold: page limits, fonts, margins, file naming, section numbering
      that mirrors the RFP
- [ ] Required attachments exist, are current (dates, certificates), and are referenced
- [ ] Pricing appears only where the RFP says it may appear (often a separate volume)
- [ ] Numbers cross-check: the same figure in exec summary, body and annex matches
- [ ] Matrix status column is updated — the reviewer signs the matrix when this round clears

**Exit rule:** zero open Mandatory rows. If any remain, stop and fix before Round 2 —
there is no persuasion worth doing on a non-compliant document.

---

## Round 2 — Craft (clarity and specificity, ~1 hour)

Read the draft as a tired evaluator at 16:00 on a Friday, with nine other bids pending:

- [ ] Each section opens with the answer, not with background
- [ ] Sentences carry one idea; paragraphs carry one point
- [ ] Specifics outnumber adjectives — run the wallpaper check (`5-win-themes`
      playbook §8) and fix every hit
- [ ] Every claim that can carry evidence does (numbers, names, dates — or is cut)
- [ ] Acronyms are expanded on first use; internal jargon is gone
- [ ] The document reads consistently — one voice, one tense, terminology from the
      RFP (their nouns, not your product names)
- [ ] Length obeys the limit: cut to the page budget, then cut 5% more
- [ ] Tables and lists carry the scannable content evaluators actually quote

---

## Round 3 — Persuasion (play the evaluator, ~45 minutes)

The reviewer simulates scoring. Give them the RFP's evaluation criteria and weights,
the executive summary, and silence:

- [ ] From the executive summary alone, they can state our three win themes with their proof
- [ ] Against each weighted criterion, they can find our best evidence in under a minute
- [ ] They can name one thing per section that beats a generic competitor's answer
- [ ] Risks are addressed before the buyer would have to raise them
- [ ] The "so what" test: for each major section they can say what we get that matters
- [ ] The price (where visible) reads justified by the value story, not bolted on
- [ ] Verdict simulation: they write the 3–5 bullet scoring notes they would submit.
      If the notes are thin, the themes are thin — back to the playbook, not more adjectives

---

## Submission day — the logistics gate

Most submission failures are logistics. Run this the day **before** deadline, then again
two hours before:

- [ ] Portal access works — credentials tested this week, from this machine
- [ ] Final files exported in the required format, named per the RFP's convention
- [ ] File sizes within portal limits; uploads tested end-to-end (a dry run beats a
      23:50 surprise)
- [ ] Signatures present where required (the authorized signer reachable all day)
- [ ] Forms cross-checked against the RFP's submission checklist, item by item
- [ ] One person owns the submit; a second person confirms receipt
- [ ] Target: submit a day early. The clock you are racing includes the portal's clock
- [ ] Screenshot/record the confirmation page and any submission ID; save it in the
      deal folder

After submission: archive the full bid room (intake, matrix, draft, reviews, submitted
files) — the debrief in Step 7's sibling (`7-debrief/debrief-library-sop.md`) needs it
within five days of the decision.
