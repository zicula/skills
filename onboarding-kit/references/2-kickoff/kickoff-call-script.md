# Kickoff Call Script

Word-for-word lines for the moments that decide how the whole project feels.
Read them as written the first few times; adapt once they feel like yours.
Worked example uses Fieldnote Studio (you) and Copperline Coffee Roasters
(client: Dana).

---

## 1. The open (first 60 seconds)

> "Thanks for making time, Dana. Quick plan for our hour: I'll restate what
> we're building so we're sure I heard it right, we'll clear the three open
> questions from your form, agree exactly what's in and out of scope, set how
> we'll communicate, and finish with next steps and owners. You'll get a
> written recap tomorrow. Sound good?"

Why it works: it promises the recap, names the scope conversation before it
happens, and ends with a question they will say yes to.

## 2. Restating the goal (segment 0:05)

> "In your form you wrote: 'more online subscription sales.' I want to push
> on that for a minute — if we only achieve one thing in this project, is it
> 'subscription signups go up,' or is it 'the brand finally looks like the
> quality of the coffee,' or something else?"

Wait. Let them finish. Write their exact words. If they say "both," say:

> "Both it is. For the plan I need them ranked — if time forces a choice in
> week five, which one wins?"

## 3. The red-list opener (segment 0:15)

> "Your form flagged three things I want to clear today so they don't bite us
> in week three. First one: Shopify admin access is still with your previous
> developer. Proposals: you send him the admin invite this week, so access
> moves through Shopify itself and I hold no passwords. Can that happen by
> Friday?"

Pattern: what we saw → what it affects → one concrete proposal → one date.

## 4. Reading the out-of-scope list (segment 0:30)

> "Now the list that saves us arguments later — what's out of scope. For this
> project: email templates, packaging design, the wholesale price-list
> system, and photography. Everything new goes through a one-page change
> request, which is a formality, not a fight — it just keeps the timeline
> honest. Anything on that list you assumed was in?"

The pause after that question is the whole point of this document. Do not
fill it.

## 5. If they say "can't that small thing just be included?"

> "It's small, and that's exactly why I want it written down instead of
> remembered. Two options: I swap it for something of similar size, or we add
> it as a one-line change request with a fixed cost. Which do you prefer?"

Both options are yes-shaped. The client keeps their dignity; the scope keeps
its edges.

## 6. Setting the rhythm (segment 0:45)

> "Here's how we stay in sync. Every Friday you'll get a five-line update:
> done, next, blocked, decisions I need, dates that matter. It takes me ten
> minutes and it means you're hearing from me without chasing. If anything is
> urgent mid-week, email me directly and mark it URGENT — I answer urgent
> same business day, everything else within one business day. Same deal in
> reverse: if I need an approval, I'll send it with a deadline, and a week of
> silence moves the milestone dates. Fair?"

> "A week of silence moves the milestone dates" is the sentence to keep. Say
> it kindly, exactly once, in week one — it does the work for the rest of the
> project.

## 7. The close (segment 0:55)

> "Let me read back the three things we agreed. One: Dana sends the Shopify
> admin invite by Friday the 9th. Two: I send the recap email today and the
> scope summary by Thursday. Three: we confirm the milestone plan Friday.
> Did I miss anything?"

Then the last question:

> "Last question, and then I'll let you go: what would have to be true for
> you to call this project a win?"

## 8. If the call goes sideways

| Moment | Line |
|---|---|
| Client brings a stakeholder you did not expect | "Great to have you — I'll send the recap to everyone, and I'd love five minutes with you after, since approvals touch your side." |
| Client re-litigates the price | "That was set in the proposal, and I'd rather keep this hour for scope and dates. Let's book 20 minutes this week if anything about the number feels off." |
| Client answers every question with a story | "That's useful context — I'm noting it for the recap. To keep our hour useful: [repeat the question]." |
| The hour ends with open reds | "We're at time with two things open. I'd rather book 15 minutes Thursday and close them properly than guess." |

## What success looks like

The client can repeat, without notes: what we're building, what's out of
scope, when they'll hear from you, and what they owe by Friday. If they can,
the project is on rails.
