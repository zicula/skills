# Communication Cadence Playbook

The playbook is your half of the rhythm: how often you write, how fast you
answer, and what to do when the client's side of the rhythm breaks. The
welcome packet told the client the rules; this file keeps you honest to them
and gives you the repair moves when it slips.

---

## A. The default cadence (adjust per client, then keep it)

| Stream | Rhythm | Owner | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly update | Every [Friday, by 16:00] | You | [email thread "Copperline — weekly"] |
| Approvals/decisions needed | Flagged inside the weekly update + a direct line when <5 days out | You | email |
| Client questions day-to-day | Reply within [1 business day] | You | email |
| Urgent (broken/down/live-damaging) | Same business day | Both | email, subject starts URGENT |
| Calls | [Kickoff + milestone reviews; ad hoc by booking link] | — | video |

Why email as the single channel: everything searchable, everything linkable,
the thread is the truth. Calls happen — and anything decided on a call gets
three lines in the same day's email: decision, owner, date.

## B. Response-time commitments (both directions)

- Yours: [1 business day] for normal mail, same business day for URGENT,
  during [Mon–Fri 9–18]. Outside hours is outside hours — the welcome packet
  already said so, kindly.
- Theirs: approvals and inputs get deadlines in writing (milestone plan,
  access checklist). The polite enforcement line, written once in the kickoff
  and repeated in updates: **"if I need an answer by a date and it doesn't
  come, milestone dates shift by the same amount."**
- Silence is an answer to schedule, but to content: ask again. A question
  that matters goes out again after [2 business days] with one added line:
  "circling this to the top."

## C. The escalation ladder (when the client goes quiet)

| Stage | When | Move |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Nudge | 2 business days after the question | One line: "Circling this to the top — need it by [date] to hold [milestone]." |
| 2. Name the cost | 4 business days | "Without [input] by [date], M3 moves to [new date]. Happy either way — just tell me which." Give the choice, not a threat. |
| 3. Offer the small version | 6 business days | "If [full input] is stuck, I can proceed with [partial/assumption] and we correct later — reply 'go ahead' and I will." |
| 4. Formal pause | 8+ business days | Written note: project pauses [date], restart needs [input]; the pause is visible in the Friday update and milestone CSV. You are protecting the schedule record, not punishing anyone. |

Worked example (Fieldnote): Copperline's wholesale copy was due Oct 14,
silent by Oct 16 → nudge Oct 16 ("need it by the 20th to hold M3") → reply
Oct 17, copy arrived Oct 13 actually logged after merge. Ladder stopped at
stage 1; two lines of email, zero drama.

## D. Over-contact clients (the opposite problem)

Signals: daily pings, multiple channels for one topic, replies within minutes
being treated as the norm.

- Do **not** train yourself to answer instantly; you train the expectation.
- Fold it into the rhythm: "Great question — it's in Friday's update, and
  I'll answer fully there." Then actually answer fully there.
- Give the legitimate urgency a home: "If it's genuinely URGENT, put URGENT
  in the subject — same-day, promise. Everything else, Friday."
- If it persists, one kind sentence on a call: "I work better in batches, and
  you get better work batched. Fridays plus URGENT — deal?"

## E. The Friday update discipline

- It goes out even in weeks where "the update is: nothing moved." Especially
  then. Reliability is the product of this email.
- It is five lines — see status-update-template.md. If it needs six
  paragraphs, that is a call, not an update.
- Log each sent update in the project thread with a consistent subject line
  so the whole project reads like a journal.

## What success looks like

- You can show a stranger the project thread and they can reconstruct the
  project from the Friday updates alone.
- The client's expected reply times are written down and honored — and when
  their silence costs dates, the cost appears in writing before it appears
  in reality.
- Mid-project, the only channel chaos is which of you gets to send the URGENT
  first.
