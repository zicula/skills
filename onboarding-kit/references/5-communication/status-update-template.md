# Status Update Template (the 5-line Friday update)

One update, five lines, every Friday. This is the smallest document in the
kit and the one that does the most work: it is how the client stops chasing
and starts trusting the process.

Rules:
- Five lines. If the truth needs more, the sixth paragraph becomes a phone
  call offer — not a longer email.
- Every line is concrete: names, numbers, dates. "Progressing well" is not a
  line.
- Send it even in empty weeks. Especially then.

---

## The template

```
Subject: [Project] — weekly update [date]

DONE:      [what moved this week — 1-2 concrete items]
NEXT:      [what moves next week — 1-2 items]
BLOCKED:   [what is waiting on whom — or "nothing"]
DECISIONS NEEDED: [what you must answer, by when — or "none"]
KEY DATES: [the next 1-2 dates that matter]
```

---

## Worked example (Fieldnote → Copperline, Fri 2026-10-24)

```
Subject: Copperline — weekly update Oct 24

DONE:      Brand kit approved Oct 27 (v1.1); events page (CR-001) built
           into staging
NEXT:      Wholesale + subscriptions pages get real copy; M3 review
           prep Mon
BLOCKED:   Nothing — photos arrived Oct 14, wholesale copy Oct 13,
           thank you both
DECISIONS NEEDED: none — next approval window is M3 review (Nov 3)
KEY DATES: M3 review Mon Nov 3 · soft launch Fri Nov 1 is the hard
           date, buffer intact
```

Ten lines with wrapping, ninety seconds to write, and it did four jobs at
once: progress is visible, the input gratitude is on record, the next
approval window is predictable, and the buffer status is public.

## The three variants you will actually send

**The empty-ish week (send it anyway):**
```
DONE:      Internal: scope summary v1.2 corrections merged; M2 assets
           prepped
NEXT:      Brand directions ready Mon — you'll get exactly 2 options
BLOCKED:   None
DECISIONS NEEDED: Brand direction pick — Fri Oct 16 (M1 gate)
KEY DATES: M1 decision Fri Oct 16
```

**The week something slipped:**
```
DONE:      5 of 6 pages in staging
NEXT:      Contact page + M3 prep
BLOCKED:   Wholesale copy — needed by Oct 20 for M3 to hold its
           Nov 3 review date; if it comes Oct 22, M3 moves to Nov 5
DECISIONS NEEDED: Confirm copy timing, Riley — by Mon Oct 20
KEY DATES: M3 review Nov 3 (holds if copy lands by Oct 20)
```
Note the shape: the slip is stated with its cost, the choice is offered, and
the client is respected enough to decide. That is the whole skill.

**The week a change request landed:**
```
DONE:      CR-001 events page approved and built (+$600, launch date
           holds — buffer absorbed it, per plan v1.1)
NEXT:      M3 review prep
BLOCKED:   None
DECISIONS NEEDED: none
KEY DATES: M3 review Mon Nov 3 · launch Nov 1 hard date
```

## Why five lines beats a long email

| Line | The question it kills |
|---|---|
| DONE | "What have you actually been doing?" |
| NEXT | "When will I see something?" |
| BLOCKED | "Why did this take longer than you said?" |
| DECISIONS NEEDED | "Nobody told me I had to decide anything" |
| KEY DATES | "I lost track of when things happen" |

## What success looks like

- Week four: the client forwards your update to their partner instead of
  asking you for status.
- Your BLOCKED lines get answered in hours, because they always carry a date
  and a cost, stated calmly.
- The updates thread alone can reconstruct the project for anyone who joins
  late — including you, in month three.
