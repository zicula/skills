# Welcome Packet Template

**Step 7 of the loop — SET RHYTHM.** The welcome packet goes out in week one,
after the recap and scope summary. It is the client-facing companion to the
cadence playbook: in one page the client learns how working with you feels —
what happens when, where, and how fast.

Rules:
- One page. This is a welcome, not a manual.
- Fill every [bracket] before sending; a packet with blanks reads as a form
  letter.
- Numbers here (response times, update day) must match the kickoff recap
  exactly. One contradiction and both documents lose their force.

---

## The template

```
Subject: Welcome to [studio] — how we'll work together

Hi [first name],

We're glad to be doing this with you. Here's the short version of how the
next [N] weeks run — everything here was also agreed on the kickoff call,
this page just keeps it in one place.

WHAT HAPPENS NOW
- This week: [scope summary + milestone plan — dates]
- Every [Friday]: a five-line update from me (done / next / blocked /
  decisions needed / key dates)
- [Next milestone]: [what it is, when, what we need from you before it]

HOW TO REACH US
- Day to day: [email] — we reply within [1 business day]
- Urgent (something is broken/down/live-damaging): mark URGENT in the subject
  — same business day
- Scheduled calls: [booking link] — [weekly slot]

WHAT WE NEED FROM YOU
- Approvals by the dates in the milestone plan (they move both ways)
- [Client input items — from access checklist, one line each]
- One feedback voice per round: single document, single round — this keeps
  your dates real

HOW DECISIONS GET MADE
- New ideas mid-project are welcome — they go through a one-page change
  request so the timeline stays honest. Approve, swap, or park: your call,
  in writing.
- Everything agreed lives in writing: recap email, scope summary, Friday
  updates. When in doubt, the thread is the truth.

WHAT YOU CAN EXPECT FROM US
- [Studio's working hours: e.g. Mon–Fri 9–18 ICT]
- [e.g. "One point of contact — me — even when Marco does the work"]
- [e.g. "Files you can open without us: milestones, updates, and the scope
  doc are yours to read any time"]

Priya
Fieldnote Studio — [site] — [phone]
```

---

## The worked example, filled

For Copperline, Priya's packet keeps every promise the recap made: updates
Friday by 16:00 ICT, replies within one business day, URGENT same business
day, one feedback voice (single document, single round — Riley gathers
wholesale notes, Dana sends the merged document), and "one point of contact —
me." The input items repeat the access checklist dates: photos and wholesale
copy by Oct 14.

## Why the odd-sounding lines are there

| Line | What it prevents |
|---|---|
| "numbers match the recap" | two documents disagreeing — the classic trust killer |
| "one feedback voice per round" | three overlapping feedback waves stretching week five |
| "the thread is the truth" | decisions made in a phone call nobody can find later |
| "your call, in writing" (change requests) | the process feeling like something done *to* the client |
| working hours + point of contact | 22:47 messages being treated as slow replies |

## What success looks like

- The client replies to the packet with something human ("this is more
  organized than my last three agencies") or with a question — both are wins.
- Response-time promises in the packet are kept so visibly in weeks one and
  two that the client stops testing them.
- When a mid-project idea appears, the client asks "change request?" before
  you do.
