# Scope & Disclaimers

Read this once. It defines what this kit is, what it deliberately is not,
and how it is allowed to grow.

## What this kit is

A set of business-process documents — questionnaires, agendas, scripts,
worksheets, checklists, templates, and CSV logs — for the operational work of
onboarding a new client as a freelancer, agency, or studio. Plain files you
copy, fill in, and adapt.

## The "3 nots" (deliberate design decisions)

1. **No contract templates.** This kit contains agreements-free process
   documents. The scope worksheet is an internal alignment record and the
   kickoff recap is a summary of a conversation — neither is a contract, and
   neither should be presented to a client as one. Real contracts, terms,
   and anything with legal force belong to your own lawyer, in your
   jurisdiction. "Process templates — not legal advice" is the rule this kit
   runs on.
2. **Nothing about employment law, taxes, or regulated advice.** Hiring,
   payroll, tax positions, insurance, and industry-specific compliance are
   outside this kit. Where a template touches a regulated topic (intake
   question 26), it routes the decision to the appropriate professional
   advisor on the client's side instead of answering it.
3. **No sensitive-data storage patterns.** The templates deliberately hold
   the smallest amount of client information needed to run a project, ask
   for platform-based access instead of raw credentials, and instruct you to
   rotate and purge at handoff. What you keep beyond that is your policy's
   business — the archive checklist tells you to have one, not what yours
   should say.

## How to adapt the kit (and what to keep)

- Fill every [bracket], delete rows and questions that do not fit your
  business, and add your own house style.
- Keep three things even after heavy editing: the traffic-light/red-flag rule
  in the questionnaire, the "not written = not included" grammar in the scope
  worksheet, and the writing-down habit everywhere else. Those three are the
  operating system; the rest is furniture.

## Worked example disclaimer

Fieldnote Studio, Copperline Coffee Roasters, Dana Whitfield, Riley, every
dollar figure, and every date in this kit are fictional, created to show
filled-in versions of the templates. Any resemblance to real businesses is
coincidental.

## Version & update policy

- v1.0 — initial release.
- Updates ship as v1.x (same purchase, re-download). New files may appear;
  existing files change only to fix errors or clarify — the structure stays
  stable so your edits do not get orphaned.
- If a platform changes its UI (and platforms do), these files stay usable:
  the kit teaches platform-level concepts (invites, shares, threads) rather
  than button locations.

## The honesty clause (for your own marketing use of this kit)

When you present your onboarding process to clients, describe what the
documents actually do. The kit makes projects organized and explicit; it
does not control outcomes, clients, or markets, and no document in it claims
to.
