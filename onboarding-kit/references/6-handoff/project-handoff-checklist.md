# Project Handoff Checklist

**Step 9 of the loop — CLOSE.** The handoff is the last impression and the
first line of your testimonial request. Its goal: the client can operate
everything without you, and you can find everything if they call back in a
year.

Rules:
- The handoff is a scheduled event (60–90 minutes), not a final email.
- Anything that had to be sent as a direct credential gets rotated to the
  client's own hands at handoff (access hygiene, see access checklist).
- The checklist completes before the final invoice is treated as done — the
  order protects both sides.

## A. Deliverables (they can open everything)

| 1 | Final files | [location] | [formats] | [ ] |
| 2 | Source/editable versions | [location] | [formats] | [ ] |
| 3 | A "how it's organized" README (10 lines) | [location] | md/pdf | [ ] |

Worked example: Fieldnote hands Copperline a Drive folder — `final/`
(exported files), `source/` (editable), `README.txt` (what's what), and the
photo-usage note for the 2024 library.

## B. Accounts & access (they hold the keys)

| # | System | Status at handoff | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [system] | [ ] client is owner/admin | transfer/invite completed |
| 2 | [system] | [ ] shared credential existed | rotated, client holds new |
| 3 | [system] | [ ] client-held by design | confirmed in writing |
| 4 | Your own team's seats on their accounts | [ ] removed or client-approved | revoke/keep, noted |

Worked example: Shopify admin fully Dana's (invite completed Oct 9);
Cloudflare zone — Marco's seat removed; Mailchimp editor seat — kept with
written approval for the 2-week support window, removed at support end.

## C. "Operate it without me" (the live walkthrough)

- [ ] 30–60 minute screen-share: the client performs each routine task while
      you watch (add a product, post an event, read analytics, update text)
- [ ] They do it twice; you talk only on the second pass
- [ ] Record the walkthrough (their OK) — the recording lives in their folder
- [ ] A one-page "run book": the 5 most common tasks, 3 lines each

Worked example: Dana posts a test event, edits the wholesale line, reads the
week's subscriptions — twice. Riley joins for the price-list edit. Recording
saved as `run-through-2026-11-04.mp4`.

## D. Documentation & knowledge

- [ ] The project's working docs handed over or archived visibly: scope
      worksheet, final milestones, CR log, Friday updates thread (exported)
- [ ] Known limitations written down (what this build does NOT handle — so
      next year's memory doesn't upgrade it)
- [ ] Where future fixes should start: [contact, contract, or "call us"]

## E. Support window (say it in numbers)

- [ ] Support window stated: [e.g. 14 days for bug-type issues on delivered
      work, from handoff date]
- [ ] What support covers / does not cover (one line each)
- [ ] After the window: [how new requests arrive — email, retainer, ad hoc]

## F. The administrative close

- [ ] Final invoice sent (after A–E are true)
- [ ] Offboarding survey sent (offboarding-survey-template.md) — 2 days after
      handoff, so the walkthrough is fresh
- [ ] Archive checklist executed (client-archive-checklist.md)
- [ ] Testimonial: if the survey response is warm, the ask goes out with the
      survey follow-up — using their own words back to them

## What success looks like

- The client performs the core tasks on the call without your help.
- A year later, a callback question takes you under five minutes to answer
  from the archive — folder, README, CR log.
- The survey response references how organized the ending felt. Endings are
  what clients describe to the next person who asks about you.
