# Sequence 2 — Borrowed trust (3 emails)

**When to use:** nobody knows you yet — no mutual client, no case study
wall. You borrow trust from a *real* connection point: a mutual contact, a
community you both belong to, content they engaged with, or their own
public work.

**Hard rule:** only name a person/community/content if the connection is
real and checkable. Fake name-dropping is the fastest way to burn a domain
reputation *and* your own.

---

## Email 1 — Day 1 · the honest bridge

**Subject:** `[Mutual context] → [First name]`

> Hi [First name],
>
> [One sentence naming the real bridge: "We're both in the [community]
> Slack — your breakdown of [topic] last week was the useful kind of
> blunt." / "Saw we share [connection]: [Name] suggested I reach out when
> I asked about teams doing [thing]." / "Your talk at [event] is the one
> I send to clients who ask about [topic]."]
>
> I work on [one-line, specific: what you do + for whom]. The reason I'm
> writing you specifically: [one true sentence — something observable
> about their situation that connects to your work].
>
> If [topic] is live for you right now: open to a short exchange? If not —
> no worries, and thanks for [the bridge thing].
>
> [Your name]

## Email 2 — Day 4–5 · give without asking

**Subject:** re: [same thread]

> [First name] — no reply needed to the last one; I made you something
> instead.
>
> [A specific, small, genuinely useful artifact: 3 observations about
> [their page/product/process] with one fix each, a short teardown of a
> competitor move relevant to them, a checklist for the problem you
> solve. Keep it under 200 words, inline — no attachments on cold mail.]
>
> Use it with or without me. If it sparks a question, I'm here.
>
> [Your name]

*This email does the qualifying for you: replies here are warm by
construction.*

## Email 3 — Day 10 · close-out with the door open

**Subject:** re: [same thread]

> Last note, [First name] — I'll stop here so I stay someone worth hearing
> from.
>
> If [topic] comes up after [their trigger: the launch / the new hire /
> Q1 planning], reply anytime in this thread — it reaches me directly.
>
> One ask, regardless: if a peer ever needs [what you do] and you liked
> the [artifact from email 2], a pass-along is the best thank-you.
>
> [Your name]

---

## Sourcing real bridges (30 seconds per prospect, before you write)

1. LinkedIn: mutual connections, groups, comments on each other's posts.
2. Communities you actually belong to (their member directory, event
   attendee lists, Slack channels) — only message people you've genuinely
   "seen around" or interacted with.
3. Their content: posts, podcast appearances, GitHub activity, conference
   talks. Quote specifics, never "loved your content".
4. Your own history: past colleagues, clients' partners, alumni networks.

If steps 1–4 all come up empty for a prospect, that person belongs in
Sequence 1 with deep personalization — not in a fake-referral email.

## Handling replies

Same protocol as Sequence 1, plus:

- **The bridge contact gets told.** If someone says "oh, [Name] sent you?",
  and [Name] actually introduced the topic — say so accurately. If they
  didn't, own it: "We're both in [community] — should have been clearer."
  Truth travels in small industries.
- **"Send it to my colleague [X]":** that's a referral — thank them, and
  start [X] on Sequence 1 with the colleague's name as the bridge.
