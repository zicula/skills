# Sequence 1 — Problem-first (4 emails)

**When to use:** you can name a specific, verifiable problem the company has
right now, and your offer fixes it. This is the workhorse sequence for
selling services to small/mid companies.

**Rules that apply to every email below (from SETUP.md Ch. 5–6):**
plain text, ≤120 words, one question per email, no link shorteners, real
signature. Follow-ups are *new information*, never "just bumping this".

Tokens in `[brackets]` — every one must be replaced with something
true. If you can't fill a token honestly, this is the wrong prospect
(or you haven't done the personalization SOP yet).

---

## Email 1 — Day 1 · the observed problem

**Subject:** `[their thing] and [problem]`

> Hi [First name],
>
> [One true sentence about them: something from their site, job post, or a
> recent change — e.g. "Saw you're hiring two SDRs for the Berlin team."]
>
> That usually means [the concrete problem your work solves] becomes the
> next bottleneck — [one specific symptom you can explain in a clause].
>
> I [one line: who you help and the outcome, e.g. "run outbound audits for
> B2B teams that just built their first sales pod"].
>
> Worth a 15-minute call, or is this not a priority this quarter?
>
> [Your name]

*Why it works: the problem is tied to an observable fact, so the first line
can't be a template. The question gives an easy out — replies (even "not
now") are wins for deliverability.*

## Email 2 — Day 4–5 · proof, not pressure

**Subject:** re: [same thread — reply to your own email]

> [First name] — one addition.
>
> [One concrete artifact of your work: a 3-bullet mini-audit of something
> public of theirs, a relevant before/after from a similar engagement —
> anonymized if needed, or a short observation about their category.]
>
> If [problem] is on the roadmap for [next quarter / after the hire
> lands], I'll show you what fixing it looked like for [peer company] — 15
> minutes, no deck.
>
> [Your name]

*Never invent a client or a result. If you don't have a peer example yet,
use the mini-audit — generosity with real observation converts.*

## Email 3 — Day 8–9 · new angle

**Subject:** re: [same thread]

> Different thought, [First name]:
>
> Most teams in [their situation] try [common approach] first. It usually
> stalls because [one honest sentence of insight you actually believe].
>
> The teams that skip that step do [one-sentence alternative]. That's the
> version I'd sketch for [company] — takes me about an hour to outline.
>
> Want the outline, even if we never talk? Happy to just send it.
>
> [Your name]

*"Even if we never talk" lowers the cost of saying yes. Low-pressure emails
get answered, and replies keep the thread healthy.*

## Email 4 — Day 14–15 · the close-out

**Subject:** re: [same thread]

> Last one from me, [First name] — closing the loop so I'm not the person
> who emails forever.
>
> If [problem] moves up the list after [trigger: the hires start / the
> funding lands / the quarter turns], the outline offer stands: just reply
> "outline" and I'll send it within a day.
>
> Otherwise, good luck with [genuine, specific well-wish].
>
> [Your name]

*A real break-up: after this, the thread is dead. Mark the prospect
`Nurture` in the tracker — people change jobs and priorities; you may
legitimately re-open in a new quarter with a new trigger.*

---

## Handling replies (this is the actual job)

- **Interested ("sure, when?"):** reply within the same working day with 2
  concrete time slots + your calendar link. Then log `Meeting` and stop the
  sequence.
- **"Not now":** "Understood — want me to check back after [trigger]?"
  Log `Nurture` with a date.
- **"We use someone":** "Makes sense — mind if I send the outline anyway?
  Useful as a second opinion." Some of your best replies come from here.
- **"Remove me":** reply once — "Done, you won't hear from me again." —
  and mark `Opted-out` in the tracker. Never email that address again.
  This is both politeness and how you keep complaints near zero.
- **Hard bounce:** remove everywhere same day; check whether you mis-sourced
  the address (see SOP).
