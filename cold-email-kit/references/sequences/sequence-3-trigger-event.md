# Sequence 3 — Trigger-event (3 emails)

**When to use:** a company did something that *changes their priorities
right now* — funding round, key hire, product launch, office move,
migration announcement, pricing change. Timing does the persuading; your
email just connects the event to a next step.

**List shape:** this sequence is built for watching a *small, curated*
list — 20–100 companies you chose on purpose — not a scraped feed. Set a
weekly 30-minute ritual to refresh triggers (see the sourcing section).

---

## Email 1 — Day 0–1 of the trigger · connect, don't congratulate

**Subject:** `[specific event] — quick thought`

> Hi [First name],
>
> Congrats on [the event — be specific: "the $12M Series A with [lead
> investor]" not "the funding"].
>
> [One sentence of what that event *predicts*: "Series A marketing teams
> usually discover their outbound data is the weak link around month two —
> hires come first, infrastructure second."]
>
> That's exactly the gap I work in: [one line, specific]. With [company]
> heading into [the phase], it may be worth a short talk before [the
> predictable pain] shows up.
>
> Open to 15 minutes this week or next?
>
> [Your name]

*Send within 48 hours of the trigger becoming public. The value is the
timing; a congrats email in week 3 is noise.*

## Email 2 — Day 4–5 · the concrete plan for their phase

**Subject:** re: [same thread]

> [First name] — since [company] is at [phase], here's the order I'd do
> things in your seat:
>
> 1. [Step one, concrete — one clause]
> 2. [Step two — one clause]
> 3. [Step three — one clause]
>
> Teams that reorder these usually hit [specific stall]. Step 2 is where I
> can carry real weight — [one clause on what you'd actually deliver].
>
> Want me to turn that into a one-page plan for [company]? Free, whether
> or not we work together.
>
> [Your name]

## Email 3 — Day 10–12 · quiet close-out

**Subject:** re: [same thread]

> Closing the loop, [First name].
>
> The one-page offer stands for another week — reply "plan" and it's
> yours. After that I'll assume the timing is wrong and leave you be.
>
> Either way: enjoy the [event momentum — "the hiring sprint"]. [Company]
> came up on my watch list for a reason.
>
> [Your name]

---

## Sourcing triggers honestly (your weekly 30-minute ritual)

Sources that are public and fine to use:

- Funding: company blog/press page, investor announcements, local business
  press
- Hiring: the company's own careers page and job posts (role = signal:
  first SDR hire = outbound build-out; first data hire = reporting pain)
- Product: launch posts, changelogs, app-store release notes
- Events: conference speaker announcements, webinar pages, meetup talks
- Their content: CEO/founder posts announcing direction changes

What is **not** okay: mass-scraping profiles you never read, buying lead
lists, or automating "congrats" blasts on triggers you can't even name.
Apart from being against this kit's rules, trigger emails fail loudly when
the personalization is fake — one wrong "congrats on the round" to someone
who *didn't* raise costs you the domain's reputation with their whole
team.

## Handling replies

Same protocol as Sequence 1, plus:

- **Trigger missed ("that was last year"):** own it — "Behind the times,
  sorry — worth a look anyway, or shall I check back at your next
  milestone?" Log the correction in the tracker so it never repeats.
- **"We handled it in-house":** offer the one-page plan anyway; in-house
  teams become buyers when they hit the stall you predicted.
