# SOP-23 · Alumni Referral Program

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Offboarding |
| **Owner** | Founder |
| **Trigger** | Handover complete (SOP-21) · testimonial approved (SOP-22) · quarterly value email cycle |
| **Time** | 10 minutes at handover · 1 hour per quarter maintaining |
| **Output** | A referral engine made of people who already know your work — the cheapest, warmest pipeline a small agency can own |

## When to use

At every project close and then once a quarter, forever. Past clients ("alumni") refer more freely than current ones — the project's results are proven, and recommending you costs them nothing but a forward. Most agencies never ask because the project "ended." That's the entire opportunity: for the client, the relationship didn't end; the *invoice* did.

## Copy-paste: the referral ask (at handover + testimonial approval, two separate asks)

The referral ask is distinct from the testimonial ask — never bundle them into one email with two favors (you'll get neither):

> Hi [NAME] — one more thing now that [PROJECT] is wrapped, and it's a good one: **who else do you know with the [PROBLEM YOU SOLVED] problem?**
>
> Not asking you to sell for me — one name and a "you two should talk" is the whole job. I'll take it from there with zero awkwardness (they'll get value from the first call whether we work together or not).
>
> If someone comes to mind, a one-line intro by email is perfect. And thank you — [RESULT] was a pleasure.

## The forwardable blurb (give them the ammunition)

People refer when it's effortless. Attach a 60-word blurb they can forward untouched:

> *Forwarding this because [YOUR NAME] did [SPECIFIC RESULT, e.g., "our site rebuild — leads up 40% in 3 months"] for us. [HE/SHE/THEY] run(s) [AGENCY], working with [NICHE] on [SERVICES]. First call is genuinely useful even if you don't hire — [HE/SHE/THEY] does a teardown of your [ASSET] with no strings. [YOUR EMAIL / CALENDAR LINK]*

Refresh the blurb's headline number quarterly (newest case study, SOP-22). A stale number is a stale pipeline.

## The thank-you (always, same week)

> Hi [NAME] — [REFERRAL NAME] and I just talked; great human, exactly the kind of project I like. Thank you for the intro.
>
> Small token: [REFERRAL THANK-YOU — e.g., "the next [X] hours on any future project for you are on me" / a gift / a donation they'd like]. No obligation ever attaches to this — the intro was the gift.

**Check the rules of your jurisdiction and their industry** before offering cash commissions (some regulated industries and government clients forbid it); a gift, discount, or free hours is the safe universal default.

## The alumni rhythm (1 hour per quarter)

1. **List:** every past client in one sheet: name, project, last result, last contact date.
2. **One value email per quarter** — no pitch: the new case study, one insight useful to *their* business, one line of "door's open" (this doubles as SOP-24's nurture bed).
3. **Three personal touches per quarter:** congratulate a launch, comment on their news, send a relevant article *they* specifically would use. alumni ≠ mailing list; alumni = 30 humans who know your work.
4. **Annual audit:** who referred in the last 12 months? Thank them again (differently). Who went quiet 18+ months? They're SOP-24 candidates now.

## Rules & red flags

- Ask at the *high point* (handover, testimonial, results call) — never during a low, and never more than once per project cycle.
- Treat referred leads like royalty, visibly: faster SLA on the first reply (SOP-01 fast-track), and report back to the referrer *with their permission to share progress* — referrers who see outcomes refer twice.
- Never let a referred project go badly without telling the referrer first. Protect the referral triangle's trust above the single project's revenue.
- Don't pay for referrals where it's prohibited (regulated industries, public sector) and always keep it transparent — sleazy referral economics eventually reach the client's ears.

## What good looks like

- 25%+ of new business arriving by referral within a year of running this rhythm (it starts slower; compounds fast).
- Every quarter: one alumni email sent, three personal touches logged, blurb number refreshed.
- The phrase "I heard about you from [PAST CLIENT]" — arriving monthly — is this SOP compounding.

## Adapt it with AI

> "Write my referral ask, my 60-word forwardable blurb (latest result: [NUMBER/PROJECT]), and my quarterly alumni email for [NICHE] past clients. My referral thank-you is [GIFT/DISCOUNT/HOURS]. Warm, zero-pressure tone — these people liked working with me; the emails should feel like me, not a newsletter."
