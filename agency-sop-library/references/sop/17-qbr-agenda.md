# SOP-17 · Quarterly Business Review (QBR) Agenda

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Retention |
| **Owner** | Founder / account owner |
| **Trigger** | Every 90 days per retainer or multi-project client — booked 3 weeks ahead |
| **Time** | 60 minutes live + 60 minutes prep |
| **Output** | A re-anchored relationship: goals re-confirmed, roadmap pitched, renewal smoothed, upsell door opened (SOP-18) |

## When to use

Quarterly, for every retainer and every client past project #2. The QBR is the highest-ROI hour in agency retention: it converts monthly reports (SOP-16) into a strategic story, surfaces churn risk while it's still fixable, and is the single best venue to pitch bigger work — from the client's side of the table.

## Prep (60 minutes, the day before)

- Pull the 3 monthly reports (SOP-16) into one trend line: what's the *story* of the quarter?
- Ask the honest internal question: *"If this QBR went badly, what would they cut first?"* Build that answer into the agenda.
- Prep ONE roadmap pitch (SOP-18 grammar) tied to their stated goal — never a services menu.
- Send agenda 3 days ahead; ask them to add one agenda item: *"What should we add? It's your hour."*

## The agenda (60 minutes, timeboxed)

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | **Results vs. goals** | The quarter in one trend line + the 3 KPI reads that matter. Numbers they can't see in monthly slices. |
| 10–20 | **Stop / Start / Continue** | You go first (vulnerability licenses honesty): one thing you'd stop, one start, one double-down on. Then theirs. Write it live. |
| 20–25 | **What changed on your side** | Their market, team, priorities. The quarter's roadmap is worthless if their goal moved — re-anchor first. |
| 25–40 | **Next-quarter roadmap pitch** | One proposal: *"Given [THEIR GOAL], I'd focus next quarter on [PLAN]. It targets [METRIC]. Options and prices in the follow-up."* This block is why the QBR exists. |
| 40–50 | **Commercial housekeeping** | Renewal date, scope tweaks, budget reality — stated plainly: *"Contract renews [DATE]; based on today, I'd propose [SHAPE]."* No renewal conversation should ever happen in the renewal month for the first time. |
| 50–60 | **The honest question** | *"Last 5 minutes, off the record: how are we doing as a partner — honestly? What would make this a 10?"* Then the last word: *"Anything we should start doing for someone ELSE on your team?"* (referral seed, SOP-23). |

## Copy-paste: the QBR invite + follow-up

> **Invite (3 weeks out):** Subject: [CLIENT] × [AGENCY] — Q3 review & next-quarter plan
> Hi [NAME] — 60 minutes to look at the quarter together: what the numbers say, what we'd each stop/start, and a concrete plan for next quarter. [BOOKING LINKS — offer 3]. Add anything you want covered; it's your hour.
>
> **Follow-up (same day):** Thanks [NAME] — recap in one line each: results: [X] · stop: [X] · start: [X] · next quarter: [PITCH ONE-LINER] (estimate by [DATE]) · renewal: [SHAPE, DATE]. And thanks for the straight answer on the last question — [COMMITMENT FROM IT].

## Rules & red flags

- Never cancel a QBR for delivery pressure. The cancelled QBR is how the client's boss concludes the agency is "just execution" — and execution gets outsourced to the cheapest bidder.
- If the client won't come ("just send it over"), send the report stack + a 5-bullet QBR-in-writing and flag the account yellow in SOP-19: a client who won't spend an hour per quarter on the partnership is already halfway out.
- One pitch per QBR. Two pitches turn a strategy hour into a sales meeting.
- The honest question block is the churn radar: anything said there gets an SOP-19 action within the week — asking and ignoring is worse than not asking.

## What good looks like

- QBR attendance rate above 80% — clients protect the hour because it produces their own quarterly plan.
- At least half of QBRs produce a next-quarter pitch that becomes signed work (that's the SOP-18 funnel, fed).
- Renewals signed before the renewal month, without discount pressure, referencing "what we planned at the QBR".
