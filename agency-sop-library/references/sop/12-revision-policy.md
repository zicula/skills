# SOP-12 · Revision & Change Request Policy

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Delivery |
| **Owner** | Project lead |
| **Trigger** | Any client feedback that isn't plain approval |
| **Time** | 10 minutes to triage each feedback batch |
| **Output** | A clean line between "included revision", "new request", and "new scope" — and invoices that match |

## When to use

From the first design draft to the last site fix. Revision chaos is the #1 profit leak in small agencies — not because clients are evil, but because nobody defined the units. This SOP defines the units, and the proposal (SOP-03 §7) and kickoff (SOP-07) already told the client about them.

## The definitions (put these in every proposal)

> **Revision round** = one consolidated set of feedback on one deliverable, from one contact, within the feedback SLA (SOP-09). Each deliverable includes **[2] rounds**.
> **New request** = feedback on something already approved, or additions inside the deliverable's spirit ("make the hero animation too"). Small ones are goodwill; a pattern of them is scope.
> **New scope** = new pages/features/channels/audiences, or reworking approved work after the client's own goals changed. New scope gets an estimate (SOP-14). Always friendly, always in writing.

## Triage (10 minutes per batch)

1. **De-merge the pile.** Client emails arrive as one anxious stream. Split into: revision items on deliverable X (round 1 or 2?), new requests, and scope.
2. **Count the round.** Round 1 and 2: do the work, no conversation needed. Round 3+: this is either a new request (goodwill, once) or new scope (SOP-14 email). Name which, kindly.
3. **One contact rule.** Feedback from five stakeholders in one round counts as *five rounds* only if you let it. Reply: "To spend your rounds well, I need one consolidated list from [NAME] — here's what I have from everyone so far, merged." Then you merge it *for* them. This one email saves more hours than any other message in this library.
4. **Ambiguous items get a clarifying question before work starts.** "Tweak the tone" costs a question to scope and three days to guess.

## Copy-paste assets

**The merge email (multiple stakeholders):**

> Hi [NAME] — collecting everyone's thoughts on the homepage draft: I've merged [PERSON 1]'s and [PERSON 2]'s notes with yours into one list (attached). Anything missing? Once you confirm this is the complete round-1 list, we'll turn it around in [X days].

**The round-3 pivot:**

> Hi [NAME] — happy to keep shaping this. Round 2 was the last of the two included in the proposal, so here's how I suggest we handle the new items: [SMALL ONES] I'll just do them, no charge — call it polish. [BIGGER ONES] feel like new scope; I'll send a short estimate by [DATE] so you can decide with the price visible. Sound right?

**The new-scope reply (fast, friendly, firm):**

> Hi [NAME] — good idea, and it's outside what we scoped, so here's the estimate instead of a surprise: [DESCRIPTION] → [HOURS × RATE = PRICE], [DAYS] of work. Reply "approved" and it joins the current sprint; the current milestone dates hold either way.

## Rules & red flags

- **Approve before you build on it.** Work stacked on unapproved work multiplies rework. "Marking [X] approved in the doc — shout within [SLA] if not" is how approvals get un-stuck.
- Goodwill is a budget, not a habit: free small requests are fine at ~1 per project *visibly* ("that one's on me"). Invisible freebies become the baseline.
- Never argue taste. If it's round 3 and pure preference ("I just don't like it"), that's what round 3 pricing is *for* — quote it without resentment. The policy absorbs the emotion.
- The client who "didn't know" about rounds: the policy was in the proposal §7 and kickoff §4. Re-send that section, not a lecture.

## What good looks like

- 90%+ of projects end within their included rounds.
- Unbilled "just one more thing" hours: under 5% of project hours (track it once — the number will scare you into using the merge email).
- Clients describe you as "structured but not bureaucratic" in testimonials (SOP-22) — that's this SOP, felt from the outside.

Keep the 'one contact' and 'goodwill budget' rules."
