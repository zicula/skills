# SOP-20 · Client Feedback Pulse

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Retention |
| **Owner** | Project lead (sends), founder (acts on low scores) |
| **Trigger** | Automatically at every milestone (M1, M2, launch) and at project midpoint |
| **Time** | 5 minutes to send · a 30-minute call when a score is low |
| **Output** | Unhappiness surfaced while it's still a fixable moment — not saved up for the end-of-project survey, or worse, for the renewal call |

## When to use

At every milestone, mechanically. Waiting until project end to ask "how did we do?" is archaeology: the moment the client got frustrated in week 3 is long gone, and by then their story about you has already hardened. The pulse catches the week-3 moment in week 3.

## The pulse (3 questions — no more, or response rate dies)

Sent by the project lead, as a plain email (no survey links for a 1–10 person agency; the format should feel like a person, not a form):

> Subject: 30 seconds of honesty about [MILESTONE]?
>
> Hi [NAME] — we just hit [MILESTONE]; before we move to the next phase, three quick questions:
>
> 1. 0–10: how likely are you to recommend us to another [THEIR INDUSTRY] after this phase?
> 2. What should we do differently in the next phase?
> 3. Anything about communication you'd change — frequency, format, channel?
>
> Straight answers only — a "7" with a reason is worth more to me than a polite "10". Takes 30 seconds.

## The routing rule (this is the SOP)

| Score | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **9–10** | Promoter | Thank + ask the testimonial question *now* (SOP-22 — the peak-happiness moment is happening) |
| **7–8** | Quiet doubt | Founder (not project lead) replies within 48h: *"Thanks for the 8 — what would make it a 9? Straight answer appreciated."* Then actually change one thing and say so. |
| **0–6** | Fire | **Call within 24h.** Email moves the account toward churn; only a voice call catches it. Script below. Log it in SOP-19's scorecard as a 🔴 sentiment signal. |

## Copy-paste: the recovery call opener (score 0–6)

> "[NAME], thanks for taking this — I saw your score and I'm calling before anything else, because a [X] from you means we did something real. No defense, I just want the story straight: what happened, from your side?"

Then: listen, take notes, repeat their three points back, propose ONE concrete fix with a date, and close with: *"I'll fix [X] by [DATE] and you'll see it in the next update (SOP-11). If it's not visibly better in two weeks, say the word."*

Follow up in writing the same day — 4 lines: what you heard, what you'll change, by when, how they'll see it.

## Rules & red flags

- **Never send the pulse and argue with the answer.** A client who takes the risk to be honest and gets a rebuttal teaches themselves to say "10" forever — and you lose the instrument.
- Pulse fatigue is real: 3 questions, ≤ 4× per project, only at milestones. Two pulses with no visible response to feedback = you've trained them that surveys here are theater.
- The 7–8 band is where accounts are *saved*; treat it with more urgency than the 0–6 (you can feel a 0–6; the 8 that quietly becomes a 6 is the one that kills retainers).
- Non-responders are a signal too: two ignored pulses = flag in SOP-19 and ask about it directly at the next touch ("didn't want to spam you with the survey — how's it actually going?").
- Aggregate the scores quarterly: pulse trend per client feeds the SOP-19 scorecard and, at 9+ averages, the testimonial/case-study pipeline (SOP-22).

## What good looks like

- 70%+ pulse response rate (plain email, 3 questions, visible follow-through gets you there).
- Every 0–6 has a same-week call and a written fix commitment — and most of those accounts finish the project as promoters.
- At project end there are zero surprise-complaints, because every real complaint already had its moment.
