# SOP-07 · Kickoff Meeting Agenda

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Onboarding |
| **Owner** | Project lead |
| **Trigger** | Welcome packet sent (SOP-06); kickoff booked within 5 business days of signing |
| **Time** | 60 minutes live + 20 minutes prep |
| **Output** | Confirmed success metrics, confirmed scope, dated access list, comms contract — in a doc the client saw being written |

## When to use

On every project start, even for clients you've worked with for years — scope creep enters through un-kicked-off projects.

## Prep (20 minutes)

- Send agenda 24h before: "60 minutes: goals (10) · scope walk (15) · how we work (10) · access (10) · risks (5) · next steps (5). Please bring whoever signs off on feedback."
- Pre-fill the access checklist (SOP-08) with what you already have; bring the gaps as named asks with owners.
- Open a live doc during the call and type in front of them — decisions written live get remembered; decisions from your memory afterward get renegotiated.

## The agenda (60 minutes, timeboxed)

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Why we're here** | Restate the project in one sentence from the proposal. "Correct?" — get the yes. |
| 5–15 | **Goals, out loud** | "Six months from now, [METRIC FROM SOP-02] — is that still the number?" Fix the number *and* the measurement method now ("we'll read it from GA4, goal X"). |
| 15–30 | **Scope walk** | Walk the included/not-included lists (SOP-03 §3) bullet by bullet. Every "oh, and also…" gets written into the parking lot, not the scope: "Good idea — noted; I'll send an estimate for it separately (SOP-14)." |
| 30–40 | **How we work** | SOP-09 live: update day, channels, feedback SLA (2 business days), single contact per side, revision rounds (SOP-12). Client reads the same rules they got in the welcome packet — confirmation, not revelation. |
| 40–50 | **Access & dependencies** | Walk the gap list: item → owner name → date. "Whom do I chase if [ITEM] is late?" — get a name. |
| 50–55 | **Risks, spoken early** | Name 2–3 things that could slip this project and what you'll do about each (e.g., "approval rounds are the usual culprit — that's why feedback has an SLA"). |
| 55–60 | **Read-back** | "Three things we agreed: [1, 2, 3]. Two things you owe me, by [DATE]: [A, B]. Next update lands [DAY]." End 2 minutes early. |

## Copy-paste kickoff confirmation email (same day)

> Subject: Kickoff recap — what we agreed, what we need by [DATE]
>
> Hi [NAME] — great session. Recap so we're locked in:
>
> **Success metric:** [METRIC], measured via [METHOD].
> **Scope:** as walked through — attached doc includes the not-included list (it's there to protect both of us).
> **You owe us by [DATE]:** [ITEM 1 — OWNER], [ITEM 2 — OWNER].
> **We owe you:** first weekly update on [DAY], access chase on [DATE] if anything's missing.
>
> Anything mis-stated above, reply and I'll correct the doc. Otherwise this is our baseline. Onward —
> [YOUR NAME]

## Rules & red flags

- Kickoff happens **before** any production work. Work that starts before kickoff gets re-litigated in month two.
- No kickoff without the decision-influencer present or a named proxy for feedback. "We'll fill in the boss later" is a dated risk in your risk block.
- Parking-lot every out-of-scope idea visibly — clients behave when they watch their idea get *estimated later* instead of silently swallowed.
- If the client shows up having "quickly chatted with the team and we were thinking bigger…" — stop, re-scope on the call, and re-confirm in writing before proceeding. Ten minutes of awkward now, or ten hours of scope arbitration later.

## What good looks like

- The recap email is answered with zero corrections (or corrected within a day — even better, it proves they read it).
- Access items arrive by the promised dates at a rate above 80% (chase the rest on the promised date — see SOP-15).
- First weekly update (SOP-11) needs no new information beyond what kickoff already established.
