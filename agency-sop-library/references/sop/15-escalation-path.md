# SOP-15 · Client Escalation Path

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Delivery |
| **Owner** | Project lead → founder |
| **Trigger** | Unanswered nudges · blocked dependency · sentiment shift · overdue invoice |
| **Time** | 20 minutes per escalation, done early |
| **Output** | Problems de-escalated with a paper trail — never a surprise confrontation |

## When to use

At the **first signal** of any of these four:

| Trigger | Signal |
|---|---|
| Silence | 2 nudges unanswered (SOP-08 chasing loop exhausted) |
| Blockage | A missed client dependency blocks work ≥ 2 days |
| Sentiment shift | Tone turns cold; "as per my last email"; boss suddenly cc'd; feedback arrives at 23:00 marked "urgent" |
| Money | Invoice overdue > 7 days |

Small agencies get hurt by escalations they *feel* but never *write down*. This SOP converts discomfort into dated, quotable facts — which is what lets both sides stay professional.

## The ladder (one rung at a time, each rung documented)

**Rung 1 — Project-lead nudge (in writing).** The chasing email from SOP-08, with the consequence attached: *"Without [ITEM], [PHASE] slips to [DATE] — flagging now so we can decide together."* Wait [2 business days].

**Rung 2 — Owner call (voice, not email).** Fifteen minutes, human to human: *"Wanted to make sure nothing's wrong on your side — [ITEM] is holding M2 and I'd rather solve it with you than around you."* Sentiment shifts get solved here or they don't get solved. Send a 3-line recap email after the call.

**Rung 3 — Written impact summary.** One page, facts only, copied to whoever now needs to see it:

> **Impact summary — [PROJECT], [DATE]**
> **Situation:** [Dependency X due DATE — not received; 2 emails (DATES) + call (DATE)].
> **Effect:** [M2 slips A→B; launch C→D; cost effect if any — e.g., "the paid campaign's booked flight time lapses"].
> **Options:** (a) [ITEM] by [DATE] → original plan mostly holds, (b) [ALTERNATIVE], (c) re-baseline.
> **Our ask:** pick a path by [DATE].

**Rung 4 — Formal pause notice (rare; founder sends it).** For unpaid invoices > 14 days or a relationship that's genuinely stuck:

> Per our agreement, we're pausing work on [PROJECT] as of [DATE] until [PAYMENT RECEIVED / DEPENDENCY RESOLVED]. Work in flight is safe; [WHAT PAUSES]. We value this project and pause to keep the math honest rather than absorb it silently and let it sour the relationship. One call reopens everything: [BOOKING LINK].

## Copy-paste: the sentiment-shift opener (rung 2, adapted)

> Hi [NAME] — the last few exchanges felt off, and I'd rather ask than guess: did something in the [REVIEW/LAUNCH/REPORT] land wrong? If we missed the mark, tell me straight — I'd rather fix a real complaint than a polite silence. 15 minutes this week?

## Rules & red flags

- **One rung at a time, and never skip to rung 4 by surprise.** The power of the pause notice comes from the three documented rungs before it.
- Emotions get calls; facts get emails. Do them in that order — call first, then the recap email that makes the facts permanent.
- Never absorb silently. Every week you absorb a blockage quietly is a week the client's memory records as *the project going fine* — until the invoice or the deadline surfaces the difference, and then *you* look like the surprise.
- If YOU are the one who slipped (missed a date, shipped a bug): skip the ladder, self-report immediately with the fix plan (SOP-11 at-risk protocol). Escalation SOPs are for two-way streets; your own slippage is a one-way obligation.
- Two escalations in one project = a health flag: schedule an SOP-19 review and ask the account question ("is this project structured right for both of us?").

## What good looks like

- Escalations resolved at rung 1–2 at a rate above 80%; rung 4 happens less than once a year and is never a shock when it does.
- Every escalation above rung 1 has a dated paper trail you could hand to an accountant — or a lawyer — without editing.
- Clients later say some version of "you were tough to work with in exactly the right ways." That's this SOP, remembered fondly.
