# SOP-16 · Monthly Report Skeleton

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Retention |
| **Owner** | Account owner |
| **Trigger** | Same day every month per retainer client ([1st business day] is common) |
| **Time** | 45–60 minutes per client |
| **Output** | A one-page report the client actually reads — and that makes the retainer look like the best line in their budget |

## When to use

Every month, every retainer. The monthly report is not documentation — it is your **retention weapon**. Retainers don't die from bad work; they die from invisible value. One well-made page per month is the difference between "why are we paying them?" and the QBR upgrade conversation (SOP-17/18).

## Copy-paste: the skeleton (one page, always this order)

> **[CLIENT] × [AGENCY] — Monthly report — [MONTH]**
>
> **1. The verdict (2 sentences).** What moved, what it means, and the one decision to make. *"Leads from the campaign are up 34% at a lower cost per lead — the win here is [X]; the decision is whether we scale spend in [MONTH]."*
> Never start with traffic charts. Start with "so what".
>
> **2. Numbers vs. targets.** A table of [3–7] KPIs — the ones from SOP-07's success metric, nothing else:
>
> | KPI | This month | Last month | Target | Read |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | [Leads] | [128] | [95] | [110] | ✅ beat |
>
> Rule: every number gets a **Read** cell — ✅ beat / ⚠️ watch / ❌ miss + five words why. Vanity metrics (impressions, followers) are banned unless tied to the goal.
>
> **3. What we did.** 4–6 bullets, outcome-worded: *"Rebuilt onboarding email flow (5 emails) — live [DATE]"*, not *"worked on emails"*.
>
> **4. What we learned.** 1–2 real observations with evidence: *"Visitors from LinkedIn convert 2× those from search — worth shifting [X]."* This section is why you're a partner, not a vendor.
>
> **5. Next month + asks.** The plan in 3 bullets + anything you need ("a quote from your ops team for the case study — 10 minutes").

## Copy-paste: filled example (the two hardest sections)

> **1. The verdict.** *Leads beat target for the second month (128 vs 110) at a cost-per-lead 18% lower — the campaign is scaling predictably now. The decision this month: approve the +30% budget test for November; the report below shows why the risk is low.*
>
> **2. Numbers vs. targets.**
>
> | KPI | This month | Last month | Target | Read |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Qualified leads | 128 | 95 | 110 | ✅ 2nd beat in a row |
> | Cost per lead | $41 | $53 | ≤$50 | ✅ down 3 months straight |
> | Lead → call rate | 22% | 31% | ≥25% | ⚠️ volume up, follow-up lagging — fix is on your side (SLA), script attached |
> | Retainer hours used | 38/40 | 40/40 | — | 🟢 healthy |

Note what the ⚠️ row does: it raises the client's own follow-up SLA problem *with a fix attached*, in a document they forward to their boss. That's the report doing account management while you sleep.

## Rules & red flags

- **Send before the monthly call, never present it cold.** The call then starts at "you've read the report — what stood out?" — thirty minutes of decisions instead of thirty minutes of narration.
- A miss gets reported like a win with a plan: ❌ row + five words + one line in the verdict about the fix. Hiding a miss for one cycle costs you the account on discovery; reporting it with a plan builds more trust than a clean month.
- Numbers must reconcile with whatever the client sees in their own dashboard. If your GA4 and their CRM disagree, name the discrepancy in section 2 — the month they catch an unexplained mismatch, every previous number becomes suspect.
- 45-minute cap per report. If it takes longer, you're reporting from too many sources — fix the UTM/tracking setup (SOP-13), not the report.

## What good looks like

- Client forwards the report to their boss with no edits. That forward is the KPI of this SOP.
- "The report is great but we have questions" meetings: shrinking, because the Reads answer them pre-emptively.
- Renewal conversations (SOP-19) reference reports, not memory: *"In your March report we flagged X…"* — six months of forwarded PDFs are your renewal argument, pre-written.
