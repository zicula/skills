# SOP-03 · Proposal Template Structure

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Sales |
| **Owner** | Founder / sales lead |
| **Trigger** | Discovery call ends with "send the proposal" agreed (SOP-02) |
| **Time** | 60–90 minutes per proposal |
| **Output** | 8-section proposal with 3 priced options + a booked walkthrough |

## When to use

Every proposal, no exceptions. The structure below exists because proposals fail for predictable reasons: they describe you instead of the client, they present one take-it-or-leave-it price, and they die in the "looks good, let me think" inbox.

## The 8 sections (in this order)

1. **Their world (100 words).** Open with the problem in *their* words from SOP-02 notes — at least two verbatim phrases. Zero sentences about your company.
2. **The outcome.** One paragraph: what will be true after the work, tied to the success metric they named. Name the metric in their units (revenue, leads, hours saved) — never in yours (pages, features).
3. **Scope — what's included.** Deliverables as a bulleted list, each starting with a verb. Under it, **"What's not included"** — 3–5 bullets of adjacent things you're explicitly *not* doing. This list is what saves you in month two (see SOP-12).
4. **Three options.** See pricing logic in SOP-04. Same layout for each: name, one-line promise, bullet scope, price. Middle option is the one you recommend — say so: "Most clients in your situation choose Option B."
5. **Timeline with client-side dependencies.** Week-by-week table, with a **"We need from you"** column on every phase. The timeline is honest only if their homework has dates too.
6. **Proof.** Two mini case snippets (situation → action → number) and one testimonial quote. If you have no numbers yet, use process proof: relevant work samples + how you de-risk (weekly updates, revision rounds).
7. **Terms that matter.** Payment [50/50 or 3 milestones] · [2] revision rounds included per deliverable · IP transfers on final payment · response times on both sides (SOP-09). Plain language, no legalese walls.
8. **One call to action + expiry.** "Reply 'Option B' and I'll send the contract today — pricing held until [DATE +14 days]." One button, one choice. No "let us know if you have questions" endings.

## Copy-paste skeleton

> **Proposal: [PROJECT NAME] for [CLIENT]**
> Prepared for [CONTACT, TITLE] · [DATE] · Valid until [DATE +14]
>
> **1. Where you are today.** [VERBATIM PHRASE 1]. [VERBATIM PHRASE 2]. The cost of leaving this alone for another six months: [CONSEQUENCE THEY NAMED].
>
> **2. What "done" looks like.** By [END DATE]: [SUCCESS METRIC IN THEIR UNITS].
>
> **3. Included / Not included.** [BULLETS] / [BULLETS]
>
> **4. Three ways to do this.** Option A [SCOPE] — $[PRICE] · Option B (recommended) [SCOPE] — $[PRICE] · Option C [SCOPE] — $[PRICE]
>
> **5. Timeline.** [TABLE: phase | weeks | we deliver | we need from you]
>
> **6. Why me.** [2 case snippets + 1 quote]
>
> **7. Terms.** [PAYMENT] · [REVISION ROUNDS] · [IP ON FINAL PAYMENT] · [COMMS SLAs]
>
> **8. Next step.** Reply "Option [A/B/C]" → contract and invoice within 24 hours. Pricing held until [DATE].

## Rules & red flags

- Send within **48 hours** of the call — acceptance rates decay hard after that.
- Never send a proposal without a **booked walkthrough** (SOP-02 close). Email-only proposals die silently.
- One proposal = one PDF/link, three options inside it. Never email pricing piecemeal.
- If they ask you to "just send a rough number" before a call: give the SOP-02 range verbally, then insist on the call. Ranges before calls, prices after.
- Reuse the skeleton, never the client paragraphs. Personalized sections 1, 2 and 5; the rest stays standard.

## What good looks like

- Proposals sent per month, and % accepted, tracked in one sheet.
- Client opens by quoting your "not included" list back to you approvingly — that's when scope trouble is already dead.
- Accepted proposals quote the outcome section in the client's first reply: "Can't wait to hit [METRIC]."

## Adapt it with AI

> "Here's my proposal skeleton: [PASTE SECTION LIST]. I sell [SERVICES] to [NICHE]. Draft section 2 (outcome) and section 3 (included/not included) for this project: [PASTE DISCOVERY NOTES]. Write 'not included' as 5 adjacent services my clients commonly assume are part of the deal."
