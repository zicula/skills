# SOP-14 · Scope Change Estimate (Change Orders)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Delivery |
| **Owner** | Project lead |
| **Trigger** | Any "can you also just…" during delivery |
| **Time** | 30–60 minutes per estimate |
| **Output** | A priced, approved-or-declined change order within 1 business day — and a project margin that survives contact with clients |

## When to use

The moment an out-of-scope idea appears (kickoff parking lot, SOP-07; mid-delivery requests, SOP-12). The fatal moves are the two extremes: silently absorbing it (your margin dies) or getting legalistic (the relationship sours). The middle path is a *fast, friendly, priced* reply — every time, the same way, so clients learn the pattern by week two.

## The flow

1. **Acknowledge warmly, on the spot** (in the meeting or the same day): *"Great idea — I'll scope it and send an estimate by [TOMORROW]."*
2. **Estimate within 1 business day**, using SOP-04 math: hours × floor rate × risk. Nothing exotic — speed matters more than precision here.
3. **Minimum change-order value: $[150–250].** Smaller items get bundled: keep a running "parking lot" list per client and offer it monthly as one line item, or fold it into a retainer tier (SOP-18). Estimating a $40 task costs you $80 of admin — that math is the whole reason for the floor.
4. **Get approval in writing before work starts.** "Reply 'approved' and it joins this sprint" — no approval, no work, no exceptions, no resentment (this is SOP-09 clause 5 doing its job).
5. **Log it.** Every change order gets a row in the project plan (SOP-10) with date, price, and approval link. At project close, the change-order total is your best pricing evidence for the next proposal to this client.

## Copy-paste assets

**The estimate one-pager (email body is enough):**

> Hi [NAME] — estimate for [CHANGE], as discussed:
>
> **What:** [1–2 sentence description of the deliverable, outcome-worded]
> **Not included:** [The adjacent thing they'll assume next — pre-empt it]
> **Price:** $[X] ([HOURS] × $[RATE]) · **Time:** [DAYS], starting on approval · **Effect on current dates:** none / [M2 moves by X days]
>
> Reply "approved" and I'll start [DAY]. If you'd rather park it, it goes on your parking-lot list — no expiry, just honesty.

**The decline-with-alternatives (when it's a bad idea or bad timing):**

> Hi [NAME] — straight answer on [CHANGE]: I'd advise against it right now, because [REASON — e.g., "it adds a second analytics setup mid-campaign and muddies the data we're using to hit your Q3 number"]. If you want it anyway, it's $[X] and [DAYS] — your call. Alternative that gets most of the value for a third of the cost: [SIMPLER OPTION].

**The monthly bundle offer (for small-item accumulators):**

> Hi [NAME] — your parking lot has 6 small items now (list attached). Two options: I bundle them into one change order at $[X] this sprint, or we fold a monthly allowance of [HOURS] hours into your retainer at [PRICE] starting next month — then small ideas just flow without paperwork. Most clients prefer the second; want me to set it up?

## Rules & red flags

- **The pattern is the product.** After two or three identical replies, clients start pre-filtering: "this is probably a change order, right?" That sentence means the SOP is installed and your margin is safe.
- Never estimate a change order mid-meeting. "By tomorrow" is the professional answer; live estimates are wrong and then owned.
- Track your unbilled change-order hours monthly (time you absorbed before the SOP caught it). Aim: → zero within one quarter.
- If the same client generates 5+ change orders per project: their budget structure doesn't match projects — pitch the retainer conversion (SOP-18), which is what the bundle email is really for.

## What good looks like

- Every out-of-scope request has a written estimate within 1 business day, 100% of the time.
- Change-order revenue: 10–20% of project revenue (below 5%: you're absorbing; above 30%: your original scoping is too thin — fix SOP-03 §3).
- Zero surprise invoices. The client's accountant never discovers your invoice before the client does.
