# SOP-24 · Win-Back Sequence

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Offboarding |
| **Owner** | Founder |
| **Trigger** | 90+ days since project end, no retainer, no active conversation — and the account scored 🟢/🟡 when it ended |
| **Time** | 20 minutes per touch × 3 touches, spaced ~6 weeks |
| **Output** | Reactivated clients — or a clean archive after 3 touches, with the relationship intact for SOP-23 referrals |

## When to use

On the alumni list (SOP-23 rhythm) whenever an account goes quiet. Win-back is slower than referral but warmer than cold outreach: these people already know your work. The entire game is *patience + relevance* — three touches over ~18 weeks, each carrying something useful, never "just checking in."

## The sequence (spaced ~6 weeks)

| Touch | Angle | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Value** | Their world, not yours | One insight specific to *their* business/market ("saw [INDUSTRY THING] — this changes [THEIR ASSET] because…"). No offer, no ask beyond "worth a look?" |
| **2 — Proof** | Show what's new | Your newest case study (SOP-22) with a one-line bridge: "similar setup to yours — [NUMBER] in [TIMEFRAME]." |
| **3 — Direct** | The honest door-opener | The direct email below — a specific idea for their business and an easy way in. |

## Copy-paste: the three emails

**Touch 1 — value:**

> Hi [NAME] — no agenda, saw [SOMETHING SPECIFIC: their launch / an industry shift / a competitor move] and thought of you: [2–3 SENTENCE INSIGHT that connects it to their business]. Might be useful, might not — seemed worth sending either way. How's [THEIR WORLD — e.g., the new store] treating you?

**Touch 2 — proof:**

> Hi [NAME] — we just closed [PROJECT] for a [SIMILAR PROFILE] client: [ONE NUMBER RESULT] in [TIMEFRAME]. Wrote it up here ([LINK], 2-minute read) — the middle section on [TOPIC] applies almost word-for-word to your [THEIR ASSET]. Anyway — no ask, just figured you'd find it interesting.

**Touch 3 — direct:**

> Hi [NAME] — last one from me for a while (not a guilt trip, just file-closing honesty): I keep having ideas for [THEIR BUSINESS] — the current one is [SPECIFIC IDEA, one sentence, tied to something you can see]. If it's interesting, reply and I'll shape it into a one-pager with a price range, no commitment. If the timing's wrong, reply "not now" and I'll check back next quarter — and either way, [GENUINE WISH — e.g., "hope the expansion's going great"].

Then act on the reply taxonomy: **"not now"** → archive to the quarterly alumni email (SOP-23), revisit in 2 quarters · **"maybe"** → book a 20-minute call, smallest next step (SOP-18 grammar) · **silence** → archive. Silence *is* an answer, and honoring it is what keeps the door unlocked for next year.

## Stop conditions (hard rules)

- **Explicit no, or a competitor signed** → archive immediately, congratulate them on the competitor signing if you hear (seriously — it's remembered).
- **Two full sequences with zero replies** → this account is an alumni-email-only account now. A third sequence isn't persistence; it's the reason people mute senders.
- **The ending was ugly** (fired mid-project, payment fight) → no sequence. One apology-and-clean-slate email if you owe one (SOP-21 grace rules), then referral-grade distance.

## Rules & red flags

- Every touch must be *about them*: an insight, a proof point, a specific idea. The moment a touch is about your pipeline targets, it reads as such and burns a cycle.
- Personalize from public signals (their site, their LinkedIn, their industry news) — five minutes of homework per touch. A win-back you could have sent to anyone will be archived by everyone.
- Space the touches; three emails in three weeks is pressure, not presence. ~6 weeks apart, ≤ 3 touches, then rest for at least 2 quarters.
- Log everything in the alumni sheet: touch dates, angles used, replies. After two quarters you'll know which angle (value/proof/direct) wakes your kind of clients — lead with that one next cycle.

## What good looks like

- 10–20% of sequenced accounts re-engage within a year (small numbers, huge margin: these close in one call, no discovery-from-zero).
- Every re-engagement starts with their reply, never with your discount. Leading with a discount teaches alumni your prices were padding all along (SOP-04 rule holds here hardest).
- The alumni sheet never has an account sitting in "should probably email them sometime" — it's in a sequence, in the quarterly email pool, or archived. Three states, no limbo.
