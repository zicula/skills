# SOP-06 · Client Welcome Packet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Onboarding |
| **Owner** | Founder / project lead |
| **Trigger** | Contract signed + first invoice paid (or same day, whichever comes first) |
| **Time** | 30 minutes (mostly template fill) |
| **Output** | Welcome email + one-page packet that kills buyer's remorse before it starts |

## When to use

Same day the contract is signed. Between signing and kickoff there's a silence, and silence is where buyer's remorse, anxiety emails, and "quick question — also can you…" live. The welcome packet fills that silence with structure and quietly installs your rules (SOP-09) while goodwill is at its peak.

## The packet (one page, PDF or Notion link)

1. **What happens next** — dated: "Kickoff call within 5 business days (you'll get a booking link today) · Access items due 3 days before kickoff (checklist attached) · First weekly update arrives [DAY] of kickoff week."
2. **Who does what** — your side (named human, not "the team") and their side (named human + who is backup). One point of contact per side, stated twice, in bold.
3. **How we communicate** — the SOP-09 contract in five lines: channels, update day, response times both ways, and the phrase "anything outside scope gets a quick estimate — never a surprise invoice."
4. **Money schedule** — invoice dates, amounts, what each payment unlocks. No client should ever be surprised by an invoice you sent 30 days ago.
5. **Your first homework** — top 3 access items with the due date (full list via SOP-08).

## Copy-paste welcome email

> Subject: Welcome aboard, [NAME] — here's exactly what happens next
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> Contract's signed — we're officially working together, and I'm glad about it.
>
> Everything you need is in the one-pager attached, but the short version:
>
> 1. **Kickoff:** book 60 minutes here → [LINK] (please pick a slot in the next 5 business days)
> 2. **First homework:** 3 access items listed at the bottom of the packet — due [DATE], so kickoff starts warm
> 3. **How we'll talk:** I send a short written update every [DAY] — and you can always reach me at [EMAIL] for anything that can't wait
>
> One promise and one ask. Promise: you will never wonder what's happening on the project — if you do, that's my failure, say so. Ask: single feedback channel through [CONTACT NAME] on your side, so nothing gets decided in a hallway without me.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

## The three moments the packet must answer

Every buyer's-remorse email is one of these three — answer them before they're asked:

| The moment | The unspoken question | The packet's answer |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 after signing | "Did I just make a mistake?" | Dated "what happens next" + the promise line in the welcome email |
| Day 2–4, quiet | "So… what's happening? Should I be doing something?" | First homework with a due date + update day named |
| End of week 1 | "Who do I even talk to about X?" | Named contacts both sides + channels + the emergency line |

## Rules & red flags

- Send the packet **the same day** the contract signs — its power decays by the hour. Automate the trigger if you can.
- Name humans on both sides. "The team will…" is where accountability goes to die.
- Never put passwords in the packet. Access credentials travel only through a password-manager share (SOP-08).
- Keep it to one page. A 12-page "welcome deck" is read by no one and signals process-heavy in the wrong way.

## What good looks like

- Zero "just checking what's happening?" emails in the pre-kickoff week.
- Client completes the top-3 homework before kickoff at a rate above 70%. Below that: your packet is too long or buried in the email.
- New clients use your channels correctly from week one — because the contract was installed before the work started, not negotiated during a crisis.

## Adapt it with AI

> "Write my one-page welcome packet for [SERVICE] projects: [PASTE THE 5 SECTION HEADINGS]. My update day is [DAY], my response SLA is [X hours], typical project length is [X weeks]. Warm, confident, no corporate speak — a busy owner should absorb it in 3 minutes."
