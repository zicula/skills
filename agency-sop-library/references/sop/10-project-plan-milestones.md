# SOP-10 · Project Plan & Milestone Baseline

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Onboarding |
| **Owner** | Project lead |
| **Trigger** | Kickoff done (SOP-07); before any production work |
| **Time** | 45 minutes per project |
| **Output** | A dated milestone table with client dependencies pinned — the baseline every later date conversation refers to |

## When to use

Right after kickoff. A project without a baseline has no such thing as "late" — only feelings. This table is what lets you say "the launch moved because the content arrived on the 14th, not the 3rd" without a single awkward word.

## Building the plan (45 minutes)

1. **Slice the scope into 3–7 milestones** — each ends in something the client can *see* (a reviewed design, a staging link, a launched campaign). Never "work on X, 60%".
2. **Sequence with dependencies named**: "Staging site → *needs* final content (CLIENT)". Client-side dependencies get an owner and a date, same as agency work (SOP-08 rule, continued).
3. **Add the 20% calendar buffer**: a 4-week project is planned to ship in 5. Buffer is *internal* — the client sees the buffered date as the date. Buffer absorbs reality; it is not slack to spend on free extras.
4. **Pin the feedback SLA into the dates**: every review milestone shows "client review due [DATE+2 business days]" — the plan itself teaches SOP-09.
5. **Set the baseline** = the table as sent. Every change is a *re-baseline* with a one-line reason in the update email (SOP-11). History stays visible.

## Copy-paste plan table

> **Project plan — [PROJECT] (baseline [DATE])**
>
> | # | Milestone (client sees) | Done by | We need from you | Owner | Due |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | M1 | [e.g., Design directions — 2 options to choose from] | [DATE] | Brand assets + font licenses | [NAME] | [DATE] |
> | M2 | [e.g., Staging site live] | [DATE] | Final homepage copy | [NAME] | [DATE] |
> | M3 | [e.g., QA passed, launch-ready] | [DATE] | Staging review feedback | [NAME] | [DATE+2d] |
> | M4 | **[LAUNCH]** | [DATE+buffer] | Launch approval | [NAME] | [DATE] |
>
> Dates shift only for three reasons, and you'll hear about each in the weekly update *the day it's clear*: scope added in writing (SOP-14), access/materials late (the table says which), or a genuine unknown we'll name — never silence.

## Re-baselining (when reality moves)

- Client dependency late: new date for the dependent milestone + one line: "Content arrived [DATE]; M2 moves from [A] to [B]. Everything else holds."
- Scope added: SOP-14 estimate approved **before** dates change. No estimate, no new dates — otherwise you're negotiating against yourself.
- Your own overrun: say it at the first signal (SOP-11 at-risk protocol) with a recovery option. Self-reported slippage costs trust once; hidden slippage costs the account.

**Worked micro-example:** a 4-week website rebuild ships as: M1 design directions (week 1, needs brand assets) → M2 staging live (week 2, needs final copy) → M3 QA passed (week 3, needs staging review in 2 days) → M4 launch (week 4 + buffer). Client dates sit in *every* row — that's what makes the plan enforceable without being confrontational.

## Rules & red flags

- Milestones the client can't see (internal sprints, refactors) exist in your tracker, never in the client plan. The client plan is a *contract of visible moments*.
- Don't plan week-by-week past 6 weeks. Far milestones are named, near milestones are dated precisely; re-date the far ones as they approach ("rolling wave").
- Two clients, same week, both "launching"? Your capacity row (SOP-01 §capacity) was wrong — fix the plan now, not the week of.

## What good looks like

- Every date conversation starts by opening the table, not opening a debate.
- Zero "silent" delays: every shift has a line in a weekly update explaining it.
- You can show a prospective client this exact table format in a sales call and watch them relax — that's the sales value of delivery discipline.
