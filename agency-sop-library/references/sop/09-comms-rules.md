# SOP-09 · Communication Rules of Engagement

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Onboarding |
| **Owner** | Founder sets it; every client agrees to it at kickoff (SOP-07) |
| **Trigger** | Welcome packet (SOP-06); re-stated at every kickoff |
| **Time** | 10 minutes to walk through |
| **Output** | The comms contract — fewer interruptions, predictable updates, decisions that don't evaporate |

## When to use

Before the first work session, with every client — installed once at onboarding, then it governs the whole relationship. Without a comms contract, you get 23:47 WhatsApp voice notes, decisions made in a hallway, and "per my last email" resentment. With one, clients *thank* you for being structured. This SOP is the single highest-leverage document in this library for a 1–10 person agency.

## Copy-paste: the contract (client sees this exact text)

> **How we work together — [AGENCY] × [CLIENT]**
>
> 1. **Channels.** Email = anything that is a decision, an approval, or a scope change (it's our shared memory). [SLACK/TEAMS] = quick questions, "is this on track?", heads-ups. Calls = anything that has survived two email rounds, or anything with feelings in it.
> 2. **One contact each.** [NAME] decides for you; [NAME] decides for us. Others are welcome to watch, not to steer.
> 3. **Update day.** Every [DAY], a short written update lands in your inbox (SOP-11) — done, next, needs-from-you, risks. Read it and you never have to ask "how's it going?"
> 4. **Response times.** Us: same business day acknowledgment, real answer within 24h. You: feedback within 2 business days — the project calendar depends on it, and deadlines shift by whatever feedback is late, in writing, before it shifts.
> 5. **New ideas.** "Can we also…" gets this reply: "Great idea — I'll scope it and send an estimate (SOP-14)." Never a surprise invoice, never a silent freebie.
> 6. **Hours.** We reply [9–18 MON–FRI, TIMEZONE]. If it's a true emergency (site down, campaign spending wrong), [EMERGENCY CHANNEL] reaches us anytime.
> 7. **No decisions in meetings we didn't both attend.** If your team discusses the project without us, whatever they decided is a proposal, not a decision.

## Installing it

- Welcome packet (SOP-06) carries the short version; kickoff (SOP-07) walks it live; the kickoff recap quotes it.
- When a client breaks a rule — and they will — reply **inside the rule**, gently: scope-idea → "Great idea — estimate coming Thursday"; hallway decision → "Happy to treat that as a proposal — here's what it changes"; weekend WhatsApp → answer in Monday's update unless it's the emergency line. The contract does the talking; you never scold.

## Rules & red flags (clients who will fight the contract)

- Insists on WhatsApp-only from day one → offer the emergency channel as the compromise; if they refuse, note it as a risk at kickoff and mirror their channel for *non-decisions* only.
- Multiple stakeholders emailing contradictory feedback → "To keep the calendar honest, I need one consolidated reply per round from [NAME] — happy to join a 15-minute internal sync to collect it."
- Client ignores the update email three weeks running → switch format, not frequency: 5 bullets, one question at the end they *must* answer to proceed. Engagement question forces the read.

## What good looks like

- 80%+ of client feedback arrives within the 2-day SLA by project's end (it starts worse; the contract trains it up).
- You work evenings zero times per week for "communication" reasons. Evenings spent on craft are yours to choose; evenings spent on channel chaos mean the contract isn't installed.
- Decisions survive: when a dispute appears, you paste the email thread instead of reconstructing memory.
