# SOP-05 · Proposal Follow-Up Cadence

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Sales |
| **Owner** | Founder / sales lead |
| **Trigger** | Proposal sent (SOP-03) |
| **Time** | 15 minutes per touch × 4 touches |
| **Output** | A yes, a no, or an archived lead — within 14 days, every time |

## When to use

The instant a proposal goes out. Most small agencies lose winnable deals by *not following up* — then feel awkward and stop. The fix is a fixed cadence where each touch adds value or asks a question. "Bumping this to the top of your inbox" is banned; it's the follow-up equivalent of sighing.

## The cadence

| Day | Move | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Send proposal + walkthrough is already booked (SOP-02). Same day: send the link again with a 3-line "what to look at first" note. | Set the reading order |
| **3** | Value nudge: a relevant case study, article, or one insight about their industry — **not** "just checking in". | Stay useful while they circulate it internally |
| **7** | Walkthrough call (already booked) or, if they cancelled and won't rebook, the *question* email below. | Force a direction |
| **14** | Close-the-loop: "Should I close the file?" | Get a real no instead of an infinite maybe |
| **30** | Breakup → archive to nurture list (they re-enter via SOP-24 or the quarterly value email). | Protect your attention |

## Copy-paste assets

**Day 3 — value nudge:**

> Hi [NAME] — no update from me, just something useful: [CASE STUDY / ARTICLE / ONE INSIGHT — one that maps to their exact situation, e.g. "a client in [ADJACENT INDUSTRY] saw [NUMBER] after fixing exactly the [ISSUE] we discussed"].
>
> Might be handy context while the team goes through the proposal. Option B's [SECTION] covers this on page [X].

**Day 7 — the question email (if no walkthrough happened):**

> Hi [NAME] — I know these decisions involve other people. One question to make sure I'm building the right thing, even if the answer changes shape: which of the three options is closest to what you're imagining?
>
> If none of them are, tell me what's missing and I'll rework it — that's a normal part of the process, not a hassle.

**Day 14 — close the loop:**

> Hi [NAME] — I don't want to nag, so let me make this easy: if it's a no (timing, budget, or you went another way), just reply "no" and I'll close the file gracefully — genuinely no hard feelings, and I'll send over [ONE MORE FREEBIE — e.g. "that checklist"] anyway.
>
> If it's a yes, reply "Option [B]" and I'll have the contract to you within the day. If I don't hear back by [DATE +5], I'll assume timing isn't right and check back next quarter.

**Day 30 — breakup (archive after this):**

> Hi [NAME] — closing the loop as promised. I'll stop emailing about the proposal; the pricing in it holds until [DATE] if things change sooner.
>
> I'll keep sending the occasional [NICHE] insight your way — if [PROBLEM THEY NAMED] comes back onto the agenda, you know where I am.

## Rules & red flags

- **Four touches maximum.** Each must contain new value or a question. Anything less is noise, and noise burns the referral bridge (SOP-23 starts here, win or lose).
- A *real no* is a win: you got information in 14 days instead of hoping for 6 months. Thank them and log the reason — "no" reasons are your pricing/positioning data.
- Ghosted after a booked walkthrough no-show: one reschedule offer, then straight to Day 14 email. Don't chase humans who booked and vanished; the score (SOP-01) was probably wrong somewhere.
- Track sent → won, sent → lost, sent → ghosted per month. If ghosted > 50%, your proposals are fine and your qualification isn't — back to SOP-01.

## What good looks like

- Every open proposal has a next dated touch in the calendar. Nothing sits "waiting to hear back" — that phrase is banned in your pipeline tool.
- Median days-to-decision under 21.
- At least a third of "no"s name the reason, because your Day 14 email made it safe to be honest.
