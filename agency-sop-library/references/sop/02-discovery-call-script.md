# SOP-02 · Discovery Call Script

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Sales |
| **Owner** | Founder / sales lead |
| **Trigger** | Qualified lead (70+) books a discovery call (SOP-01) |
| **Time** | 30 minutes live + 10 minutes prep |
| **Output** | Decision: send proposal (SOP-03) / nurture / decline — and notes written in the client's own words |

## When to use

On every discovery call. The script's job is not to sell — it's to find out whether their problem, money, and timeline are real, and to collect the exact words they use to describe the problem so the proposal mirrors them.

## Prep (10 minutes before the call)

- Re-read their inquiry and your SOP-01 score. Write your **walk-away floor** on paper: below $[FLOOR] you decline on the call.
- Open a notes doc with these headings already typed: *Current state · Success metric · Decision path · Budget · Timeline · Their words*.
- Camera on, agenda stated in the invite: "30 minutes: your situation, what success looks like, and whether we're a fit."

## Copy-paste: the script (30 minutes, timeboxed)

**Minutes 0–2 · Frame it.**
"Thanks for making time. Plan for our 30 minutes: I'll ask about where you are and what success looks like, you'll ask about how I work, and by the end we'll each know if this is worth pursuing — a no is a fine outcome for both of us." (Permission for honesty lowers everyone's guard.)

**Minutes 2–10 · Current state.**
- "Walk me through how [PROCESS/WEBSITE/CAMPAIGN] works today."
- "What made you look for help **now** — what happened?" (The *now* is the real budget qualifier.)
- "What have you already tried?"

**Minutes 10–18 · Success metric.**
- "Six months from now, if this worked, what's different? Give me the number or the moment."
- "What happens if you do nothing for six months?" (Cost of inaction = your value ceiling in SOP-04.)
- Push once: "Is that a nice-to-have or a board-level must?"

**Minutes 18–24 · Money and decision path.**
- "Projects like this typically land between $[LOW] and $[HIGH] depending on scope. Does that range work with your budget?" (Anchoring here filters tire-kickers before you write a proposal.)
- "Who else weighs in before a decision like this is final, and what does that process look like?"
- "If we started [MONTH], when would you need to see results by?"

**Minutes 24–30 · Close the loop.**
- "Here's what I heard: [2–3 sentences in THEIR words]. Did I get that right?"
- "I'll send a proposal by [DAY +2 business days]. It'll have three options with different scopes — [NAME] is the one I'd recommend, but you'll see the trade-offs."
- "One question so the proposal lands right: [LEFTOVER UNKNOWN from SOP-01 — usually budget or authority]."
- Book the follow-up **on the call**: "Can we hold 15 minutes [DAY +4] to walk through it together?" Never end without that slot.

**Bail-out line** (any time it's a no): "I don't think I'm the right partner for this, and I'd rather tell you now than in a proposal — [REASON]. [REFERRAL] would serve you better."

## Rules & red flags during the call

- Won't discuss budget even after you anchor a range → likely 40–69 lead; send no proposal without budget confirmed in writing.
- "Our last agency just didn't get us" with no specifics → probe: "What would 'getting you' have looked like, concretely?" If they can't answer, they can't be kept happy either.
- Third party silently present who won't speak → name it: "I see we have [NAME] with us — love to hear your take at some point."
- Asks you to start "small and free, then we'll scale" → decline politely; free pilots anchor your price at zero.

## What good looks like

- Notes doc filled **during** the call, with at least 3 verbatim client phrases — those phrases become the proposal's opening.
- Every call ends with a scheduled proposal-walkthrough slot or a decided no.
- Proposal-sent → proposal-accepted rate above 50%. Below that: you're proposing to unqualified leads — tighten SOP-01, not this script.
