# SOP-18 · Upsell Triggers & Pitch Grammar

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Retention |
| **Owner** | Anyone who spots the trigger; founder prices it |
| **Trigger** | One of the six trigger events below happens on an active account |
| **Time** | 15 minutes to log, one email + one call to pitch |
| **Output** | Expansion offers that feel like service — because they arrive as answers to the client's own events |

## When to use

Upselling isn't a quota activity; it's a *listening* activity. This SOP is a standing table: when the trigger fires on the left, the pitch on the right is already written. Agencies that "don't like selling" don't need to — they need to notice and connect.

## The trigger table

| # | Trigger (event) | The pitch (connect it to their goal, always) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **KPI beats target 2 consecutive months** (SOP-16 shows it) | Scale the engine: *"Leads beat target 2 months running — this is when scaling spend compounds instead of gambles. Want the 30% scale plan?"* |
| 2 | **Client mentions a new initiative** in any call (launch, new market, new hire, funding) | Map your services to *their* news: *"That launch is exactly what [SERVICE] is for — want me to sketch what it'd look like for it?"* |
| 3 | **Support/requests cluster outside scope** (SOP-14 parking lot ≥ 4 items, or change orders repeating) | Retainer conversion: *"These keep coming as one-offs — a monthly [HOURS]-hour retainer makes them flow without estimates. Here's what it'd include."* |
| 4 | **Contract end within 60 days** (calendar is a trigger) | Renewal + expansion together: *"Before renewal on [DATE]: what worked, what's next — I'd propose [BIGGER SHAPE] for [PRICE]." (delivered via SOP-17)* |
| 5 | **Your champion changes jobs / new stakeholder appears** | Re-anchor immediately: new stakeholder inherits your QBR deck, a 30-minute intro, and the results story. An un-anchored new boss is a churn event on a timer. |
| 6 | **They reference a competitor's move** ("[RIVAL] just launched X") | Counter-move framing: *"Want the 2-week version of that for us? Here's what it'd take."* |

## Pitch grammar (every pitch, all three moves)

1. **Their goal, not your service.** *"Since you want [GOAL]…"* — never *"We also offer…"*
2. **Smallest next step.** A sketch, a 2-week pilot, an estimate — not a $40k proposal. Reduce the yes to something that costs them a reply.
3. **Range before detail.** *"Typically lands $[X]–$[Y] — want the sketch?"* Interest confirmed before you invest hours in scope.

## Copy-paste: the trigger email (trigger #1 shown; pattern is universal)

> Subject: Your leads beat target again — worth scaling?
>
> Hi [NAME] — second month in a row above target (report attached). Two months of data changes this from "nice" to "signal": the campaign scales predictably right now.
>
> The smallest next step if you're curious: I'll sketch a 30%-higher-spend plan with projected [METRIC] and a stop-loss — one page, no commitment. Typically this kind of plan lands in the $[X]–$[Y]/month range.
>
> Want the sketch by [DAY]?

## Rules & red flags

- **One open pitch per account at a time.** Pitch-stacking reads as quota behavior and cheapens the next legitimate one.
- Deliver bad news in the same season as pitches. An agency that only calls when selling trains clients to dread your calls; the honest ❌ row in SOP-16 buys the right to trigger #1.
- Trigger #5 is a fire alarm, not a drip campaign: new decision-maker who doesn't know you is how 18-month accounts die in month 19. Book the intro within 2 weeks of their arrival.
- Log every trigger + pitch + outcome in the account sheet. After a quarter you'll see which triggers actually convert — work those harder and let the dead ones rest.

## What good looks like

- Expansion revenue at 20–30% of total revenue — the cheapest revenue in the business, won without a single cold call.
- Clients hear pitches as *"they noticed"* rather than *"they're selling"* — that's the grammar working.
- Every retainer account has exactly zero triggers sitting unactioned for more than a week.
