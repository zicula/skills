# SOP-22 · Testimonial & Case Study Request

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Offboarding |
| **Owner** | Founder |
| **Trigger** | Within 24 hours of a compliment, a 9–10 pulse (SOP-20), or a results milestone the client celebrated |
| **Time** | 10 minutes to ask · 20 minutes to shape · 2 hours to build the case study |
| **Output** | Specific, quotable, number-carrying testimonials — and one case study per great project |

## When to use

The rule that does all the work: **ask within 24 hours of the compliment — never later.** "That's amazing, thank you!!" in your inbox is a perishable asset. Most agencies say "thanks" and move on; the disciplined one converts gratitude into proof within a day.

## The ask (make it effortless — effort kills testimonials)

Never "could you write us a testimonial?" (blank page = never happens). Give structure:

> Hi [NAME] — that just made my week, thank you.
>
> Since you're happy (and only because you are): would you put a few words in writing? Three prompts, answer any or all, rough is perfect:
>
> 1. What was going on before we started? (the "before")
> 2. What did we do that stood out — working style counts as much as the work?
> 3. What's the result you'd point to? Numbers welcome, honesty over precision.
>
> Two lines per answer is plenty. If it's easier, reply in bullets and I'll shape a draft for your approval — you edit anything, nothing goes public without your sign-off.
>
> And to be clear: a "not now" costs nothing. [NAME]

The **draft-for-edit** route converts best: busy clients approve-and-tweak; they don't compose.

## Shaping what they send back

- **Keep their words.** Light edits for length and typos only — polished marketing-speak reads fake and they'll notice their voice missing.
- **Anchor specifics:** "great to work with" is worthless; "they caught that our checkout was losing mobile sales — recovered about $4k/month" is a weapon. If their draft is vague, one follow-up: *"Could you add the number that made you happiest? Even rough."*
- **Collect permissions explicitly:** full name, title, company, photo, and where it may appear (website, proposals, social). Log the permission date next to the quote.
- **Quote bank:** one file/table — quote, who, permission, where used. Every proposal (SOP-03 §6) pulls from it; every listing too.

## From testimonial to case study (within 30 days, best projects only)

Structure (one page): **Before** (their words from the discovery notes, SOP-02) → **What we did** (3–5 moves, outcome-worded) → **The number** (their metric from SOP-07, before/after) → **Their quote** (the shaped testimonial) → **What it took** (timeline + price range if they permit — price ranges pre-qualify your future leads, SOP-01 bonus).

## Copy-paste: the 30-day follow-up (if they went quiet)

> Hi [NAME] — no rush and no pressure on the words from last month; your inbox is a war zone, I get it. If it's easier, just reply with the one number you'd brag about from [PROJECT] and one sentence why — I'll draft around it as before. If it's a no, we're completely good, and [RESULT] was a blast either way.

## Rules & red flags

- One nudge only, ~30 days later. A second chase converts goodwill into a favor they resent — the exact opposite of the asset's purpose.
- Never write a fake testimonial or ghost-write one they don't edit. It's dishonest, it leaks, and clients talk.
- Fresh testimonials beat old ones: rotate the two quotes in proposals (SOP-03 §6) quarterly; an agency whose newest proof is 3 years old is telling on itself.
- The testimonial conversation is also the natural door to SOP-23 (referrals): the thank-you email after they approve the quote adds the "one small favor" — the referral ask.

## What good looks like

- 60%+ of happy clients provide a usable quote within a week (the 3-prompt + draft-for-edit format gets you there; the blank "any chance you could…" request doesn't).
- Every proposal carries a quote from the last 2 quarters, with a number in it.
- One case study per quarter enters the quote bank — your listing pages (Gumroad/Etsy/website) and SOP-01 nurture emails feed on these forever.
