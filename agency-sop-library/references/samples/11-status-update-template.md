# SOP-11 · Weekly Project Status Update

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Delivery |
| **Owner** | Project lead — same person, same weekday, every week |
| **Trigger** | Every [DAY] from kickoff week to launch, even when "nothing happened" |
| **Time** | 10–15 minutes per week per project |
| **Output** | A 5-minute client read that kills the "how's it going?" email forever |

## When to use

Every week, every active project, same weekday — even in weeks where the only honest content is "we were blocked; here's what we did about it." **Consistency beats content.** The update's real product is the client's *never having to wonder*.

## The template (email or client's shared doc — pick one and never switch)

> **[PROJECT] — Weekly update #14 — [DATE]**
>
> **Status: 🟢 On track** (🟡 at risk / 🔴 blocked — never bury a yellow)
> *One line on why: on track for [MILESTONE] on [DATE].*
>
> **Done this week**
> - [Outcome-worded, 3–5 bullets: "Checkout flow redesigned and sent for your review" — not "worked on checkout"]
> - [ … ]
>
> **Next week**
> - [2–3 bullets with the milestone each rolls up to]
>
> **Need from you**
> - [MAX 2 items, each with a name and a date: "Homepage copy approval — [NAME] — by [DATE] (M2 dependency)"]
>
> **Risks & changes**
> - [Anything 🟡/🔴 goes here at the FIRST signal, with what we're doing about it]

## The at-risk protocol (the part that saves accounts)

Raise it **at the first signal, not at the deadline**, with options — never apologies alone:

> **Status: 🟡 at risk** — font licenses are still pending (asked [DATE], chased [DATE]). M1 design sign-off may slip from [A] to [B].
> Two options: (a) proceed with a licensed lookalike now, swap later — no date impact, or (b) hold for originals and we re-baseline M1 together. Your call by [DATE] and I'll lock either path.

A yellow raised early with options reads as competence. A red discovered at deadline reads as negligence. Same facts, opposite outcome — the difference is only when you spoke.

## Copy-paste: the "quiet week" update

> **Status: 🟢 On track** — heads-up that this week looks quiet from outside: [REASON — e.g., "we're in the content-production block for M3"].
> Done: [1–2 real things]. Next: [THING] lands [DATE]. Need from you: [ITEM or the word "nothing this week"].

Even "nothing" gets sent. The one week you skip is the week their boss asks.

## The update that saved an account (filled example)

> **[PROJECT] — Weekly update #9 — Tue 14 Oct**
>
> **Status: 🟡 at risk** — analytics verification found the checkout event was double-counting since launch; real conversion is ~12% lower than reported.
> *Why it's yellow, not red: fix ships Thursday; we'll re-issue last month's numbers corrected (SOP-16 will reconcile the same way your dashboard does).*
>
> **Done:** double-count traced to the tag firing on both page and event · fix staged on production mirror · 90-day corrected trend built
> **Next week:** corrected dashboard live · funnel review call (booked Friday)
> **Need from you:** none this week.
>
> One bad-looking yellow, honestly raised with the fix already staged, beats a green that collapses at audit time. Clients renew agencies that catch their own errors.

## Rules & red flags

- Max 2 "need from you" items. If you need five things, the plan (SOP-10) is wrong or the client is over-flexible — re-baseline instead of demanding five dates at once.
- No new scope, pricing, or grievances in the update — those get their own thread (SOP-12/14/15). The update stays the place the client *wants* to open.
- Send it even if the client stopped reading; the archive is your timeline for SOP-15 escalations and year-end reviews (SOP-17).
- For retainers, this template becomes the monthly report skeleton (SOP-16) — same muscles, longer horizon.

## What good looks like

- "How's it going?" emails: zero by week 3.
- Client replies reference the update ("saw the yellow — let's do option a") — that's the format working as a decision channel.
- When someone new joins the client side, they read updates #1–N and are fully caught up in ten minutes, no meeting.
