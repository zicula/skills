This proposal sets out a monthly consulting retainer: what you can expect from me each month, how we will work, and what it costs. The goal is simple — senior-level firepower on your problems without the overhead of a full-time hire or a big-firm engagement.

1. The situation

You do not need another report; you need decisions made and shipped. The recurring work on your desk — prioritization, vendor and tooling choices, the occasional fire — needs an experienced operator who already knows your context. A retainer buys exactly that: me, on call, inside your business, month after month.

2. Scope of the retainer

Every month, the retainer includes

  • Two strategy working sessions (90 minutes each) — structured working meetings with written decisions and owners, not slideware.
  • On-call advisory — direct access by email/Slack/voice notes on business days, answers within one business day, urgent items same day.
  • One deep-dive deliverable — a focused piece of work you choose monthly: a build-vs-buy analysis, a pricing review, a process redesign, a vendor negotiation brief, or equivalent.
  • Monthly written review — what moved, what stalled, and the three highest-leverage moves for next month.

Out of scope (available as separate projects)

  • Implementation and hands-on build work beyond scoping and specs.
  • Full-time on-site presence, or being exclusive to your company.
  • Regulated professional services (legal, accounting, tax filings).

3. How we work

Work happens in your tools, visible to your team from day one — no parallel universe of documents nobody reads. Sessions are booked at fixed recurring times so they actually happen. Requests land in a shared queue; anything urgent jumps the queue with a short written note so trade-offs stay visible.

4. Term and cadence

The retainer starts on acceptance and renews monthly. Minimum initial term: 3 months — the first month is setup and context-loading; the value compounds from month two. Either side may end the retainer with 30 days' written notice after the minimum term.
