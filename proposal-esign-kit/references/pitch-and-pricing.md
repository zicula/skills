# PITCH & PRICING — how to position the kit you just bought

You bought this to send proposals, but the quiet upside is bigger: a clean accept flow with a real evidence trail changes how *you* price. This file is the short strategy memo.

## The market you are actually in

Solo service sellers (coaches, consultants, boutique agencies) are squeezed between two worlds:

- **Big e-sign platforms** price per seat and per envelope, wrap your proposal in *their* branding, and want a monthly relationship for what is, honestly, an occasional document.
- **Free tools** (PDF + "please sign and scan") produce zero evidence, zero momentum, and make you look like a side project.

2026 pricing reality check (from public pricing pages, verified September 2026): entry tiers cluster at **US$10–19/month** with hard caps — e.g. 110 documents per *year*, or 10 sends per *month*, with overage fees above. At 15–30 proposals a month, a solo seller can trip a cap designed for a casual user while paying for one seat.

Your alternative: **own the whole loop.** Your domain, your brand on the page, no caps, no seat count, no per-document meter running. The kit cost less than one month of one e-sign seat; everything after break-even is margin.

## What to charge for the work that flows through it

The kit doesn't set your fees; it removes the last excuse to undercharge:

- **Anchoring.** A branded proposal on your own domain, with a 90-day plan, a milestone table, and a real acceptance flow reads like an established practice — because it behaves like one. Sellers who move from "email thread" to "structured proposal with acceptance" typically stop competing on price almost immediately.
- **Deposits change the client mix.** A 30–50% deposit filter repels the clients who were never going to be easy money and attracts the ones who decide like businesses. Your close *rate* may dip a point; your close *quality* and cash flow will not.
- **The retainer template exists on purpose.** One-off projects are a treadmill; the monthly retainer template is there so that when a client asks "can we just keep you?", the upgrade is a two-minute document, not a negotiation from scratch.

## Where the kit pays for itself — the honest arithmetic

Take a solo consultant sending 6 proposals a month. A capped entry e-sign plan: ~US$10–19/month, forever, plus the risk of overage fees in a busy quarter. A mid-tier plan with the features a signed deal actually needs: US$19–25/month. The kit is a one-time ฿1,490 (≈US$42) — **two to four months** of the cheapest subscription, then zero. Add one saved deal per quarter from faster follow-up (FOLLOW-UP-SOP.md) and the comparison stops being funny.

There is no hosted-service number to compare against beyond that, because there is no hosted service to pay for. Your pages live on free static hosting. That is the whole business case.

## The line you must never cross (also yours to keep)

Never present the acceptance record as a certified or statutory signature instrument. It is a strong, honest record of intent and consent — say exactly that. The templates' fineprint already does this; keep it when you customize. The honesty *is* the differentiator: the seller who explains evidence precisely is the one buyers trust with deposits.
