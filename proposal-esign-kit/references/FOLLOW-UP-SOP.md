# FOLLOW-UP SOP — the 4-touch sequence that gets proposals signed

Most proposals don't die from rejection; they die from silence. The client meant to get to it. This SOP is a calm, repeatable sequence that surfaces the real objection without ever sounding desperate. Total writing time per deal: ~8 minutes.

**Rules first**

- Every touch has one job and one ask. No "just checking in" — that phrase converts silence into permanent silence.
- Every touch offers a graceful exit. Paradoxically, exits get replies.
- Never resend the link "in case they lost it" before touch 3; asking permission first is what separates a professional from a notification system.
- All times are business days. If you work weekends, your clients' silence still follows weekday clocks.

---

## Touch 0 — the send (day 0)

**Channel:** email. **Subject:** the outcome, not the document — `"90-day plan to get Acme's ops off your plate"` beats `"Proposal"`.

Body: 4 sentences. (1) Here is the proposal for [outcome]. (2) It is a 4-minute read on any phone. (3) You can accept right on that page — type your name, tick consent, done; the system records your acceptance and gives you a receipt to keep. (4) "If the timing is wrong or the scope is off, tell me which — I'd rather hear it now."

That last sentence is not decoration. It is the exit ramp that makes every later touch safe.

Then: log the send date. Nothing else today. Touching it again today is the classic amateur tell.

## Touch 1 — the value add (day 2)

**Channel:** email, reply in the same thread. **Ask:** none.

Send one short thing that makes the project more real: the two risks you'd watch in their situation, a 60-second voice note on the first week's plan, a before/after sketch. End with zero questions. The psychological effect: your proposal thread is now the most useful thing in their inbox, and silence starts to feel expensive.

## Touch 2 — the deadline reminder (day 5, or {{valid_days}} − 3)

**Channel:** email. **Ask:** a decision or a date.

> "Quick heads-up — I'm holding the [price/start date] from the proposal for you until [date]. After that my calendar opens to other projects. If you're still interested, reply with a yes or a 'not now, revisit in [month]' — both are fine, silence helps nobody."

This works because it is true: the templates' validity term ({{valid_days}} days) is real, you really do release the slot, and you really do accept "not now". Scarcity you invented reads as scarcity you invented. Scarcity that is operationally true reads as respect for everyone's time.

## Touch 3 — the break-up (day 9, or {{valid_days}})

**Channel:** email, then *optional* phone/DM for warm relationships. **Ask:** permission to close the file.

> "I'll assume the proposal isn't a priority this quarter and I'll close the file tomorrow — no hard feelings, and I mean it. If it should stay open, tell me what's in the way: scope, budget, or timing? I can usually fix two of the three."

Roughly half of your "gone quiet" deals answer this one. The ones that don't were never deals; you just got that verdict in writing instead of in your head, five weeks earlier than usual.

## The objections, decoded

- **"Can you send it again?"** — Yes, but never as a naked re-send. Resend the link with one new sentence of context: "Same proposal — I've re-attached it so you don't have to dig. Section 4 is the one most people have questions about." (Generate a *fresh* link only if the terms changed; same terms, same link, same token — cleaner records.)
- **"We need to run it by legal."** — Good sign, not a stall. Reply: "The terms sections are 5 and 6; the acceptance page produces a signed evidence receipt you can file. If your legal team prefers wet ink, they can print and sign — the terms are identical either way." You just became the vendor who made their lawyer's day shorter.
- **"Too expensive."** — Ask "compared to what?" once, gently. Then offer scope surgery, not discounts: "I can hit the core outcome with phase 1 only at [smaller number] and we schedule the rest when it's earning."
- **Total silence** — touch 3 exists for exactly this. Do not write touch 4. A closed file you can reopen; a pest you cannot.

## After the yes

1. Verify + file the audit-trail JSON the moment it lands (SETUP.md step 4) — while the goodwill is fresh, so is the file.
2. Same-day: thank-you note + deposit link (DEPOSITS.md) + kickoff date. Momentum converts; invoices don't.
3. Log in your tracker: sent / touch-1 / touch-2 / signed (or closed). After ten deals you will know your real touch-3 conversion — most solo sellers discover it is their single highest-leverage hour of the week.
