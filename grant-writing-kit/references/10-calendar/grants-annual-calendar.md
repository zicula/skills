# The Grants Annual Calendar — running the cycle at 2-4 hours a week

A grants calendar turns "we should apply for more grants" into weeks that have a
shape. This page has two calendars: the **annual rhythm** (what each month is for,
US-nonprofit-typical) and the **weekly rhythm** (the three blocks that carry the
system). Below both: the blank to build your own from the tracker.

## The weekly rhythm (the calendar that matters most)

```
 MONDAY — 10 min: the radar
   Open 1-pipeline Deadlines sheet. The week's writing blocks get calendared
   NOW, not Wednesday. Red rows (overdue) get their phone call today.
 WEDNESDAY — 60-120 min: the writing block
   One application, one section, protected time. The LOI library and skeletons
   make the block assembly, not authorship — that is why 90 minutes is enough.
 FRIDAY — 10 min: the truth pass
   Every tracker row's status is real; the Log has the week's events; next
   week's Next-action dates exist.
```

Three blocks. Thirty working days at that rhythm produces: twelve writing blocks,
four radar escalations handled before they were emergencies, and a Log that writes
your year-end review for you. The rhythm fails in a predictable way — the Friday
pass gets skipped first, the tracker drifts from reality within a month, and the
radar stops being believed. Guard the Friday ten minutes.

## The annual rhythm (US-typical; your tracker's real dates overrule it)

| Month | The grants work (2-4 h/week) |
|---|---|
| **January** | Year-plan: refresh boilerplate numbers (one-pager, metrics, history); list the year's target funders; calendar known cycles from last year's Log |
| **February** | Spring-cycle LOIs: many community foundations and family funds close March-May — research their guidelines now, score, commit |
| **March** | Second research season (with October, the SOP's scheduled run): refresh the prospect list; renewals from last spring's awards get their proposals drafted |
| **April** | Reports for calendar-year funders come due — write them as renewals (skeleton 6); spring LOIs in writing blocks |
| **May** | Spring submissions land; mid-grant stewardship emails (Email 2) for live grants; board sees the what-changed memo before the fiscal-year close |
| **June** | Fiscal-year close for June-30 orgs: awarded-vs-target review in the tracker; book the summer's proposal work |
| **July** | The light month, deliberately: stewardship visits and Email 3 invitations; calendar refresh for fall cycles; the report-calendar blocks for any summer awards |
| **August** | Fall preparation: guidelines pulled for fall-deadline funders; scorecards run; the big proposals (state, workplace) start their writing blocks |
| **September** | Fall writing season begins in earnest — the heaviest writing month; radar discipline matters most (the worked example's red row lives in September) |
| **October** | Second research season; next year's prospect list started; renewals from fall awards get Email 5 previews |
| **November** | Year-end submissions; the year's reports drafted while the data is fresh; boilerplate numbers pre-updated for January |
| **December** | Stewardship month: year-end thank-yous with one number each (Email 1's muscle, reused); the annual report's grant section drafted from the Log; close the year in the Log |

Government-funder note: federal and many state cycles float with appropriations
and program years — where the annual rhythm says "typically," their published
dates say "actually." The tracker's Deadline column is the authority; this table
is the tide table behind it.

## The annual review (45 minutes, first week of January)

1. **The Log tells the year:** awards, passes, renewals, the funders who went
   quiet. Read it top to bottom — it is your strategy memo for next year.
2. **Win/lose arithmetic:** proposals submitted vs. awarded, by funder type.
   Two cycles of data and you know which funders reward your effort — spend
   next year's hours there.
3. **The scorecard's report card:** did GO verdicts win more than NO-GO would
   have? Re-weighting (go-no-go-guide.md) happens now, logged, if the answer
   says so.
4. **Boilerplate refresh list:** every number that changed (metrics, budget,
   history milestones) — January's week-one work, before the year's first LOI
   cites a stale figure.
