# 00 — START HERE

**Grant Writing Kit — the grants operating system for a small organization.**

This kit gives you one repeatable cycle for every funder, every year:

```
 research ──► score ──► prepare ──► write ──► submit ──► track ──► report ──► renew
 (per funder)  (30 min)  (one-time,  (1-3 h    (per the    (weekly    (per award)  (stewardship)
                          then       per LOI,  checklist)  30 min)
                          maintained) 1-3 days
                                      per proposal)
```

Eight links. The same links every time. Small organizations that "do grants" do not
lose because they cannot write — they lose because each application is improvised:
deadlines live in inboxes, the go/no-go decision is a mood, the org description gets
rewritten from scratch every time, and the funder who gave last year hears nothing
until the renewal is due. This kit replaces the improvisation with files you fill in.

## The two ways to use this kit

**Track D — Deadline** ("a proposal is due in three weeks and I am the only one on it")
→ Score it today (`2-go-no-go/`, 30 minutes). If the verdict says commit, work the
checklist and the skeleton this week. `SETUP.md` walks you through it file by file.

**Track Y — Year** ("we need grants as a line of our budget, not as emergencies")
→ Build the boilerplate library first (`4-boilerplate/`, one afternoon, once), set
up the tracker with 10-20 real prospects (`3-funder-research/` + `1-pipeline/`,
one morning), then run every opportunity through the same cycle. By the second
quarter the system compounds: reports become renewals, renewals become relationships,
and boilerplate absorbs most of every new application.

## Your first hour (Track D and Track Y both start here)

1. Open `README.md` (three minutes — folder map and honest scope).
2. Open `1-pipeline/grant-pipeline-tracker.xlsx` and look at the **Deadlines** sheet
   (five minutes). The numbers you see are the whole point of the kit: what's due
   this week, what's overdue, what's in play.
3. Score the opportunity on your desk in `2-go-no-go/go-no-go-scorecard.xlsx`
   (25 minutes). The guide next to it explains every criterion, and the sheet ships
   with a worked example to copy the rhythm from.
4. Read the verdict cell. Either you commit the hours — and the boilerplate templates
   are tonight's job — or you pass gracefully (the guide has the email) and keep the
   week for the grants that fit.

## The month, if you commit

```
 Day 1-2   boilerplate: mission one-pager + impact metrics      (2-3 h, once)
 Day 3     tracker: 10-20 prospects via funder-research SOP    (2-3 h)
 Day 4     score everything; pass or commit — log the calls    (1 h)
 Day 5-15  write what you committed: LOI first, budget alongside (2-4 h per LOI)
 Day 16    submit early; log it; thank-you email same week      (1 h)
 Weekly    Monday radar check + Friday tracker update           (30 min x 2)
 Later     report on time, steward all year, renew deliberately (per award)
```

The order does not change with the deadline; only the pace does. Score before you
write — a 25-minute scorecard has saved whole weeks — and report after you win,
because the report is where the next grant starts.

## The honest scope

This kit is process, templates and method. It does not promise awards — outcomes
depend on your program and your funders, and a disciplined pass is a win for your
calendar. It contains no legal or compliance advice: government grants carry rules
of their own, and this kit's checklist tells you to copy those rules verbatim and
ask the program officer early — it does not interpret them for you. The funder
research SOP uses public sources; it ships no funder database, because lists age
badly. And the worked example threaded through every file — the Riverbend Literacy
Council — is fictional: its numbers exist so you can see each tool working before
you trust it with yours.
