# Proposal Skeleton 3 — Capital / Facilities

**Use when:** building, renovation, major equipment, or a campaign gift request.
**Typical length:** 5-8 pages plus a project budget and campaign chart. **Time:**
2-3 days, most of it on numbers and commitments — capital funders check both
against your other documents.

```
 SECTION                              WHAT IT MUST PROVE
 ──────────────────────────────────   ──────────────────────────────────────────
 1. Executive summary                 the project, the total, the campaign state
 2. The organization                  you can build AND operate
 3. The need for the project          the physical problem, evidenced
 4. The project                       scope, cost, timeline, professionals named
 5. Campaign status                   money in hand — the credibility section
 6. Operating after completion        the plan that answers "who runs it in 2030"
 7. Project budget + sources          uses = sources, contingencies visible
```

## 1. Executive summary (½ page)

> [Organization] is raising [$1.2M] to [project in one sentence]. The problem it
> solves: [the physical constraint with one number]. To date: [$W] committed —
> [top sources]. We request [$50,000] from [Funder] toward [phase/component].
> Completion: [date]; the completed facility's operating plan is enclosed.

## 2. The organization (¾ page)

Compressed org-history plus the two capital-specific proofs: **stewardship** (how
you maintained what you already own/lease — maintenance budget line, the state of
current facilities) and **management** (who administered past projects, or the
project manager this one employs). Quality check: one sentence on why the
*organization* — not just the project — deserves capital trust: years, results,
clean financial reviews.

## 3. The need for the project (¾-1 page)

The physical problem in evidence: code violations, capacity caps, cost of the
status quo (lease dollars, transport costs, program hours lost), accessibility
failures. Numbers beat narrative — inspection dates, waitlists, square footage,
cost per year of doing nothing. One paragraph ties the facility constraint to the
mission: the people this keeps unserved.

## 4. The project (1-1.5 pages)

- **Scope:** what gets built/renovated/bought — measurable (square feet, seats,
  systems replaced).
- **Professional team:** architect/contractor named with their relevant projects;
  if not yet selected, the selection process and its date.
- **Timeline:** design, permitting, construction, occupancy — with the honest
  version of each date. Permitting delays are normal; a plan without contingency
  time reads inexperienced.
- **Community role:** who shaped the design (users, neighbors, board), and how
  that shows in the plans.

## 5. Campaign status (¾ page) — where capital proposals are won

A sources-and-uses table (§7) plus a short narrative:

| Source | Amount | Status |
|---|---|---|
| State [program] grant | $600,000 | awarded, contract signed |
| Two family foundations | $150,000 | committed, letters enclosed |
| Board campaign | $40,000 | full board participation |
| Individuals | $85,000 | raised to date |
| **This request** | **$50,000** | — |
| To be raised | $275,000 | campaign plan enclosed |

Rules: lead with what is committed, not what is hoped; **unanimous board giving** is the cheapest credibility you can buy; the "to be raised" number is
stated plainly with the plan that reaches it. A campaign chart without commitments
in the first third reads as a wish.

## 6. Operating after completion (¾ page) — the question that kills proposals

The completed facility changes your budget; show that you know by how much:

> Completion adds [$38,000/year]: utilities, maintenance contract, one custodial
> role. Against it: [$61,000/year] in lease and transport costs eliminated, and
> [revenue the space enables — program fees, rentals, expanded enrollment worth
> $X]. Net operating impact: positive [$Y]/year from year one (operating pro
> forma enclosed). The maintenance reserve is budgeted at [amount]/year, per the
> [facility plan / building condition assessment] enclosed.

## 7. Project budget and sources

Separate from your operating budget: construction/professional/equipment/
contingency lines (10% contingency minimum — state the basis), and the sources
table from §5. Every number here should trace to a professional estimate or
contract, named in the basis column, same discipline as the budget workbook.

**Section prompt (AI):** "Here is my campaign sources-and-uses table — [paste] —
and my draft campaign-status section: [paste]. Check the section against the
table: every commitment named must appear in the table with the same amount and
status, and the to-be-raised arithmetic must tie. List mismatches; do not fix
them silently."

## Capital-specific final pass

- [ ] Every commitment in §5 has a letter or agreement in the attachments
- [ ] Timeline includes permitting/contingency time, stated
- [ ] §6's operating numbers tie to the pro forma attached
- [ ] The ask amount appears in summary, §5 table, and cover letter — identically
- [ ] Site visit offered with a specific view (the need visible from the sidewalk)
