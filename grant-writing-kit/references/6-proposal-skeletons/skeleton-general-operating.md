# Proposal Skeleton 1 — General Operating Support

**Use when:** an invited full proposal for unrestricted/general-operating funding.
**Typical length:** 3-6 pages plus forms. **Time:** a focused day, mostly assembly —
GOS proposals are your boilerplate, organized. **Worked example org:** the fictional
Riverbend Literacy Council ($310K budget, 240 learners/year).

```
 PAGE BUDGET (4-page version)                    SOURCE FILE (mostly pre-written)
 ─────────────────────────────                   ────────────────────────────────
 1. Executive summary        ½ page   ◄──────── mission one-pager + ask
 2. Organization & capacity  ¾ page   ◄──────── org-history + staff-bios
 3. Community need           ¾ page   ◄──────── needs-statement
 4. Programs & results       1 page   ◄──────── program-profiles + impact-metrics
 5. Use of funds             ¾ page   ◄──────── budget workbook + this skeleton §5
 6. Accountability           ¼ page   ◄──────── impact-metrics + report skeleton
    Attachments: per checklist (usually budget, board list, 990/financials)
```

## 1. Executive summary (½ page)

Purpose: the whole proposal for the skimming reader — org, need, results, ask.

> [Organization] strengthens [community] by [one-clause mission] for [population].
> The need: [one sourced statistic]. Last year we [two results with numbers].
> We request [$15,000] in general operating support — [X]% of our budget — to
> [what it holds: the staff line / the pipeline / the seats]. [One sentence on
> reporting: "We report annually against our published metrics."]

Quality check: a board member of the funder could repeat your ask after one read.

**Section prompt (AI):** "Compress these inputs — [paste one-pager, needs stats,
results, ask] — into a 150-word executive summary in this order: org, need,
results, ask, reporting. Mark any fact you add that was not in my inputs as
[FACT NEEDED]."

## 2. Organization and capacity (¾ page)

Purpose: prove the organization is managed and will exist to finish the grant.
Pull, do not rewrite: two founding/milestone sentences from org-history; the
budget trend and funding mix; one staff credential sentence per named person;
board table reference.

Quality checks: numbers with years (no "rapidly growing" without the figures);
the funding mix named honestly; governance in one sentence (size, meeting rhythm).

## 3. Community need (¾ page)

Purpose: the problem, sized locally. Paste your needs-statement's three moves —
problem, gap, bridge — trimmed to fit. The GOS version tilts toward the *organiza-
tional* gap: what part of the need your whole operation answers (referrals taken,
hours covered, populations served that no other provider reaches).

Quality check: every statistic has (Source, Year) in the sentence; your own
service data (waitlist, turned-away) appears in the gap paragraph.

## 4. Programs and results (1 page)

Purpose: what you run and what it produces. One short block per program from the
program profiles — one line of model, one line of numbers — then the impact
metrics table (or its top four rows). End on the outcome ladder: outputs, then
outcomes, then the life results your follow-up survey supports.

Quality check: each program's block is ≤ 5 lines; every outcome names its
instrument; percentages carry denominators.

## 5. Use of funds (¾ page) — the GOS-specific section

Purpose: make "unrestricted" legible. GOS funders know money is fungible; they
want to know what their dollar *holds in place*.

> A grant of [$15,000] equals [X]% of our [$310,400] budget and would hold in
> place: the part-time instructor line ($4,230 — 6 h/week × 30 weeks), intake
> and assessment ($933 across learners served), and facility costs at our two
> library sites. Revenue covering the remaining [X]%: [list top three sources
> with amounts]. Diversification this year: [one true sentence — new contract,
> new individual-giving tier].

Quality check: the math ties to your budget workbook (percent, line items); no
line item is invented for the proposal.

## 6. Accountability (¼ page)

Purpose: how the funder learns what happened. Name the report you produce (the
skeleton in `8-reporting/`), the metrics you will report (from the table above),
and the audit/review your financials receive. One sentence of invitation: site
visits, learner events, board meetings.

## Assembly checklist

- [ ] Every section's facts came from boilerplate files, not from memory
- [ ] The ask appears in summary, use-of-funds, and budget — same number, three places
- [ ] Percentages recomputed this year (stale percentages are the classic GOS error)
- [ ] Attachments per the funder checklist — checked off, named per their file rules
- [ ] Read aloud once; every place you stumble, a reviewer stumbles too

**Whole-proposal prompt (AI):** "Assemble sections 2-6 from these pasted
boilerplate files — [paste]. Keep every fact as written; cut nothing that carries
a number. Flag repetitions. Mark any sentence where you supplied a new fact as
[FACT NEEDED]." Then verify the flags to zero.
