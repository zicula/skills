# Proposal Skeleton 4 — Capacity Building

**Use when:** the funder strengthens organizations — systems, technology, planning,
staff development, evaluation capacity. **Typical length:** 4-6 pages. **Time:**
1-2 days. The persuasion here is internal: you are asking funders to invest in the
machine, not the meal, so the proposal's evidence is organizational, not programmatic.

```
 SECTION                            THE JOB IT DOES
 ─────────────────────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────────
 1. Executive summary               growth + the bottleneck + the fix + the after
 2. Organization snapshot           the growth that created the bottleneck
 3. The capacity gap                named, evidenced, with its current cost
 4. The proposed solution           what, who builds/delivers it, by when
 5. How we will know it worked      before/after measures — the whole ballgame
 6. Budget + narrative              the fix, itemized, with its basis
```

## 1. Executive summary (⅓ page)

> [Organization] has grown [from X to Y in n years]. That growth has outrun
> [named system]: [one sentence of the daily cost]. We request [$10,000] to
> [the fix in one sentence], delivered by [who]. Success, measured: [the before →
> after pair]. This is [our second infrastructure investment since year — the
> first: what it enabled].

## 2. Organization snapshot (½ page)

The growth story in numbers: participants, budget, staff, programs — three years
of each (org-history file). One paragraph on what the growth *strained*: not a
complaint, a diagnosis. Capacity funders fund orgs that are growing into their
next size, not orgs describing generic needs. Quality check: every growth number
matches org-history exactly.

## 3. The capacity gap (¾ page) — argue the cost

Name one bottleneck and quantify what it costs you, in three currencies:

- **Time:** "outcome reporting takes two staff-days per funder question"
- **Risk:** "records live in four spreadsheets; a laptop loss is unrecoverable"
- **Obligation:** "our new state contract requires quarterly reports we cannot
  yet produce on schedule" (the strongest of the three — tie the fix to a
  commitment you already carry)

Include the diagnosis trail: how you know this is the bottleneck (an audit, a
time-tracking week, a near-miss, board discussion minutes). Funders fund
diagnosed problems; undiagnosed ones read as "we want money for software."

## 4. The proposed solution (¾-1 page)

- **What:** the specific fix — system implemented, plan written, training
  delivered, process redesigned. Not "upgrade our technology" but "implement a
  case-management system with [n] licenses, migrating [which records], retraining
  [which staff]."
- **Who:** the consultant/vendor selection — named if chosen (with the reason and
  the quote), otherwise the selection process with its date and its criteria.
- **Staff time:** whose hours the project consumes and where those hours come
  from (capacity projects fail from unbudgeted staff time; naming it reads as
  experience).
- **Timeline:** phases with dates, and the go-live date the measures hang on.

## 5. How we will know it worked (¾ page) — do not dilute

Pick 2-4 before/after measures you will actually take:

| Measure | Before (measured, dated) | After (target, measured how) |
|---|---|---|
| Time to produce a funder outcome report | 2 staff-days (timed 6/2026) | ≤ 2 hours (timed at first quarterly report) |
| Records in one system | 4 spreadsheets, no backup discipline | all records migrated; weekly backup verified |
| Reporting deadlines met | 1 late report in FY26 | 4 of 4 on schedule |

The before numbers must be *measured now*, before the grant — a baseline gathered
after award is the classic capacity-project failure, and funders know it. Include
the honest failure clause: what you will do if the after-target misses (extend
training, revisit configuration), because capacity projects bend.

## 6. Budget and narrative

Itemize the fix: licenses/fees, consultant line (with quote basis), staff training
time (real hours × real rates), materials. The narrative's key paragraph is the
**sustainability line**: what the fix costs to keep after the grant (renewals,
maintenance hours) and where that already sits in your operating budget. A
capacity investment without a named maintenance plan is, to this funder type, a
toy you will abandon.

**Section prompt (AI):** "Here is my capacity-gap draft: [paste]. Play the
skeptical reviewer: is the bottleneck one specific thing, is its cost quantified
in at least one of time/risk/obligation, and does the diagnosis say how we know?
List weaknesses as questions; do not rewrite."

## Final pass

- [ ] The bottleneck is one thing, named — not "we need to modernize"
- [ ] Baselines measured and dated before submission
- [ ] Consultant/vendor selection process has dates and criteria
- [ ] Staff time to run the project is budgeted somewhere true
- [ ] Maintenance-after-grant has a line in the operating budget narrative
