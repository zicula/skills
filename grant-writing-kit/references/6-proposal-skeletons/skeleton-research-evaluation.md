# Proposal Skeleton 5 — Research / Evaluation

**Use when:** research fundings, evaluation projects, community-based participatory
research, or the evaluation component of a larger grant. **Typical length:** varies
widely with the funder's RFP — follow their structure; this skeleton maps the
sections research reviewers score. **Time:** 2-4 days; method clarity is the work.

```
 SECTION                        WHAT REVIEWERS CHECK
 ─────────────────────────────  ─────────────────────────────────────────────
 1. Abstract / summary          can a non-specialist state the question?
 2. The question + literature   is the gap real and worth this money?
 3. Design + methods            could a competent peer replicate this?
 4. Community partnership       roles, benefits, data ownership — agreed in writing
 5. Protection of participants  consent, privacy, the required review process
 6. Timeline + management       phases, deliverables, who is accountable
 7. Dissemination               who learns the results, in what order
 8. Budget                      every line ties to a method step
```

## 1. Abstract (150 words, one paragraph)

Question in plain language, method in one sentence, sample and setting in one,
partnership in one, the answer's use in one. If a board member cannot repeat the
question after reading, the abstract is not done.

## 2. The question and why it is open (½-¾ page)

State the research question as a question. Then the gap: what is known (briefly,
with 3-6 citations), what is unknown, and why *this* setting can answer it
(access, partnership, prior data). End with the practical stake: who changes what
when the answer arrives.

## 3. Design and methods (1-2 pages) — the section that decides funding

- **Design named:** mixed-methods, quasi-experimental, case study, participatory
  evaluation — with one sentence on why that design fits the question.
- **Sample:** who, how many, recruited how, with the attrition assumption stated.
- **Instruments:** named (the assessment, the survey, the interview protocol) with
  their evidence basis; new instruments flagged as piloted first.
- **Analysis:** the approach in one honest sentence per data type — descriptive
  and pre/post comparisons are respectable; do not decorate.
- **Limitations, stated before reviewers find them:** what this design cannot
  show, and why the answer is still worth having. Reviewers trust proposals that
  name their own limits.

**Section prompt (AI):** "Review this methods draft as a skeptical reviewer:
[paste]. List every place where a step is unstated, an instrument unnamed, or a
claim stronger than the design supports. Do not rewrite; list."

## 4. Community partnership (½ page)

Who contributes what: the community partner's role in design, recruitment,
interpretation, and dissemination — with the memorandum-of-understanding status
(draft enclosed / signed). Include what the community gets: findings first, in
plain language, compensation for participant time, a named advisory input route.
Data ownership stated: who holds the data, who may use it for what, after the
project.

## 5. Protection of participants (¼-½ page)

Consent process in plain language; privacy protections (what is collected, what is
de-identified, where it lives); compensation stated. **Name your review process
and follow its real requirements and timeline** — for academic partners this is
the institutional human-subjects review; build its weeks into the timeline. This
kit does not interpret review requirements; it schedules them and points at them.

## 6. Timeline and management (¼ page + table)

Phases with months: preparation/review, instrument finalization, enrollment,
data collection windows, analysis, community reporting, dissemination. A
management sentence: who runs the day-to-day (named, with bios referenced), and
the decision route when something slips.

| Phase | Months | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Review + instruments | 1-3 | approvals in hand; protocol final |
| Enrollment + baseline | 4-6 | [n] consented; baseline complete |
| Data collection | 7-14 | [what, on what rhythm] |
| Analysis + community report | 15-18 | plain-language report; public session |

## 7. Dissemination (¼ page) — the community, then the field

Order matters in community-engaged work: findings return to the community first
(public session, plain-language report, in the languages of the participants),
then the field (paper, conference, practitioner brief). Name the venues honestly —
the plausible one, not the famous one.

## 8. Budget (form + narrative)

Every method step has a line: participant compensation (recruitment math visible:
[n] × [$]), instruments and licenses, transcription/translation hours, analyst
time, community-report costs (venue, languages, childcare for the session).
Evaluation consultants priced per the quote; the narrative walks the budget in
study-phase order.

## Final pass

- [ ] The question is a question, answerable by this design
- [ ] A competent peer could replicate from your methods alone
- [ ] Partnership MOU status stated; data ownership written
- [ ] Review-process weeks in the timeline
- [ ] Budget lines trace to method steps, both directions
