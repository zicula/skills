# Proposal Skeleton 2 — Program / Project

**Use when:** the standard invited proposal for a specific program or project.
**Typical length:** 6-10 pages plus forms. **Time:** 2-3 focused days. This is the
skeleton most funders' guidelines map onto; where their required section names
differ, use their names in their order.

```
 SECTION                                   WORD BUDGET      SOURCE
 ───────────────────────────────────────   ──────────────   ─────────────────────
 1. Executive summary                      150-200 words    last (write it last)
 2. Statement of need                      400-600          needs-statement
 3. Program design                         700-1,000        program-profile
 4. Evaluation plan                        250-400          impact-metrics
 5. Organizational capacity                250-400          org-history + bios
 6. Sustainability / future funding        150-250          this skeleton §6
 7. Budget + narrative                     forms + 400-600  7-budget/
```

## 1. Executive summary (write it last)

> The [Funder] invests in [their priority language]. [Organization] requests
> [$25,000] over [period] to [the delta: expand / launch / strengthen Program X
> from A to B]. The need: [one sourced statistic + your waitlist number]. The
> program: [one sentence of model]. The evidence: [one outcome with instrument].
> We will report [two metrics] on your schedule.

## 2. Statement of need (400-600 words)

The three moves from your needs-statement template: problem sized locally → the
gap (who is unserved, with your own evidence) → the bridge to your approach.
Tailoring rule for this skeleton: re-read the funder's priority sentence and make
sure the *gap paragraph* lands inside their population and geography, not adjacent
to it.

Quality checks: 2-4 statistics, all with (Source, Year) in-sentence; the gap is
specific and current (waiting list, referrals declined, hours unserved); one
sentence connecting the need to the funder's stated interest.

**Section prompt (AI):** "Draft the statement of need from my pasted research
notes and these statistics — [paste]. Three moves: local problem, unserved gap,
bridge to our approach. Every statistic must carry its source and year exactly as
I gave them; mark [FACT NEEDED] if you state any number I did not provide. 500
words maximum."

## 3. Program design (700-1,000 words) — the heart

Expand the program profile's steps into prose a stranger could operate from:

- **Model and schedule:** who, what, how often, where, over what period — with the
  calendar visible ("two 90-minute sessions weekly, twelve-week cycles, three
  cohorts a year").
- **Participants:** eligibility, recruitment, intake, and the retention plan (what
  keeps people coming — buses, hours, childcare, texts).
- **Staffing:** named roles with the bios' credential lines, plus volunteers with
  training and supervision described.
- **Partners:** what each contributes, and the status of the commitment (letter
  enclosed / MOU signed / handshake — say which, honestly).
- **The delta this grant buys:** from X seats to Y seats, at $Z per participant —
  the arithmetic in one visible sentence.

Quality checks: a reader could describe the program back to you after one pass;
every claim of capacity ties to a named person or partner; the delta arithmetic
appears here AND in the budget.

**Section prompt (AI):** "Expand my pasted program-profile steps into a 900-word
program design with these subsections: model and schedule, participants and
retention, staffing, partners, the delta this grant buys. Use only pasted facts;
mark [FACT NEEDED] on anything new."

## 4. Evaluation plan (250-400 words)

Four sentences of structure: **what you will measure** (the 2-4 metrics from the
impact table that this grant touches, named with definitions), **with what
instrument** (assessment, log, survey — named), **on what rhythm** (mid-grant and
end-of-grant dates in the calendar), **reported how** (their template, their dates,
and your offer of a site visit). If the funder names their own indicators, use
theirs and map yours beneath.

Quality check: the plan names one measure you could report badly — and says what
you would do then (report honestly, with the revision explained). Funders trust
plans that survive bad quarters.

## 5. Organizational capacity (250-400 words)

Compressed org-history (founding, years, steadiness), the team who will run *this*
program with credential lines, board governance sentence, and the partner
commitments' status. Two quality checks: every named role appears in the budget's
personnel lines; history numbers match the org-history file exactly.

## 6. Sustainability (150-250 words) — answer the real question

The funder asks: what happens to this program when our grant ends? The strong
small-org answer is a funding map, not a promise:

> The expansion's [$25,000] covers [period]. At term: [name the actual plan —
> "the state contract renewal (decision [date]) covers the instructor line;
> individual giving, which grew [X]% last year, is budgeted to absorb materials;
> the remaining [$Y] appears in our FY27 draft budget, which accompanies this
> proposal." ] If [the risk] occurs, [the honest fallback: the program retrenches
> to the pre-grant model, whose funding is secure.]

## 7. Budget and narrative

Build in `7-budget/grant-budget.xlsx` — every line with its basis, the indirect
row following the funder's rule copied verbatim into the Checklist sheet — then
write the narrative per `budget-narrative-template.md`: one paragraph per category
that a non-accountant can follow, each number traceable to a basis cell.

## Final pass (order matters)

1. Numbers audit: every number in prose appears in the budget or boilerplate —
   grep your own draft for digits and chase each one.
2. Name audit: funder name, program name, contact names — correct everywhere.
3. Prompt 2 (AI tailoring): paste the guidelines; verify the AI's claimed
   alignments one by one.
4. Read aloud; fix every stumble.
