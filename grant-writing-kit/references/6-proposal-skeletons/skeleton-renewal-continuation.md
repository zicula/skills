# Proposal Skeleton 6 — Renewal / Continuation

**Use when:** re-applying to a funder who supported the current or a previous
grant — the highest-value proposal in the kit, because renewing costs a fraction
of winning new. **Typical length:** often shorter than the original (2-5 pages);
some funders use a renewal form instead. **Time:** 1 day, plus the report being
current (that is the real prerequisite).

```
 THE RENEWAL ARGUMENT, IN ONE LINE:
  "Here is what your money did — exactly as promised.
   Here is what it set up. Here is the next increment, sized like the last."
```

```
 SECTION                          WHAT IT MUST DO
 ───────────────────────────────  ─────────────────────────────────────────────
 1. Results against your grant    mirror their metrics — same words, real numbers
 2. What changed because of it    one participant story + one system change
 3. Stewardship recap             how you treated them as a partner (proof list)
 4. The next increment            the delta, priced like the last grant
 5. Budget + sustainability       the line items + where the rest comes from
```

## 0. Before writing: the renewal readiness check (30 minutes)

- [ ] The final report for their current grant is submitted — or its deadline is
      calendared and the draft is real. **An unwritten report is an unwinnable
      renewal; write the report first.**
- [ ] Their grant's own metrics are extractable from your tracker/metrics files —
      you know what you promised.
- [ ] The stewardship record is real (see §3's proof list): thank-yous sent,
      visits hosted or offered, their name used as they permit.
- [ ] The next increment is sized near the previous award (a renewal ask 3x the
      original is a new proposal wearing a renewal's clothes; if the need is
      genuinely 3x, treat it as a new application and say so).

## 1. Results against their grant (¾-1 page) — the section that wins it

Structure as a mirror: their metrics' words, your actuals.

> Your 2025 grant of [$15,000] committed to three measures: [their words —
> "expanded access to evening classes"], [metric two], [metric three]. Results:
> [actual one — number + instrument + year]; [actual two]; [actual three —
> including the one that missed, if one did: "placement of east-side learners
> reached 71% against a 75% target; the gap drove this proposal's transportation
> line."]

Rules: report in their vocabulary, not yours; targets and actuals sit side by
side; a missed target with an explanation and a fix reads as management — hiding
it reads as risk. The numbers must match the grant report you filed (funders
compare; a mismatch is the fastest way to lose the renewal).

## 2. What changed because of it (½ page)

One participant story (with consent, named or anonymized by their preference) in
four sentences — the human reading of §1's numbers. One system sentence: what the
grant let you build that persists (the trained tutor cadre, the assessment cycle,
the employer partnership). Quality check: the story contains no number that is not
also in §1's table — it illustrates, it does not introduce.

## 3. Stewardship recap (¼ page) — proof, not gratitude

A short list of what stewardship actually looked like:

> During the grant period: thank-you sent within 48 hours of award (copy
> enclosed); your program officer visited the east-side class in March; our
> spring report included the photos you requested; [funder] is named in our
> annual report and on the site plaque per your recognition guidelines; our ED
> spoke at your grantee panel in May.

This section is why stewardship is in the kit at all: the recap is written from
the Log and the emails you already sent (`8-reporting/stewardship-emails.md`).
If the list is thin, the fix is not prose — it is the next eight months of
actually doing these things.

## 4. The next increment (½ page)

The delta, priced like the last grant:

> Building on that base, we request [$16,500] to [the specific next step — "hold
> the second east-side section for a full year and add transportation support,
> which the 71% figure shows is the binding constraint"]. Concretely: [what it
> buys — 12 more seats/cycle, 2 vans monthly, coordinator hours]. Same measures,
> one year further: [the targets — 75% → 80% placement, 240 → 270 learners].

## 5. Budget and sustainability (¼ page + form)

Line items with bases from the budget workbook; the sustainability paragraph
shows the funding map beyond this renewal (who else renews, what grows, what
happens if this one lapses — the honest fallback). Funders renew orgs whose
funding picture they can see, not orgs that depend on them silently.

**Section prompt (AI):** "Here is the funder's metric language from our current
grant — [paste] — and my results section: [paste]. Reorder the results to mirror
their metrics' words one for one, keeping every number exactly as given. Mark
[FACT NEEDED] anywhere a metric lacks a stated actual."

## Final pass

- [ ] Every number in §1 matches the filed report exactly
- [ ] Their metrics' vocabulary, your actuals, side by side
- [ ] The story illustrates; it introduces nothing
- [ ] Stewardship list is evidence (dates, copies), not adjectives
- [ ] Ask sized near the last award, or explicitly re-framed as new
