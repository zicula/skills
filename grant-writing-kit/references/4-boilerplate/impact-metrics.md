# Impact Metrics — the numbers, defined once

**What it is:** the small set of measures your organization reports everywhere —
LOIs, proposals, reports, the annual letter — each with a definition, a source, and
a collection rhythm. Funders differ in what they ask, but every question is a
variation on the 6-10 metrics you define here.

**Time to build:** 90 minutes (most of it deciding definitions). **Update cadence:**
collect on the rhythm below; review definitions each January.

---

## The template (6-10 metrics, each row filled completely)

| # | Metric | Definition (exact) | Source / tool | Rhythm | 2025 | 2026 target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Learners served | Unduplicated adults completing intake in the calendar year | Intake log | Annual count, monthly tally | 240 | 270 |
| 2 | Reading-level gain | % of goal-setting learners advancing ≥1 level, pre/post | Standardized placement assessment | Per 12-wk cycle | 64% | 65% |
| 3 | Learner retention | % of matched pairs active at 6 months | Tutor-hours log | Quarterly | 89% | 90% |
| 4 | Credential/employment results | Learners reporting a credential, raise or job tied to program goals | 90-day follow-up survey | Semi-annual | 19 | 25 |
| 5 | Volunteer leverage | Trained active tutors; tutor-hours delivered | Training records + hours log | Quarterly | 38 tutors / 4,100 h | 42 / 4,400 h |
| 6 | Cost per learner | Program cost ÷ learners served | Budget ÷ metric 1 | Annual | $1,293 | $1,240 |

## Definition rules (where the fights are won)

1. **A definition is exact enough when two staff would count the same thing the
   same way.** "Learners served" = *unduplicated adults completing intake* — not
   "people who called," not "enrolled." Unduplicated vs. duplicated is the single
   most common funder confusion; state which you use.
2. **Every metric names its source.** The assessment, the log, the survey, the
   spreadsheet. "Source: coordinator's judgment" is not a source.
3. **Percentages carry their denominator in the definition.** "64% advanced a
   level" — of *goal-setting learners who completed the post-assessment* (62 of
   97), not of everyone who ever attended. Write the denominator down.
4. **Targets are set from your own trend, not from what sounds good.** 64% → 65%
   is a target you can defend; 64% → 90% is a number a program officer will ask
   you to explain.

## The outcome ladder (know which rung each metric sits on)

```
 OUTPUT ────────► OUTCOME ────────► RESULT
 things you        change in          change in
 count             participants        their lives
 served, classes,   reading level,      credential, job,
 hours, tutors      confidence, pass     income, stability
 easiest to count ────────────── hardest to count (and most persuasive)
```

A balanced set touches all three rungs. Funders notice when every metric is an
output (activity without evidence of change) or when everything claims life results
without a collection method. Metrics 2-4 in the example show the ladder done
plainly: assessment-based change, retention, then a follow-up survey for the top rung.

## Collecting: keep it boring

- **One spreadsheet or form per metric**, owned by one named person, updated on the
  rhythm — that is the whole system. Monthly tallies in a shared sheet beat an
  annual scramble.
- **The 90-day follow-up survey** (metric 4's source) is five questions, sent by
  text or email, with two attempts and a phone fallback; expect 30-50% response and
  report the response rate alongside the number.
- **When a number looks wrong, investigate before reporting.** A sudden jump is a
  definition problem or a double-count until proven otherwise. Funders forgive
  revisions announced openly; they remember corrections they discovered.

## Where it gets used

The mission one-pager quotes three; program profiles report per program; the
proposal skeletons' Evaluation section is largely this table tailored to the
funder's language; the report skeleton reports actuals against these targets; the
what-changed memo attributes results to funders. Define once, report everywhere —
and when a funder demands a metric you do not collect, say so honestly and offer
the nearest one you have, with its definition.
