# Mission One-Pager — the asset every application opens with

**What it is:** one page that states who you are, what you do, for whom, and with
what results. Nearly every LOI and proposal contains a compressed version of it;
writing it once means every future application starts at 80% instead of zero.

**Time to build:** 60-90 minutes the first time. **Update cadence:** every January
and whenever a program, leader or headline number changes.

---

## The template

```
[ORGANIZATION NAME]
[One-line mission — the sentence you would say at a bus stop.]

WHO WE SERVE
In [place/region], [population] face [the problem in one sentence].
We serve [specific population — number and description], reaching [number]
people in [year].

WHAT WE DO
[Program 1 name]: [what it is] — [how many served per year].
[Program 2 name]: [what it is] — [how many served per year].
[Program 3 name]: [what it is] — [how many served per year].

WHAT DIFFERENCE IT MAKES
In [year]: [result 1 with number]. [Result 2 with number]. [Result 3 with
number]. (Numbers come from the impact-metrics template.)

WHAT WE NEED
Our annual budget is [$X]. [$Y]% comes from [sources]. This year's gap is
[$Z], which is why [the ask you are making].

CONTACT
[Name, title] · [email] · [phone] · [website] · [EIN if you wish]
```

## The worked example (fictional — Riverbend Literacy Council)

> **Riverbend Literacy Council**
> *Every adult in Riverbend County can read well enough to live the life they want.*
>
> **WHO WE SERVE** — In Riverbend County, an estimated 1 in 8 adults reads below
> the level a job application requires. We serve adults 18+ reading below a ninth-
> grade level — parents, workers, new English speakers — reaching 240 learners in 2025.
>
> **WHAT WE DO** — One-to-one tutoring (150 learners matched with 38 trained
> volunteer tutors). Two evening ESL classes (70 learners). Workplace reading
> program with three employer partners (20 workers).
>
> **WHAT DIFFERENCE IT MAKES** — In 2025: 64% of goal-setting learners advanced
> one full reading level; 19 learners got a credential or a raise they attribute
> to the program; 89% of matched pairs stayed together six months or longer.
>
> **WHAT WE NEED** — Our 2026 budget is $310,400; 41% individual giving, 27%
> foundations, 18% events, 14% government contracts. The gap is $52,000, which is
> why we are seeking support for our evening-class expansion.
>

## The quality bar

1. **One page.** If it spills, you are writing two documents — cut until it is one.
2. **Numbers, not adjectives.** "Many adults struggle" says nothing; "1 in 8 adults
   reads below the level a job application requires" says everything. Every claim
   of scale carries a number and a year.
3. **One sentence of problem, one of response.** The one-pager states; the proposal
   argues. Do not let it become an essay.
4. **The gap sentence is honest.** Name where your money comes from and what is
   missing — funders read the funding mix as maturity, not weakness.
5. **It reads aloud in 90 seconds.** Read it to someone outside your field; every
   place they frown is a place a program officer will too.

## Where it gets used

The LOI templates compress it into two paragraphs; proposal skeletons expand its
sections into the Organization Capacity section; the stewardship emails quote one
number from it; the annual report borrows its structure. When your January update
changes a number, change it here first — then check the tracker for applications in
flight that cite the old one.
