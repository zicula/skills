# Staff and Board Bios — the people, in 80 words each

**What it is:** short, uniform biographies for everyone an application names —
staff, board officers, key volunteers or contractors. Funders read them for one
thing: *are the right people in the right seats?* Uniform bios answer fast.

**Time to build:** 15 minutes per person (interview each person for 10 of them).
**Update cadence:** on any personnel change; review every January.

---

## The template (80 words, this order)

```
[NAME], [role] — [years with the org / years in the field].
[Credential or lived experience that fits the role — degree, license,
certification, or the experience that substitutes.] [One specific
responsibility or result: what they own, or one number they produced.]
[One human line that connects them to the mission — said plainly.]
```

**Rules:**

- **Same length, same order, every bio.** Role, credential, owned result, human
  line. Uniformity is what makes the team read like a team.
- **The owned result carries a number when one exists.** "Runs the tutor-training
  program (38 tutors certified since 2022)" teaches more than "experienced trainer."
- **Lived experience is a credential.** A former learner now tutoring is a stronger
  bio line than a generic degree — say it plainly and respectfully.
- **No adjective salads.** "Passionate, dedicated, dynamic" are cut words. The
  result and the human line do that work.

## The quality bar

1. **Every named person owns something with a number or a date in it.**
2. **The board table shows profession + mission tie, not just names** — funders
   check governance composition, and the tie column pre-answers "why these people."
3. **Consent and accuracy.** Each person reads and approves their own bio before it
   ships. Titles and credentials are checkable facts; write only checkable ones.
4. **Contractors get real bios too** (the evaluator in your budget, for instance) —
   an application that names its evaluator with a credential reads managed.

## Where it gets used

Proposal skeletons paste these into Organization Capacity; LOIs name the ED in one
line; the report skeleton names program staff in the delivery section; the
stewardship emails introduce new staff by quoting their human line.
