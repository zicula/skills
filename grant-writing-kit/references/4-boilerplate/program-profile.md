# Program Profile — one page per program, reusable

**What it is:** a one-page factual description of each program, written once and
tailored per funder. Proposals and LOIs both describe programs; writing the
description from a profile stops the "each application invents the program again"
problem.

**Time to build:** 40 minutes per program. **Update cadence:** every January with
the year's numbers; immediately when the program model changes.

---

## The template

```
PROGRAM: [name]                    YEAR LAUNCHED: [year]
ONE LINE: [what it is, in one sentence a stranger understands.]

WHO IT SERVES
[Population, eligibility, how they enroll/find you.] Capacity: [number
served per cycle/year]. Waitlist: [number, if any.]

HOW IT WORKS (the model, 4-6 steps)
1. [Step — e.g., intake and goal-setting, 45 min, week 1.]
2. [Step — e.g., 12-week cycle, two 90-min sessions/week.]
3. [Step — e.g., mid-cycle progress check, week 6.]
4. [Step — e.g., cycle-end assessment and next-step plan.]

WHAT IT COSTS TO RUN (feeds 7-budget)
Annual cost: [$X]. Per participant: [$Y] = [$X ÷ number served].
Staff time: [roles + hours]. Other inputs: [space, volunteers, materials].

WHAT IT ACHIEVES (numbers from impact-metrics.md)
Output: [number served, sessions held]. Outcome: [% or number achieving
the program's goal, with year]. Evidence: [assessment/tool used].

WHAT IT NEEDS NEXT (the growth edge, 1-2 sentences)
[The specific constraint or expansion this program faces — the seed of
your next ask.]
```

## The worked example (fictional — evening ESL classes)

> **PROGRAM:** Evening ESL Classes · **YEAR LAUNCHED:** 2019 (second site 2023)
> **ONE LINE:** Twice-weekly evening English classes for working adults at library
> sites, in twelve-week cycles.
>
> **WHO IT SERVES** — Adults 18+ who speak a language other than English at home,
> placed by a short interview into level 1-3. Enrolment by referral (library,
> employers, word of mouth) and open intake twice a year. Capacity: 72 seats per
> cycle across two sites. Current waitlist: 31.
>
> **HOW IT WORKS** — 1. Placement interview and goal-setting (week 0). 2. Twelve-
> week cycle, two 90-minute sessions per week, class size 12. 3. Progress check
> with individual plan at week 6. 4. Post-cycle assessment and next-step referral
> (next level, tutoring, or workforce program).
>
> **WHAT IT COSTS TO RUN** — Annual cost: $19,800. Per participant: $275 =
> $19,800 ÷ 72. Staff: part-time instructor ($23.50/h), coordinator oversight
> (2 h/week). Inputs: free library classrooms (documented partnership), 3
> volunteer conversation aides.
>
> **WHAT IT ACHIEVES** — Output 2025: 70 learners enrolled, 1,680 learner-hours
> delivered. Outcome: 58% of completers advanced one level on the placement
> assessment; 21 completers entered the tutoring program or workplace program.
> Evidence: standardized placement assessment, pre/post.
>
> **WHAT IT NEEDS NEXT** — The east-side site holds the waitlist; one additional
> evening section (6 hours of instruction per week) would seat 12 more learners
> per cycle.

## The quality bar

1. **The model is steps, not vibes.** A funder should be able to describe your
   program back to you after reading the four to six steps.
2. **Per-participant cost is computed, not guessed** — annual cost divided by
   participants served, and the division shown. It is the number program officers
   quote in their approval memos.
3. **Outcome names its evidence.** Which assessment, which tool, which year. An
   outcome without a method is a hope.
4. **The growth edge is one specific constraint.** "One more section would seat 12
   more learners" is an ask a funder can act on; "we hope to grow" is not.

## Where it gets used

Proposal skeletons 2 and 5 build their Program Design section directly from the
steps; the LOI library compresses the profile to one line + one outcome; the budget
workbook's line items trace to "What it costs to run"; the report skeleton reports
against the same output and outcome numbers. Six profiles at most for most orgs —
write the ones you would name in a grant.
