# Needs Statement — the problem, evidenced and local

**What it is:** the case that the problem you address is real, sized, and happening
here. It is the section funders weight most and applicants write worst — usually by
leaning on adjectives ("desperate," "critical," "underserved") where the funder
wants numbers with sources.

**Time to build:** 90 minutes the first time, 20 minutes per funder to tailor.
**Update cadence:** whenever your source data refreshes (many government series
update annually; note each source's year).

---

## The template (three moves, in order)

```
MOVE 1 — THE PROBLEM, SIZED LOCALLY
In [place], [population] experience [problem]. [Statistic with source and
year.] [Second statistic with source and year.]

MOVE 2 — THE GAP
Existing responses: [what exists]. What remains: [the specific gap —
who is unserved, what is unavailable, what fails]. [Evidence of the gap:
waiting list, turned-away count, funder/agency report.]

MOVE 3 — THE BRIDGE TO YOU
[Organization] addresses this gap by [your approach in one sentence].
[One sentence on why your approach fits the evidence or your results.]
```

**Sizing rules:**

- **Local first, context second.** One county-level or city-level number outweighs
  a national one. Order: your place → your state → national (if at all).
- **Two to four statistics, no more.** Every one named with source and year, e.g.
  "(Riverbend County Health Department, Community Health Assessment, 2025)".
- **Good free sources:** census data tools (population, income, language at home),
  state agency dashboards (education, health, workforce), your county health
  assessment, school district report cards, United Way community reports, and your
  own service data (waiting list, referrals you cannot take).
- **Your own data is evidence too.** "In 2025 we referred 38 adults to classes we
  had no room for" is the most local number that exists — put it in Move 2.

## The worked example (fictional — Riverbend Literacy Council)

> **THE PROBLEM, SIZED LOCALLY** — In Riverbend County, an estimated 11,700 adults
> — about 1 in 8 — read below the level a job application or a child's report card
> requires (Riverbend County Community Health Assessment, 2025, using the national
> literacy estimate applied to county population). Among the county's 4,100 adults
> who speak a language other than English at home, 1,600 report speaking English
> "not well" or "not at all" (American Community Survey, 2024 5-year estimates).
>
> **THE GAP** — The county's adult-education program offers day classes with a
> current waitlist of nine months (Riverbend Works quarterly report, Q2 2026).
> No evening provision exists east of the river, where the county's manufacturing
> shifts end at 5 p.m. Our own intake kept a standing list of 38 adults we could
> not place in 2025.
>
> **THE BRIDGE TO YOU** — The Literacy Council addresses this gap with evening
> classes at two library sites and one-to-one tutoring for learners whose schedules
> or levels do not fit a class. In 2025, 64% of goal-setting learners advanced one
> full reading level — a result we can trace to keeping class size at 12 with a
> trained tutor for every four learners.

## The quality bar

1. **Every number has a source and a year — in the sentence.** A statistic without
   "(Source, Year)" is an opinion with a digit.
2. **The gap is specific.** Not "more services are needed" but who, where, and how
   many — the waiting list and the turned-away count are the strongest local
   evidence you own.
3. **No blame.** Describe systems and circumstances, not the failings of the people
   you serve. Funders read respect.
4. **The bridge is one sentence, not a proposal.** The needs statement argues the
   problem exists; the program-design section argues your answer works.
