# Organization History — credibility in four paragraphs

**What it is:** the short, factual story of your organization — founding, growth,
steadiness — that proposals use in the "Organization Capacity / Background" section.
Its job is to answer one funder question: *does this organization deliver what it
says, reliably?*

**Time to build:** 45-60 minutes. **Update cadence:** every January (add the year's
line to the timeline) and on any leadership change.

---

## The template

```
FOUNDED
[Organization] began in [year], when [one sentence of origin — the real one:
a problem, a person, a first meeting or class].

MILESTONES (one line per year, newest impact first)
[Year] — [what happened: program added, first award of a kind, scale crossed].
[Year] — [what happened].
[Year] — [what happened].
(Write 4-7 lines. Each line is a fact with a number where one exists.)

STEADINESS
Annual budget over the last three years: [$X] → [$Y] → [$Z].
Funding mix: [sources with %]. Board: [number] members, [how they are
selected], meeting [frequency].

LEADERSHIP
[Executive director name] has led the organization since [year], with
[one line of relevant background]. [Program lead name] runs [program],
with [credential/experience]. Full bios live in staff-bios.md.

WHAT WE HAVE LEARNED (optional, 1-2 sentences)
[One honest sentence about a change the org made because experience
taught it — funders read adaptation as maturity.]
```

## The worked example (fictional — Riverbend Literacy Council)

> **FOUNDED** — Riverbend Literacy Council began in 2011, when a parish volunteer
> tutoring program outgrew its basement and its founders incorporated to take
> referrals from the county library and the workforce board.
>
> **MILESTONES** — 2025: 240 learners served; workplace reading program launched
> with three employer partners. 2023: first state humanities award; second
> evening class added. 2021: went from one to two full-time staff. 2019: 100th
> learner matched; first evaluation partnership with the university. 2016: first
> foundation award, $7,500.
>
> **STEADINESS** — Budget: $262,000 → $287,500 → $310,400 over three years.
> Funding mix 2025: individuals 41%, foundations 27%, events 18%, government
> contracts 14%. Board: nine members, drawn from former learners, tutors and
> employers, meeting monthly except August.
>
> **LEADERSHIP** — Maya Reyes has led the Council since 2018; she tutored in the
> founding program for four years before joining as program coordinator. Devin
> Park runs the class programs and tutor training (M.Ed., adult education).
>
> **WHAT WE HAVE LEARNED** — In 2022 we replaced open-ended tutoring pairs with
> twelve-week goal cycles; completion rose and tutor burnout fell. We have kept
> the cycle structure since.

## The quality bar

1. **Facts with numbers beat narrative.** One line per milestone, each with a number
   where one exists. Save the storytelling for the needs statement.
2. **The funding mix is stated, not hidden.** A single-source org reads fragile; a
   diverse mix reads managed. State it and show the trend line.
3. **Steadiness is the section's spine.** Three budget years and a real board say
   "we will exist to finish your grant" better than any adjective.
4. **The learned sentence is honest and specific.** "We changed X to Y and Z
   improved" is the strongest sentence in the document — but only when it is true.

## Where it gets used

Proposal skeletons 1, 2 and 4 compress it into their Organization Capacity section
(80-120 words); the LOI library uses one founding sentence plus one milestone;
stewardship emails quote a milestone when thanking funders who "have been with us
since [year]." When a grant report lands, mine it for next January's milestone line.
