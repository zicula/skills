# Go/No-Go Guide — scoring a grant before spending a day on it

The scorecard (`go-no-go-scorecard.xlsx`) exists because the most expensive grant is
the one you write badly for six days and lose. Twenty-five honest minutes on the
scorecard tells you which grants deserve your hours — and which deserve a pass that
protects them for next cycle.

```
  15 criteria, 4 groups                    6 gates (the dealbreakers)
  ─────────────────────                    ──────────────────────────
  Fit 30 · Access 34 · History 16 ·        eligibility · geography/size ·
  Capacity 20  =  weight 100               deadline · money real · match · owner

  you type 0-4 in column E  ──►  weighted column computes itself
  ──►  TOTAL /100 + VERDICT update live
       └── any gate = No  ──►  verdict is WAIT, whatever the total says
```

## Scoring rules

1. **0 to 4 per criterion, with the evidence named in the Notes column.**
   4 = true and evidenced · 3 = small gap, closable in days · 2 = partly true ·
   1 = a stretch · 0 = false today.
2. **No evidence, at most 2.** "I think we'd qualify" is a 2, and the Notes cell
   says what would move it to 4. The Notes column is the score's receipt — a score
   without a receipt is a feeling.
3. **Score against their words, not your hopes.** Keep the funder's guidelines open
   beside you. If the guidelines say the funded window is $150K-$1M budgets and yours
   is $310K, that criterion is a 4, not "probably fine."
4. **Score with the deadline in sight.** Capacity criteria are about the real weeks
   between now and the due date, not about a normal week.

## The four groups

- **Fit (30)** — is this our work? Mission, population, use of funds, language.
  Weak Fit warnings: you would have to describe the program differently for them to
  recognize it, or the money would fund something you were going to stop doing.
- **Access (34)** — can we actually get in? Eligibility, geography, org size,
  relationship, exclusions. This group carries the hard gates; most honest NO-GO
  verdicts die here, and that is the system saving you six days.
- **History (16)** — do they fund orgs like us, at sizes like ours, more than once?
  Three years of grantee lists (the research SOP shows where they live publicly)
  answers all three criteria in twenty minutes.
- **Capacity (20)** — can we produce our best work by the deadline? Hours, boilerplate
  readiness, match. The criteria teams skip, and the ones that decide whether the
  application is competitive or merely submitted.

## The verdict bands

| TOTAL | Verdict | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| any gate = No | **WAIT — a gate is not met** | Close the gate or pass. Read the Gates sheet; the evidence cell says what closing takes |
| 70-100 | **GO — commit the hours** | Writing blocks on the calendar today; tracker row to `Writing LOI` |
| 50-69 | **CONDITIONAL — fix low scorers** | Every criterion ≤ 2 gets a condition with a date in the Log. Re-score when they close. Commit only if the conditions close in time |
| 0-49 | **NO-GO — pass this round** | The pass email, then Log the decision. You just got your week back |

A CONDITIONAL is a real decision, not a soft no: it commits you to the conditions,
dates them, and re-scores. What it does not allow is writing "to see how it goes."

## The gates

Gates are the questions that make the total irrelevant when the answer is No. The
worked example ships with gate 3 (deadline feasibility) set to **No** — the fictional
team's board has not signed the budget numbers their LOI would cite — so a strong
76 reads WAIT until the gate closes. That is the design: in the real world, boards
vote late, letters arrive late, registrations take weeks, and a scorecard that let
"mostly ready" count as ready would send you into a deadline you cannot make.

Set the gates honestly from your funder's guidelines and your own calendar. Flip the
example's gate to Yes to see the verdict snap to GO — then set it back.

## How to pass gracefully

A pass is a professional act, and funders remember it better than most losing
applications. Three moves, in one email, within a week of the decision:

1. **Name it plainly.** "We are not submitting this cycle — the timeline and our
   board calendar do not give us room to submit our strongest application."
2. **Stay in the relationship.** "We would like to be on your mailing list for the
   fall cycle, and we would welcome fifteen minutes with the program officer."
3. **Ask one specific thing.** A grantee-list introduction, feedback on fit, the
   date the next cycle opens. One ask, not three.

Then: tracker row to `Passed`, one Log line, and the funder moves to the research
list for next cycle with a warmer contact than you started with.

## Re-weighting (only after ten logged scores)

Weights are defaults, not doctrine. After you have scored ten real opportunities and
lived with the outcomes, adjust — a consultant running clients' grants may weight
Capacity higher; an org with deep funder relationships may move weight from History
to Fit. Two rules: total stays 100, and the change gets a dated line in the Log so
future scores are comparable to past ones.

## The worked example

Row-by-row notes in the sheet tell the story: a three-person literacy council scoring
a community foundation's general-operating LOI. Strong Fit, real eligibility
evidence, two honest 2s in Access (relationship not yet booked, grantee-window
evidence thin), modest History, and a Capacity story every small org recognizes —
enough hours, current boilerplate, no match problem. TOTAL 76, band GO, held at WAIT
by gate 3. Score yours, log it, and act on what it says the same day.
