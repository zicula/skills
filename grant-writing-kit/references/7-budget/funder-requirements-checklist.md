# Funder Requirements Checklist — how to use it

The 22-row checklist lives inside `grant-budget.xlsx` on the **Checklist** sheet,
because budgets are where un-read rules do the most damage. This page is the
method; the sheet is the tool.

## The rule that makes the checklist work

**Copy the funder's words verbatim into column C. Do not paraphrase.**

A paraphrased rule is a missed requirement: "submit by 5 PM" becomes "submit
Friday" in someone's memory, and the timezone — Eastern? Pacific? — was the whole
rule. The verbatim quote, with its page/section noted in column D, is your receipt.
When a question arises in week five, you reread their words, not your memory of
them.

```
  GUIDELINES PDF ──► read with the checklist open ──► column C = verbatim quote
       │                                                  column D = page/§
       ▼                                                  column F = status
  Status ladder:  Not checked → Confirmed
                             ↘ "Conflict — ask the program officer"
                                (a conflict is a question asked EARLY,
                                 while a guess late is an interpretation made alone)
```

## The weekly rhythm while writing

1. **Day one:** read the guidelines start to finish with the checklist open —
   30-45 minutes for a typical foundation program. Fill every row you can; a row
   you cannot fill is a re-read, not a guess.
2. **Mid-writing:** re-check the three rows that kill applications — deadline
   (row 1), attachments (row 9), indirect/match rules (rows 6-7) — against the
   draft. Rules shift; funders post amendments and FAQ updates.
3. **Submission week:** every row reads `Confirmed` or has the program-officer
   question answered in writing. A `Not checked` row in submission week is a
   stop: ask, then submit.

## When the guidelines conflict with your plan

Mark the row `Conflict — ask the program officer` and write the question the same
day. One question, specific, with the quote attached: *"Your guidelines (p.4) say
X; our situation is Y; may we confirm the intended treatment?"* Program officers
answer process questions like this routinely — it is their job, and asking early
reads as rigor. Guessing late reads as the other thing.

What this checklist deliberately does **not** do: interpret rules for you. For
government funders with regulatory requirements, the row says what their page
says, and the interpretation belongs to your authorizing official or qualified
counsel. The kit manages your documents and deadlines; it stays out of ruling on
regulations.

## The three rows that decide most outcomes

- **Row 1, the deadline with timezone:** the internal deadline (five days earlier
  for an LOI, ten for a proposal) goes into your tracker's Next-action date the
  moment this row is confirmed.
- **Row 9, attachments:** assemble the folder the day the row is confirmed —
  letters and signatures are the long-lead items; a missing attachment voids more
  submissions than weak prose ever has.
- **Rows 6-7, indirect and match:** these change what the budget *can* contain;
  discovering them after the budget is built means rebuilding it.

## After the decision

Two rows get added information: the award window (row 13) — so follow-up timing
is planned, not anxious — and renewal language (row 19), which is copied into the
tracker row's Notes for the renewal proposal eighteen months out. The checklist
file per funder joins that funder's folder (`funder-program-date`), and next
cycle's version starts as a copy with statuses reset — the verbatim quotes carry
forward.
