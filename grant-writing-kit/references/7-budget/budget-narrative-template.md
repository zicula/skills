# Budget Narrative Template — every number speaks

The budget workbook (`grant-budget.xlsx`) holds the numbers; the budget narrative
tells a reviewer why each one is what it is, in plain sentences a non-accountant
follows. Funders read the narrative looking for one thing: **does this org know
what things cost, and why?**

**Rule of the form:** if the funder provides a budget form and a narrative form,
theirs wins for structure — this template fills inside their headings.

---

## The template (one short paragraph per category)

```
PERSONNEL
[Role], [hours/period] at [$rate/hour] = [$amount]. The rate is [where it
comes from: current payroll / approved scale]. [Why this time is needed:
the task it performs in the program model.]

FRINGE BENEFITS
[$amount], calculated at [rate]% of salaries — [whose real rate: "our
actual loaded rate for part-time staff, per the payroll report"]. If the
funder caps the rate, say so: "the funder's cap of [X]% applies; our
actual rate is [Y]%."

TRAVEL
[What travel: tutor/site mileage, n miles × $rate — name the rate source:
"the rate our finance policy sets"]. Local travel only; no out-of-area
travel is budgeted. [Or, if any: purpose, per-funder-rules basis.]

EQUIPMENT / SUPPLIES  (separate if the funder separates — their $ threshold)
[Items with unit math: "60 workbooks × $22, vendor quote 2026-08."]
The quote or price basis named per line.

CONTRACTUAL / CONSULTANTS
[Who, doing what, for how much — "independent evaluator, end-of-year
learner-outcome report, $1,500 fixed fee (quote enclosed)"]. If selected
later: the selection criteria and its date.

OTHER DIRECT
[The honest miscellaneous: printing, translation, participant materials —
each line with its basis. If nothing: say so.]

INDIRECT COSTS
State their rule in their words: "The funder [allows up to X% / does not
fund indirect costs / requires a negotiated rate]." Then what you did:
"We request [0% / X%] accordingly." If the rule is unclear — this is a
question for the program officer, asked before submission (Checklist row 6).

MATCH / COST SHARE  (only if required or offered)
[Source, amount, and the documentation: "classroom space provided by the
county library, valued at $600/cycle per the partnership letter of
[date]." Note what the funder counts as match, per their rules.]
```

## The worked example (fictional — the $10,473 ESL expansion)

> **Personnel.** Part-time ESL instructor, 6 hours/week for 30 weeks at $23.50/hour
> = $4,230. The rate is the Council's current part-time instructor scale (FY26
> payroll). Program coordinator oversight, 2 hours/week at $28.00 = $1,680 —
> existing staff, hours reallocated from intake scheduling now automated.
> **Fringe.** $591, calculated at 10% — our actual loaded rate for part-time staff
> per the payroll report. **Travel.** $180: tutor mileage between the two satellite
> sites, 450 miles × $0.40 (the rate in our finance policy), documented by monthly
> log. **Supplies.** $1,320: 60 student workbooks × $22 (vendor quote, 2026-08);
> $342: 60 placement-assessment licenses × $5.70 (same quote). **Contractual.**
> $1,500: independent evaluator, fixed fee, end-of-year learner-outcome report
> (quote enclosed; the same evaluator produced our 2025 report). **Other direct.**
> $420: translation of intake and safety materials into three languages (service
> quote 2026-09); $210: classroom printing (print-shop estimate). **Indirect.**
> $0 — the guidelines state this program does not fund indirect costs. **Match.**
> $600: classroom space at both library sites, per the partnership letter of
> 2026-07-14, valued at market rate for equivalent space.

## Quality bar

1. **Every line's basis is named** — payroll report, quote with date, policy rate,
   partnership letter. A number without a basis is a guess in a suit.
2. **Math visible where the funder will check it:** hours × rate, units × price,
   head-count × per-person cost. The narrative shows the multiplication the
   workbook hides.
3. **Realistic beats impressive.** Rounded-up lines read padded; round-to-reality
   with the quote attached reads managed.
4. **The indirect paragraph states their rule, not your wish.** This is the single
   most common technical miss in small-org budgets — the Checklist sheet exists
   to catch it before submission, and the narrative confirms you checked.
5. **Consistency sweep at the end:** the narrative's totals equal the workbook's
   totals equal the form's totals — three places, one number, recomputed today.
