# LOI Library — ten letters of intent, one per scenario

A letter of intent (LOI) — some funders call it a letter of inquiry — is a one-to-
two-page ask that decides whether you are invited to submit a full proposal. Every
letter below is complete: replace the [brackets] with your facts, cut what your
funder's guidelines do not ask for, and keep the length at or under the guideline.

**Before using any letter:** read `loi-craft-guide.md` (hooks, the one-ask rule,
the do/don't table) and have these ready —
- your mission one-pager (`4-boilerplate/`) — letters 1-2 sentences of it
- your needs statement's two best local numbers with sources
- the program profile or org facts that match the funder's focus
- the ask: an amount that traces to a real budget line (`7-budget/`)

**Universal skeleton behind all ten letters** (funders vary the order — follow
their guidelines if they specify one):

```
 1. Header — your letterhead, date, addressee with correct title
 2. Re: line — "[Organization] request for [program], [$amount]"
 3. Opening hook — one sentence of their priority language + your fit (2 sentences)
 4. Who you are — one-pager compressed (2-3 sentences)
 5. The need — two local numbers with sources (2-3 sentences)
 6. The request — what the money does, with the one number that proves scale
 7. Capacity — why you will deliver: results, staff, years (2 sentences)
 8. The ask restated + what it buys ($ per thing)
 9. Recognition/visibility line (only if true)
10. Close — contact, offer of the full proposal, thanks
```

## LOI 1 — General Operating Support (GOS)

*Use when:* the funder offers unrestricted or general-operating grants. The letter
argues the organization, not one program.

> [Date]
>
> [Name], [Title]
> [Funder]
> [Address]
>
> **Re: [Organization] — request for general operating support, [$15,000]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> [Funder]'s guidelines name [their priority language — e.g., "strong neighborhood
> institutions as the backbone of county life"]. [Organization] is that institution
> for [your field]: since [year], we have [one sentence of what you do and for whom].
>
> [Organization] serves [number] [people] annually in [place]: [program one — one
> clause with number], [program two], and [program three]. Our annual budget is
> [$310,400], of which [X]% is individually raised — a mix that keeps us [one
> honest clause about stability].
>
> The need is measurable: [local statistic with source and year], and [second
> statistic]. Unrestricted support is where it meets us: GOS lets us hold [the
> line the money really holds — e.g., "the instructor position and the intake
> pipeline that keep referrals from going unanswered"] — the costs that programs
> grants rarely cover and that every program depends on.
>
> Our results are public and assessed: in [year], [headline outcome with number
> and evidence — e.g., "64% of goal-setting learners advanced one full reading
> level (standardized pre/post assessment)"]. [Staff credential line.] We have
> delivered [X] years without [relevant failure — e.g., "a missed funder report"].
>
> A grant of [$15,000] — [what it equals: "6% of our budget"] — would [what it
> holds open: "keep both evening classes running through the spring cycle"]. We
> would be glad to report against our audited [or reviewed] financials and our
> published metrics at year's end, and we welcome a visit any [program day].
>
> Thank you for considering [Organization].
>
> [Name, title, phone, email]

**Do not:** invent a pseudo-program to look specific — GOS funders see through it.
**Do:** name the exact staff line or cost the money holds; that is the difference
between "unrestricted" and "unexplained."

## LOI 2 — Program / Project Support

*Use when:* the funder funds specific programs. The letter argues one program's
gap, model, and cost.

> **Re: [Organization] — request for [Program Name], [$25,000]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Your [program name] guidelines ask for [their language — e.g., "projects that
> expand access to [field] in [region]"]. We write to propose exactly that:
> [one sentence of the program].
>
> The gap is local and current: [statistic with source, year], and [second
> statistic — ideally a waiting list or turned-away count]. [Organization]'s
> [Program Name] responds: [model in one sentence — who, what, how often, where].
> In [year] the program served [number] at [per-participant cost]; [one outcome
> with number and evidence].
>
> This request expands the program from [current state] to [expanded state]:
> [the specific delta — "one additional evening section, seating 12 more learners
> per cycle"]. The [$25,000] covers [2-3 line items with amounts from your budget
> workbook: "instruction ($X), assessment licenses ($Y), translated materials
> ($Z)"], and the full budget follows your form with every line's basis named.
>
> [Organization] has run [Program Name] since [year] with [capacity proof:
> staff credential, partner commitments in writing, retention or completion
> number]. [Partner sentence if true: "The [partner] provides classroom space at
> both sites (partnership letter enclosed)."]
>
> We would measure the expanded program against our published metrics — [name
> the two you will report] — and report on your schedule. Thank you for your
> consideration; I would welcome questions at [phone/email].
>
> [Signature block]

**Do not:** propose a program you would build only if funded — funders fund track
records. **Do:** make the delta arithmetic visible (from X to Y, at Z cost).

## LOI 3 — Capital / Facilities

*Use when:* building, renovation, equipment, or a facility campaign. Funders here
want the campaign context, the total, and who else is in.

> **Re: [Organization] — request to the [Funder] Facilities Fund, [$50,000] toward
> the [Project Name] campaign**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> [Organization] is raising [$1.2M] to [what the project does — "renovate the
> vacant [building] into [what it becomes], doubling [capacity metric]"]. Your
> guidelines' priority on [their language] is, verbatim, what this project is.
>
> **The case.** [One paragraph: the physical problem with numbers — e.g., "our
> leased space fails accessibility code (inspection 3/2026), costs $38,400/year
> with no equity accrual, and caps enrollment at 140 against a waitlist of 90."]
>
> **The project.** [Scope one sentence.] Total: [$1.2M] — construction [$X],
> [architectural/professional] [$Y], contingency [$Z]. To date: [$W] committed —
> [who: "the state [program] ($600K), two family foundations ($150K), and our
> giving by every board member ($40K)"]. Your [$50,000] would name [naming opportunity,
> if the campaign has one] and close [which phase].
>
> **The organization behind it.** [Two sentences: years, results, the staff who
> will run the completed facility.] The completed facility's operating plan is
> budgeted: [one sentence showing you can afford to run what you build].
>
> Campaign timeline: [groundbreaking/completion dates]. We would welcome a site
> visit — the building is at [address], and the need is visible from the sidewalk.
>
> [Signature block]

**Do not:** send a capital letter without commitments in hand — the first third
proves the campaign is real. **Do:** show the operating plan; "who runs it after"
is the capital funder's first doubt.

## LOI 4 — Capacity Building

*Use when:* the funder strengthens organizations (systems, planning, technology,
staff development) rather than delivering programs. The letter argues the
organizational bottleneck, not a program.

> **Re: [Organization] — request to the [Capacity Initiative], [$10,000]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> [Organization] has grown [from X to Y — e.g., "from 140 to 240 learners in three
> years"]. The growth has outrun one specific piece of our infrastructure: [the
> bottleneck, named — "our client records live in four spreadsheets, so a funder's
> question about outcomes takes two days of manual counting — and our new state
> contract requires quarterly outcome reporting we cannot yet produce on time"].
>
> We request [$10,000] to [the fix — "implement a case-management system and
> retrain intake on it"], covering [items with amounts: "licenses ($X),
> implementation consultant ($Y), staff training time ($Z)"]. The consultant,
> [name/qualification if chosen, else "to be selected from three proposals"],
> has [relevant credential].
>
> What changes, measurably: [before → after with numbers — "funder-report
> preparation: 2 days → 2 hours; outcome data current to the week, not the
> quarter; zero missed reporting deadlines on the new contract's schedule"].
> We will evaluate success by [the specific check — "the first quarterly report
> submitted on schedule, and a staff time audit before/after"].
>
> [One sentence of organizational stability: years, budget, board.] This is our
> second infrastructure investment in [n] years — [the prior one and what it
> enabled, if true].
>
> [Signature block]

**Do not:** ask capacity money for generic "staff development" — name the
bottleneck and the measurable after. **Do:** tie the fix to an obligation you
already have (a contract, a growth number, a reporting requirement).

## LOI 5 — Research / Evaluation

*Use when:* academic partners, evaluation projects, or community-based research.
The letter argues method and partnership, briefly.

> **Re: [Organization] — letter of intent, [RFP/Program name], [$XX,XXX]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> We propose [project title], a [duration] study of [research question in plain
> language], conducted by [institution/PI] in partnership with [community
> organization].
>
> **The question.** [One paragraph: what is unknown, why it matters locally —
> with one statistic — and how the answer changes practice.]
>
> **The method, briefly.** [Design, sample, instruments — 3 sentences. E.g.,
> "A mixed-methods design: pre/post assessment of [n] participants across [sites]
> using [instrument], plus [number] interviews. Analysis: [approach]. The full
> protocol follows your format requirements."]
>
> **The partnership.** [Organization] brings [what the community partner
> contributes: population access, [number] years of service data, participant
> recruitment capability, community advisory input]; [institution] brings
> [analytic capacity]. Roles, data ownership and dissemination are agreed in a
> memorandum before enrollment (draft enclosed). Participants are compensated
> [$amount] and consent per [your institution's human-subjects process — name it;
> follow its requirements and timeline exactly].
>
> **Timeline and ask.** [n] months; [$amount] requested of the [$total] project.
> Findings return to the community first: [how — a public session, a plain-language
> report] — then [publication/dissemination].
>
> [Signature blocks — both partners]

**Do not:** write past your method — an LOI reviewer here is checking whether a
real design exists. **Do:** state who owns the data and when the community sees
results; these are the questions community-based reviewers read for.

## LOI 6 — Faith-Based Program

*Use when:* a religious organization applies to a funder that welcomes faith-based
applicants (many community funders do). The letter is the same craft with one
adjustment: lead with the program's public benefit, describe the faith context
honestly.

> **Re: [Organization] — request for [Program Name], [$X]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> [Funder]'s focus on [their priority] describes work [Organization] has done
> since [year]: [one sentence — the program's public benefit]. [Organization] is
> the [social-outreach arm / congregational nonprofit] of [congregation]; the
> [Program Name] is open to all [community members] regardless of [anything] and
> is delivered [where — "at the community center, which is open to the whole
> neighborhood"].
>
> The need: [two local statistics with sources]. The program: [model in one
> sentence — who, what, how often]. Results: [outcome with number and year].
>
> The [$X] requested covers [line items with amounts]. [Organization] commits
> [in-kind or cash share, if any — with its basis]. [Partner sentence: the
> school, food pantry, or agency that receives referrals.]
>
> [Staff/volunteer capacity line with numbers.] Our [annual report / program
> report] to the [denomination/community] is public, and we report to funders on
> their schedule against the metrics above.
>
> [Signature block]

**Do not:** assume the funder shares the faith — assume they share the outcome.
**Do:** state openness and eligibility plainly if your guidelines ask; vague here
reads evasive.

## LOI 7 — Arts & Culture

*Use when:* arts councils, cultural funds, creative-place programs. The letter
pairs artistic merit with public benefit — the two criteria these panels score.

> **Re: [Organization] — request to the [Arts Fund], [$8,500] for [Project/Season]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Your guidelines ask [their language — e.g., "work of artistic merit that engages
> [community]"]. [Project/Season] is our answer: [one vivid sentence of the work —
> what an audience member will actually experience].
>
> **Artistic merit.** [Artist/company/directors and their standing — one sentence;
> the work's relation to your past work — one sentence: "our fourth commissioned
> season, and our first with [notable artist], whose [credential]."]
>
> **Public benefit.** [Audience numbers: expected attendance, free/low-cost share,
> where — "1,800 projected across 6 performances; 400 free tickets to [schools/
> senior centers], transportation included."] [Community role in making the work,
> if true: "the [community] chorus of 30 joins the final movement."]
>
> **Capacity and support.** [Organization] has produced [n] seasons [without
> deficit]; the [$8,500] covers [artist fees ($X) — note if artists are paid at
> or above your local arts-council rate standard, accessibility costs ($Y)].
> Other support: [earned income, other funders — shows a funded package].
>
> We will report attendance, free-ticket distribution and audience feedback on
> your schedule. Thank you for your consideration.
>
> [Signature block]

**Do not:** write only about the art — panels score public benefit with real
weight. **Do:** say whether artists are paid, and at what standard; panels ask.

## LOI 8 — Health / Human Services

*Use when:* health foundations, hospital community-benefit funds, insurance
foundations. The letter speaks in measures: reach, screening, access, referral
completion.

> **Re: [Organization] — request for [Program Name], [$X]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Your 2026 priorities include [their words — e.g., "increasing access to primary
> and preventive care in [region]"]. [Program Name] does this work at street
> level: [one sentence of the model — who, what, where, how often].
>
> **The need.** [Two local health statistics with sources — e.g., county health
> assessment measures: uninsured rate, travel time to care, condition prevalence.]
> [One sentence connecting the statistic to your catchment.]
>
> **The program and its numbers.** Since [year]: [number] served; [the outcome
> your evidence supports — "84% of clients reached completed their referral within
> 30 days (client tracking log, 2025)"; "screenings completed: [n], with [n]
> abnormalities referred for follow-up"]. [Staff credentials — licensure where
> relevant.]
>
> **The ask.** [$X] covers [line items with amounts]. This funding would [the
> delta — "extend hours to two evenings, projected +140 client visits/year based
> on current demand"].
>
> We report per your template and coordinate with [local health coalition/
> hospital community-benefit process, if applicable]. [Signature block]

**Do not:** overstate clinical claims — describe what your program measures, within
its scope. **Do:** name your referral partners and completion rates; access funders
think in systems, and your program is a node in one.

## LOI 9 — Education / Youth

*Use when:* education foundations, family foundations, youth-serving funds. The
letter shows learning evidence and school/family partnership.

> **Re: [Organization] — request for [Program Name], [$X]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Your guidelines prioritize [their language — e.g., "expanded learning time that
> produces measurable academic growth"]. [Program Name] is [one sentence — who,
> what, when, where].
>
> **The need.** [Two education statistics with sources — district report-card
> data, assessment results, attendance data — plus your own waiting list if true.]
>
> **The program and its evidence.** [Model in one sentence.] In [year]:
> [participation number: hours per child, attendance rate]; [academic outcome
> with instrument — e.g., "% of regularly attending students gained [n] reading
> levels (district assessment, pre/post)"]; [school/teacher partner quote or
> number, if available]. [Staff credentials; background-check policy stated
> plainly.]
>
> **The ask.** [$X] covers [line items with amounts: staffing ratio, curriculum,
> family engagement]. The delta: [from X to Y students, at $Z per student].
>
> [School district partner sentence, if true — MOU, referrals, data sharing.]
> We report attendance, growth measures and family-survey results on your
> schedule. [Signature block]

**Do not:** promise test-score gains you have not measured — name your instrument
and your honest result. **Do:** state safety infrastructure (background checks,
ratios, supervision) in one plain sentence; family funders read for it first.

## LOI 10 — Environment / Conservation

*Use when:* environmental funds, community greening programs, conservation
districts. The letter pairs ecological outcome with community benefit and a
maintenance plan.

> **Re: [Organization] — request to the [Environment Fund], [$X] for [Project]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Your call for [their language — e.g., "projects that restore [habitat] while
> engaging [community]"] matches [Project] exactly: [one sentence of the project
> — what happens on the ground].
>
> **The site and the problem.** [One sentence of place; two facts with sources —
> condition data, water quality, canopy loss, survey results.]
>
> **The project.** [Scope: acres/trees/units; methods named; who does the work —
> staff, contractor with credential, volunteers.] Timeline: [dates]. The ecological
> outcome we will measure: [metric + method — e.g., "survival rate of planted
> stock at 24 months (stem counts, per your monitoring protocol)"].
>
> **Community benefit and maintenance.** [Who uses/benefits; the volunteer or
> school role, with numbers.] After establishment, [who maintains it — the named
> plan, budget line, or MOU]; the [$X] covers [establishment items with amounts],
> and maintenance is budgeted in our operating plan at [$Y]/year.
>
> [Partners: the municipality, district, or landowner — with the agreement status:
> signed or in what stage.] We report per your protocol and can host a site visit
> any time. [Signature block]

**Do not:** skip the maintenance plan — "who waters the trees in year three" is
the question that kills greening applications. **Do:** name the landowner
agreement's status; reviewers check site control before anything else.
