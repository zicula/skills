# Stewardship Emails — five emails that keep funders warm

Renewals are won in the months nobody is asking. These five emails, sent from
your tracker's triggers, keep the relationship alive between decisions. Rules for
all five: **short (under 150 words), specific (one number or one moment), and
not a disguised ask** — the ask emails are the LOI and the renewal proposal;
everything here is relationship.

```
  Email 1 ── award/thanks          within 48 hours of the award call
  Email 2 ── mid-grant update      halfway through the grant period
  Email 3 ── the invitation        once per grant, at a natural moment
  Email 4 ── report delivery       the day the report ships
  Email 5 ── renewal preview       4-6 weeks before the renewal LOI
```

## Email 1 — Thank you (within 48 hours of award)

> Subject: Thank you — and what happens next
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Thank you for the [Foundation]'s grant of [$15,000] to [Organization]. The
> board heard the news at [meeting date] and asked me to pass along their
> thanks — [one specific tie: "several members know your evening-class site
> from the library"]. The money goes to work immediately: [what it holds, one
> clause]. Our report will reach you by [date from the award letter], and you
> are welcome to see a class any [day] — [program officer name] has the
> standing invitation.
>
> With gratitude, [Signature]

The 48-hour rule is the whole trick: the same thanks sent in week three reads
like administration; sent in two days it reads like culture.

## Email 2 — Mid-grant update (halfway, unsolicited)

> Subject: Halfway through your grant — one number
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> A short note from mid-grant: [the one number that is going well — "the new
> evening section filled all 12 seats in week one, and 10 of the 12 are the
> east-side learners this grant was about"]. One thing we are watching:
> [one honest watch item, one clause]. The full report comes [month]; until
> then, [the one thing you'd want them picturing — "Thursday nights at the
> east-side library are the loudest room in the building"].
>
> [Signature]

Send it even when nothing is remarkable — the habit is the asset. Do not send
only good news here; the one watch item builds the credibility §4 of the report
will spend.

## Email 3 — The invitation (once per grant period)

> Subject: You are welcome in the room any Thursday
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> [One-paragraph note: a moment from the program — a learner milestone, a
> tutor anniversary, a class photo moment.] If you would like to see it:
> [class] runs [day, time, place], and the door is open — no event, no
> program, just [the thing they funded, happening]. [Offer the alternative:
> "If Thursday is impossible this season, our [annual showcase] is [date] and
> the invitation stands."]
>
> [Signature]

Keep it pressure-free and specific; funders visit orgs that make visiting easy
and without making it awkward when they do not.

## Email 4 — Report delivery (the day it ships)

> Subject: [Organization]'s report on your 2026 grant — and thank you
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Our report on the [$15,000] grant is attached — [one headline result from
> it, one clause]. [If a miss was handled: the honest sentence, matching the
> report's §4.] Thank you for the partnership this grant represented; the
> results are yours as much as ours. [If renewal cycles apply and honest:
> "We expect to submit for next year in [month] and will be in touch then."]
>
> [Signature]

The report that arrives with a two-sentence email from a human beats the report
that arrives from a portal with nothing — send both if the portal requires it.

## Email 5 — Renewal preview (4-6 weeks before the renewal LOI)

> Subject: Planning our [year] application — a heads-up
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> A short heads-up ahead of your [cycle] deadline: [Organization] intends to
> apply for [the increment — "a second year supporting the east-side section,
> which your 2026 grant launched"]. [One sentence of the interim result that
> grounds it.] Two questions as we draft: [one real process question — "does
> your GOS program accept renewals through the same LOI process?"] and [one
> genuine readiness question — "is there anything you would want to see from
> this year that we did not include last time?"].
>
> [Signature]

This email converts the renewal from a cold application into a scheduled
conversation — and the answers routinely sharpen the application before a word
is drafted.

## The system that makes it work

Each email's trigger lives in the tracker: award date → Email 1 in 2 days;
grant midpoint → Email 2; report submission date → Email 4 on the day; renewal
deadline minus 6 weeks → Email 5. Log every send in the Log sheet — the
stewardship recap in your renewal proposal (`skeleton-renewal-continuation.md`
§3) is written from that Log, which is why the recap requires no invention.
