# Grant Report Skeleton — results, honestly told

A grant report is two documents wearing one envelope: the accountability document
(numbers against promises) and the first draft of your renewal. Write it as both.
**The prerequisite:** the funder's own template wins whenever one exists — fill
this skeleton inside their headings, and copy their questions verbatim into your
draft so each gets its answer.

**Time:** 2-3 hours when your metrics files are current; a full day when they are
not (which is the argument for the metrics rhythm in `4-boilerplate/impact-metrics.md`).

---

## The skeleton

```
 1. GRANT AT A GLANCE (½ page, table)
    Funder / program · grant number · period · award amount
    The 2-4 metrics they funded, each: target → actual → evidence source

 2. WHAT THE MONEY DID (per budget category, ½-¾ page)
    Category totals vs. budget, with one sentence of explanation per
    variance over ~10%. Personnel tell the truth here: the instructor
    hours, the coordinator time — name what the hours bought.

 3. RESULTS IN PARTICIPANTS' TERMS (¾ page)
    The outcome numbers with instruments (pre/post, survey, logs) —
    denominators stated. One participant story (consented; four
    sentences; it illustrates numbers from section 1, introduces none).

 4. WHAT DID NOT GO TO PLAN (¼-½ page — do not skip)
    The honest paragraph: what underperformed, why, what you changed.
    Funders renew orgs that diagnose; they avoid orgs that only
    celebrate. A report with zero difficulties reads as unaudited.

 5. WHAT COMES NEXT (¼ page)
    The continuation logic: what persists after the grant (the trained
    cadre, the assessment cycle), what this report's data says about
    next year, and — where honest — the seed of the renewal ask.

 6. THANK YOU + RECOGNITION (2 sentences)
    Specific thanks tied to what the money did, recognition per their
    guidelines, invitation to visit.
```

## The numbers section, done right

- **Targets and actuals side by side, always.** A number alone makes the reader
  hunt for the promise it answers.
- **Denominators in the sentence:** "64% of goal-setting learners who completed
  the post-assessment (62 of 97) advanced one full reading level." The evaluator
  who finds the denominator later will ask in writing; give it first.
- **Money and results in the same report, cross-referenced:** "the $4,230
  instructor line produced 1,680 learner-hours" ties section 2 to section 3 —
  the sentence program officers quote upward.
- **The variance rule:** any line over ~10% off budget gets one sentence of why
  and what you did. Unexplained variances generate the follow-up emails.

## The worked example (fictional, abridged)

> **GRANT AT A GLANCE** — Riverbend Community Foundation, General Operating
> Support, G-2026-0142, 2025-10 to 2026-09, $15,000. Metrics: learners served
> (target 240 → actual 247, intake log); level gains (64% of assessed
> goal-setters, standard pre/post); retention at 6 months (89%, tutor-hours log).
>
> **WHAT THE MONEY DID** — The grant held the part-time instructor line ($4,230)
> and intake/assessment costs ($933) across the year; remaining funds sustained
> facility and coordination costs that program grants do not cover. Personnel
> variance: +4% — two additional intake weeks added when the waitlist passed 30.
>
> **WHAT DID NOT GO TO PLAN** — East-side placement reached 71% against our 75%
> target: the binding constraint turned out to be transportation, not seats.
> We responded by moving one section to the east-side library and adding a
> travel line for next year — the basis of the expansion request that follows
> under separate cover.

## Filing mechanics

- **Their deadline, ten days earlier internally** (tracker Renewal due / Reporting
  statuses; the radar watches it).
- **Match the report to the metrics you promised in the proposal** — same
  definitions, same instruments (`impact-metrics.md`). If a definition changed
  mid-grant, say so and show both.
- **File the report, then immediately** run the stewardship sequence
  (`stewardship-emails.md`): delivery email (Email 4), and the renewal conversation
  if their cycle supports it. A filed report that ships silently is a relationship
  running cold at its warmest moment.
- **Mine before you file:** the report's sections 1, 3 and 5 become next year's
  proposal inputs, the annual report's data page, and January's org-history
  milestone line. One report, four documents — which is why the honest version is
  also the efficient one.
