# The "What Changed" Memo — which grant paid for what

The question that started this template is the one from the forums: *"I'm stuck
trying to work out which grant paid for what."* It arrives eighteen months into
grant seeking, when three funders overlap on two programs, a board member asks a
simple question, and the books answer in accounting's vocabulary, not the
program's. The memo is the bridge: one page per question, built from your tracker
Log and budget, attributing money to results in words your board and funders read.

**Time:** 20-40 minutes per memo once the habit below exists. **Trigger:** board
meetings, funder visits, annual reports, and any morning the overlap question
appears.

---

## The template

```
WHAT CHANGED — [question in plain words, with date]
Prepared [date] by [name] · Sources: tracker Log, [FY] budget, [reports]

THE QUESTION
[E.g., "Which grants paid for the east-side evening classes this year,
 and what did each buy?"]

THE MONEY (table — one row per funding source)
| Source | Period | Amount | What it paid for (categories/lines) |
| Riverbend Community Fdn GOS  | Oct-Sep | $15,000 | instructor line + intake |
| State Cultural Programs Fund | Nov-Sep | $25,000 | coordinator + evaluation |
| Library partnership (in-kind)| Sep-Aug | $  600 | classroom space, both sites |

THE RESULTS (table — same rows, carried across)
| Source | Result it is linked to | Evidence |
| Community Fdn GOS | 1,680 learner-hours delivered | instructor timesheets |
| Cultural Programs | 64% level-gain rate, independently evaluated | evaluator report |
| Library space | 24 sessions hosted at 2 sites | partnership log |

WHAT THIS CHANGES (2-4 sentences)
[E.g., "GOS is carrying the staff line that every result depends on — its
 renewal is the year's first priority. The state grant's evaluation line
 produced the only externally verified number we have; repeat it. The
 library partnership is undervalued in money terms and central in
 delivery terms — the partnership letter renews in March."]
```

## The habit that makes the memo possible

The memo is a report; the habit is bookkeeping-adjacent and weekly:

1. **Every grant in the tracker carries its Awarded amount and date** — that is
   the memo's source column, kept current by the Friday update.
2. **Programs have cost lines** (the program profiles' "what it costs to run"),
   so attribution is arithmetic: which funded lines sit under which program.
3. **In-kind is recorded with its basis** (the letter, the market-rate note) —
   otherwise it vanishes from every table and undervalues the partners you most
   depend on.
4. **Restricted vs. unrestricted is marked in the tracker Notes.** A restricted
   grant bought what its agreement says; GOS is the flexible layer everything
   else stands on — the memo's job is to make that visible before someone
   "saves" the flexible money away.

## The two questions this memo answers best

- **For the board:** "Are we dependent on any one grant?" The table shows the
  concentration in one glance, and the what-changes paragraph states the plan
  (which renewal, which diversification) in the same glance.
- **For a funder:** "What did your money do that others' did not?" — the
  cross-walk version: their grant's lines, their grant's results, one page. Send
  it with the report or before a site visit; it is the document program officers
  forward to their boards.

## What the memo is not

It is not the accounting: allocation methods, cost pools and audit trails belong
to your bookkeeper and auditor, and where a funder's agreement prescribes an
allocation method, their method governs. The memo reads the accounting and the
tracker and translates — it does not replace either. When a number in the memo
and a number in the books disagree, the books win and the memo gets fixed; that
is also the fastest way to find a tracker error before a funder does.
